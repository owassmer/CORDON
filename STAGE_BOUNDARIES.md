# Stage boundaries

CORDON is derived in the order below because each stage supplies meaning that the next stage must preserve. Current status belongs only in `state/CURRENT.json`.

## Shared contract

A stage opens only from accepted upstream meaning. Its output must identify its canonical owner, its downstream consumer, its negative scope, and a check capable of falsifying its mechanical claims. Reviews assert; evidence and adjudication decide. Owen accepts a stage.

An accepted stage reopens only when a named proposition and source show that its meaning is wrong or incomplete. A downstream schema preference, missing convenience field, or implementation difficulty is not a reopening ground.

## A — legal provision decision map

**Purpose:** establish the operative, point-in-time legal meaning that later stages must preserve.

**Owns:** provisions and versions; actors and authority; modalities; triggers and pure conditions; branches and effects; exceptions; evidence and effectiveness requirements; source spans; dependency closure; bounded legal unknowns.

**Excludes:** normalized clocks and numeric parameters; formulas; data bindings; Actions; domain nouns; platform types.

**Exit:** the decision-reachable legal forest resolves from controlling authority to operational descendants and back from materially different real cases, with bounded unknowns explicit and Owen's acceptance recorded.

## B — clocks and parameters

**Purpose:** extract the temporal and quantitative system stated by accepted Stage A meaning.

**Owns:** clock kinds and anchors; units, windows, recurrence, thresholds, lookbacks, source-stated dependencies, normalized parameters, expression dispositions, and bounded open terms.

**Excludes:** elapsed-time arithmetic; calendar and geometry algorithms; source data binding; Actions; nouns; platform design. It cannot invent a clock from silence or fill one source's omission from a neighboring source.

**Exit:** every in-aperture temporal or numeric expression has one disposition; every minted item resolves to an accepted producer and exact phrase; versions and populations close; checks discriminate; changed content has explicit acceptance.

## C — deterministic math and geometry

**Purpose:** turn accepted legal predicates, clocks, and parameters into deterministic calculations.

**Owns:** formulas; calendar arithmetic; geometry and topology; predicate evaluators; survey-adequacy calculations; numerical boundaries; explicit unresolved-input behavior.

**Excludes:** reinterpretation of law; acquisition policy; record ownership; Actions; domain or platform modeling.

**Exit:** independent recomputation, boundary/property tests, and mutation tests establish fidelity to A and B across materially different cases.

## D — data and evidence contracts

**Purpose:** specify how every required input is obtained, identified, versioned, corrected, and judged complete enough for its consumer.

**Owns:** source roles; grain and keys; provenance; cadence; joins; missingness; conflict behavior; public and controlled evidence boundaries; correction and acquisition routes.

**Excludes:** changing upstream legal or mathematical meaning; operational write actions; ontology compilation.

**Exit:** every C input and accepted factual predicate has an honest contract or an explicit unavailable state, with population, identity, permissions, and lineage tested.

## E — government Actions and external events

**Purpose:** define the minimum decision and write plane through which the operator changes or reconciles official state.

**Owns:** Action families; authority; actors; parameters; preconditions and refusals; atomic effects; external-event reconciliation; idempotency; concurrency; evidence of completion.

**Excludes:** convenience CRUD without a decision purpose; noun proliferation; platform-specific compilation.

**Exit:** every Action corresponds to accepted authority and practitioner work, changes state atomically, fails safely, and has a falsifiable completion read.

## F — nouns and fact-bearing relationships

**Purpose:** derive the minimum real-world semantic model required by accepted decisions, evidence, computations, and Actions.

**Owns:** identities and lifecycles; fact owners; relationship direction, grain, cardinality, and temporal metadata; admission and rejection rationale.

**Excludes:** objects added only for navigation, reporting fashion, platform convenience, or speculative future work.

**Exit:** the model covers every accepted decision and Action, preserves independent distinctions, and removes every type with no necessary fact or relationship to own.

## G — platform ontology compilation

**Purpose:** compile accepted F semantics and E behavior into the chosen operational substrate.

**Owns:** platform object and property mappings; links; interfaces; Actions and Functions; permissions; audit types; build and migration order; live resource-budget arithmetic.

**Excludes:** re-deriving product meaning from platform affordances or current agent/tool limitations.

**Exit:** the compiled system is deployable, permission-correct, readable in its destination, and passes end-to-end acceptance against A–F. Stage G acceptance opens implementation planning; it does not itself authorize a platform write.
