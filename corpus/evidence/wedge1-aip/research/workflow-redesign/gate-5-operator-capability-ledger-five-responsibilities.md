# Gate 5 operator capability ledger — five responsibilities

Status: **Gate 5 derivation; operator-capability input to later representation work**  
Date: 22 August 2026  
Authority: `REDESIGN_SEQUENCE.md`, `connected-operating-model.md`, `gate-5-operator-reground.md`, and `gate-5-core-ontology.md`

## Purpose and boundary

This ledger derives the minimum capabilities the cooperative or recognized producer-organization operations manager needs across Land, Funding, Applications, Field Work and Payments. It does not select Ontology types, status vocabularies, platform components, applications, navigation, screens or a final surface.

The unit of analysis is an **operator capability**: something the manager must be able to inspect, compare, select, explain, stage or execute, or hand off to answer the six accepted questions and preserve the next lawful route. The five responsibilities remain connected responsibilities, not software partitions.

The six questions are:

1. What changed, and what does it affect?
2. What is now required, allowed or blocked?
3. Who chooses, who decides and which route continues?
4. Is the route ready for the named action?
5. What was done, and who accepted it?
6. Did the intended result land, and what remains open?

This ledger contains **30 capabilities**: six operator capability classes for each of five responsibilities. A capability may answer more than one question. The count is not a proposed count of objects, Actions, Functions, Interfaces or any other implementation construct.

## Information and authority separation

Each capability uses four explicit layers.

- **Operator-visible state** is the smallest current, decision-ready projection the manager must inspect or act upon. It includes only facts, conflicts, clocks, consequences and bounded outcomes that can change the named decision or action.
- **Backstage evidence** supports, recalculates or audits that projection. It can include source documents, raw submissions, legal-corpus acquisition, provenance, extraction details, calculations and evidence files. It is reachable for substantiation but is not promoted into the manager’s primary operating state merely because it exists.
- **External authority outcome** is a decision or consequential result owned by a member, professional, contracting party, inspector, bank or public authority. CORDON records and propagates it; CORDON does not manufacture it.
- **Authorized Action need** states whether CORDON must only read, derive or prepare, or may perform a bounded mutation for an authorized actor. Any mutation requires an exact actor, target, scope, authority basis, refusal conditions, atomic effect and consequential record. Staging does not equal release, dispatch, acceptance or settlement.

A handoff carries the achieved bounded effect, the evidence needed by the receiver, the next responsible actor and any active clock. It never transfers decision authority.

## Cross-responsibility minimum

Every operator-visible projection below needs the same small connective bundle when applicable:

- the affected member, land and active or dormant line of work;
- the route family and named operator decision or action;
- the current responsible actor and next decision owner;
- the material changed fact, requirement, conflict or outcome;
- the active legal, administrative, commercial, biological or financial clock;
- the bounded consequence, scoped hold and next handoff; and
- enough basis references to explain the answer without exposing acquisition and ingestion mechanics.

There is no global case status, generic readiness, universal completion state or undifferentiated exception queue. A later event reopens only the decisions and actions whose premises it can change.

## 1. Land and official duties

### L1 — Inspect land change and affected work

**Questions answered:** 1, with inputs to 2 and 6.

**Manager capability.** Inspect the current member–land situation and see which compulsory, voluntary, application, field, payment or aftercare lines a material land, official-condition, duty, access or governing change affects.

**Minimum operator-visible information bundle**

- affected member and land identity at the grain needed to locate the consequence;
- current official condition, applicable area or regime, and any active duty;
- changed proposition and effective date or consequential event date;
- affected active or dormant work, grouped by independently governed route;
- unaffected work explicitly preserved where confusion would otherwise stop it;
- responsible actor, next decision owner, active clock and consequence of delay; and
- material unresolved source or scope conflict only when it can change the named decision.

**Backstage evidence.** Raw notices, legal acts, gazette copies, map-layer acquisition, source versions, extraction history, provenance and proposition-level reconciliation remain backstage. They support the visible answer and can be opened for substantiation.

**External authority outcomes.** Official condition, zoning, order, legal duty, appeal, stay, inspection result and legally effective publication remain outcomes of the competent body.

**Authorized Action need.** No Action is needed to create an official condition or duty. CORDON may detect a material change, recompute affected work and record that the manager acknowledged or routed the impact. It must refuse any request to alter an external official fact without a received authoritative correction.

### L2 — Compare applicable duties, permissions and scoped holds

**Questions answered:** 2 and 4.

**Manager capability.** Compare the currently applicable compulsory and voluntary routes on the same land without merging their authority, evidence, clocks or consequences.

**Minimum operator-visible information bundle**

- each applicable route and the proposition that activates it;
- what is required, allowed or blocked for the named next decision or action;
- the actor responsible for compliance and the authority competent for the proposition;
- conditional dependencies only when consequential, including public or civic land, Natura 2000, landscape, water, plant movement or certification;
- active clocks and the consequence of acting, waiting or missing the clock;
- conflicts or incompatibilities scoped to the actions they can change; and
- work that can continue non-binding or unaffected.

**Backstage evidence.** Full legal matrices, superseded provisions, all possible conditional rules, legal-corpus ingestion and detailed conflict-resolution work remain backstage unless they become consequential to the named action.

**External authority outcomes.** Permit, VIncA, water, landscape, plant-health and other competent decisions remain external outcomes. A Comune outcome counts only for the exact publication, asset, ordinance, enforcement, contracting, permit or programme role granted to it.

**Authorized Action need.** Comparison and applicability reasoning are derived support, not authority Actions. CORDON may stage a request to the actor who can resolve a material conflict or missing decision; it may not clear, waive or refuse a legal requirement.

### L3 — Select the land route to coordinate

**Questions answered:** 3, with consequences for 4.

**Manager capability.** Select which lawful route or combination of independent routes the cooperative will coordinate, while preserving decisions reserved to the responsible person, public-asset manager, member and competent authority.

**Minimum operator-visible information bundle**

- currently open route alternatives and any route that must continue regardless of cooperative preference;
- the chooser, decision owner and authority basis for each consequential choice;
- member participation or refusal where voluntary work is involved;
- cooperative mandate scope and any limit on acting for the member;
- current feasibility and the immediate consequence of each selection; and
- individual fallback when collective coordination is not selected or available.

**Backstage evidence.** Negotiation notes, unused alternatives and complete authority-document detail remain backstage unless needed to explain the selected scope or a refusal.

**External authority outcomes.** The competent authority establishes duty and official acceptance; the member chooses participation; the actual public-asset manager controls its work. Those outcomes are recorded, not selected by CORDON.

**Authorized Action need.** CORDON may record an authorized cooperative coordination selection or stage a member route choice for the member. It must refuse to convert cooperative selection into member consent, public duty, permit or official acceptance.

### L4 — Explain current land consequence and authority

**Questions answered:** 1–3 and 6.

**Manager capability.** Explain in plain operational terms why the current condition applies, what changed, who owns each decision, which work is held and what remains open.

**Minimum operator-visible information bundle**

- concise current proposition and consequence for the selected land and date;
- basis reference sufficient to support the proposition;
- jurisdiction, land scope and responsible actor;
- material disagreement and the exact decision it blocks;
- route-specific next step, next owner and deadline; and
- bounded statement of what the answer does not establish.

**Backstage evidence.** Source discovery, ingestion, ranking, extraction and non-consequential legal history remain backstage.

**External authority outcomes.** The explanation can quote or summarize an external decision but cannot recast it as a CORDON decision.

**Authorized Action need.** No mutation is required to explain. CORDON may generate a cited explanation and prepare a referral or notice package; release to an external recipient requires an authorized sender and preserved content.

### L5 — Stage land correction, access or compliance coordination

**Questions answered:** 4 and 5.

**Manager capability.** Stage the next land-side action that the cooperative is authorized to coordinate, such as requesting authoritative record repair, confirming cooperative-held access facts, arranging access, or preparing a response package.

**Minimum operator-visible information bundle**

- named action, target land and exact scope;
- actor authorized to act and authority or mandate basis;
- route-specific preconditions and unresolved refusal conditions;
- required recipient, supporting contents and deadline;
- intended atomic effect, such as “correction requested” or “access arrangement recorded”; and
- explicit distinction between preparation, release and external acceptance.

**Backstage evidence.** Raw identity records, title documents, correspondence drafts and transmission mechanics remain backstage until required in the staged package.

**External authority outcomes.** Source-record correction, access consent, official response and legal compliance determination remain outcomes of their owners.

**Authorized Action need.** CORDON may assemble and route the package, record an internally owned access arrangement, and register the consequential handoff. It must refuse release without sender authority, and must not mark the requested correction or compliance as achieved before the owner’s outcome arrives.

### L6 — Hand off a resolved land basis and reopen on change

**Questions answered:** 5 and 6, with feedback to 1.

**Manager capability.** Hand a usable land and duty basis to Funding, Applications or Field Work and later reopen only affected work when an official, access or land fact changes.

**Minimum operator-visible information bundle**

- achieved bounded result, such as current duty understood, access arranged or authoritative correction received;
- recipient responsibility and named next action;
- basis and evidence required by that recipient;
- surviving unresolved issue and its owner;
- active clock and consequence; and
- affected-work set to reopen if a premise changes.

**Backstage evidence.** Complete evidence packages and change-detection lineage remain backstage except for references required by the receiver.

**External authority outcomes.** Correction accepted, duty discharged, appeal stayed or compliance accepted remain externally owned outcomes.

**Authorized Action need.** CORDON may issue the handoff, propagate the received consequence and selectively recompute downstream readiness. It must not propagate a staged or pending request as a completed outcome.

## 2. Funding, adhesion and collective inclusion

### F1 — Inspect opportunity and population change

**Questions answered:** 1 and 2.

**Manager capability.** Inspect a material opportunity or governing change and the complete in-scope member–land–intervention population it affects, independent of member interest or management nomination.

**Minimum operator-visible information bundle**

- changed opportunity, rule, budget, deadline or governing fact;
- complete evaluated in-scope population and the basis of inclusion;
- affected and unaffected evaluations after selective recomputation;
- separate current indications for public eligibility, evidence readiness, adhesion, cooperative mandate, public concession and capacity status;
- action-specific deadlines and consequences; and
- unresolved facts with the actor able to cure them.

**Backstage evidence.** Population-building logic, raw programme documents, evaluation traces, source ingestion and unchanged calculations remain backstage but auditable.

**External authority outcomes.** Public eligibility, ranking, concession, budget movement and enterprise-status determinations remain public-authority outcomes.

**Authorized Action need.** CORDON may evaluate the complete in-scope population and selectively recompute affected work. It must not treat a computed result as public eligibility or concession.

### F2 — Compare viable member routes and feasible portfolios

**Questions answered:** 2 and 3.

**Manager capability.** Compare public, private, alternative and self-funded recovery paths and compare feasible collective portfolios under approved constraints and fairness safeguards.

**Minimum operator-visible information bundle**

- each member’s current recovery path and incremental opportunity from cooperative inclusion;
- route-specific public eligibility indication, readiness, member intent and mandate state kept separate;
- non-discretionary legal, safety, instrument, firm-commitment and resource constraints;
- approved fairness safeguards and permitted decision inputs;
- binding scarce resources and feasible schedules;
- expected realized Xylella-loss reduction, uncertainty and material sensitivity for each compared portfolio; and
- inclusion, deferral and fallback consequences.

**Backstage evidence.** Detailed model calculations, scenario generation, uncertainty propagation, raw biological estimates and discarded infeasible combinations remain backstage and reproducible.

**External authority outcomes.** Public admissibility, ranking and concession remain external. Existing private-resource commitments are facts from their actual owners, not public or cooperative decisions.

**Authorized Action need.** Portfolio computation is derived support. CORDON may produce a non-binding recommendation and alternatives under the approved policy; it must refuse to commit capacity through the comparison itself.

### F3 — Select pursuit and commit scarce cooperative capacity

**Questions answered:** 3 and 4.

**Manager capability.** Select whether the cooperative accepts the negotiated mandate and pursues an opportunity, then commit, override, release or reallocate cooperative-controlled capacity within governance authority.

**Minimum operator-visible information bundle**

- member adhesion and mandate terms, separate from public qualification;
- recommended feasible portfolio, alternatives, binding constraints and sensitivity;
- capacity state distinguished as tentative availability, reservation, firm commitment or scheduled use;
- authorized decision maker, override authority and review rights;
- exact resources, members, land, intervention scope and time window affected;
- individual fallback and consequence for deferred work; and
- recorded decision or override reason.

**Backstage evidence.** Full optimization traces, internal deliberation and non-material alternatives remain backstage unless needed for review.

**External authority outcomes.** Member participation, beneficiary funding-source election and public concession remain decisions of their respective owners.

**Authorized Action need.** An authorized cooperative role may use CORDON to record pursuit, accept a mandate within its scope, commit or release cooperative capacity, or override a recommendation with a reason. CORDON must refuse action by an unauthorized role, action beyond mandate, infeasible commitment, or mutation of public eligibility, concession, duty, creditor identity or fallback.

### F4 — Explain inclusion, deferral and fallback

**Questions answered:** 2, 3 and 6.

**Manager capability.** Explain why a member route is eligible or uncertain, included or deferred, what constraint binds, what alternative exists and what additional evidence could change the result.

**Minimum operator-visible information bundle**

- decision layer being explained: rule indication, member choice, cooperative mandate, pursuit, capacity or public outcome;
- inputs actually used and any approved safeguard or non-discretionary constraint;
- incremental expected-loss-reduction rationale and material uncertainty;
- nearest feasible alternative or materially equivalent option;
- consequence for the member’s existing recovery path; and
- next owner and change that would trigger reconsideration.

**Backstage evidence.** Full scoring, optimization and sensitivity artifacts remain backstage and linked for audit.

**External authority outcomes.** CORDON may explain a received public decision but cannot substitute its rationale for the public authority’s stated basis.

**Authorized Action need.** No mutation is required to explain. CORDON may prepare a decision record or member communication; sending it requires the authorized cooperative or beneficiary role.

### F5 — Stage adhesion, mandate and opportunity pursuit

**Questions answered:** 3 and 4.

**Manager capability.** Stage the distinct voluntary decisions and materials required before collective pursuit or capacity commitment, without collapsing them into one approval.

**Minimum operator-visible information bundle**

- member participation choice awaiting or received;
- negotiated cooperative execution mandate and scope;
- cooperative acceptance and pursuit decisions awaiting or received;
- action-specific missing evidence and cure owner;
- deadline, signing authority and funding-source election only when consequential; and
- exact effect that the next authorized release would create.

**Backstage evidence.** Draft agreements, identity evidence, signatures, correspondence and transmission details remain backstage until part of the release package.

**External authority outcomes.** Member adhesion and beneficiary election remain member or beneficiary outcomes. Public admissibility and concession remain authority outcomes.

**Authorized Action need.** CORDON may assemble adhesion or mandate packages, request missing contents, route for signature and record received decisions. It must refuse to infer consent from interest, release on behalf of a member without authority, or turn cooperative pursuit into public approval.

### F6 — Hand off the funded or fallback route

**Questions answered:** 3, 4 and 6.

**Manager capability.** Hand an authorized route to Applications or Field Work with the exact decision effects, commitments and open conditions they need, while preserving an individual or self-funded fallback.

**Minimum operator-visible information bundle**

- member, land, intervention and route scope;
- received member and cooperative decisions;
- capacity reserved or committed, with resource and time bounds;
- public opportunity basis and current authority outcome if any;
- unresolved application, permit, finance or evidence conditions;
- fallback route and consequence of collective deferral; and
- next owner, named action and clock.

**Backstage evidence.** Complete evaluation record, negotiation evidence and portfolio computation remain backstage.

**External authority outcomes.** Concession, public ranking and any member-specific entitlement remain externally owned and member-specific.

**Authorized Action need.** CORDON may issue the route handoff, reserve or release cooperative-controlled capacity under authorization, and selectively reopen the allocation when a material premise changes. It must not erase fallback or rewrite a public or member decision.

## 3. Applications and public review

### A1 — Inspect application change and affected decisions

**Questions answered:** 1, 2 and 4.

**Manager capability.** Inspect a changed farm-file fact, programme rule, authority request, deadline, filing result or permit result and identify exactly which preparation, release, review or downstream readiness decisions it affects.

**Minimum operator-visible information bundle**

- application route, beneficiary, land and intervention scope;
- changed fact or received request/outcome and consequential date;
- affected filing contents, named actions and downstream work;
- current owner of the underlying fact;
- active response or filing clock and delay consequence;
- unaffected preparation that may continue; and
- scoped hold where a binding release or commitment must wait.

**Backstage evidence.** Raw form versions, portal snapshots, ingestion, field-level diffs and complete correspondence remain backstage unless required to explain or cure the change.

**External authority outcomes.** Farm-file correction, professional judgment, permit decision, public admissibility, ranking and concession remain outcomes of their authorized owners.

**Authorized Action need.** CORDON may detect the impact, update the working preparation from received facts and reopen affected readiness. It must not edit an authoritative source record or invent an authority outcome.

### A2 — Compare filing routes, requirements and exceptions

**Questions answered:** 2 and 4.

**Manager capability.** Compare the requirements for preparing, releasing, repairing or responding on the actual route and identify the actor who can cure each missing or adverse premise.

**Minimum operator-visible information bundle**

- named application or response action;
- required contents, assertions, signatures, permits and source facts for that action;
- current availability, validity and owner of each material premise;
- consequential enterprise, continuity, cumulation, guarantee, permit or funding-source condition only when triggered;
- deadline and consequence of proceeding or waiting;
- route alternatives or repair path; and
- external decision owner.

**Backstage evidence.** Full form schemas, source-file payloads, all possible programme rules, validation logs and evidence files remain backstage.

**External authority outcomes.** The CAA owns authoritative farm-file repair within its role; the technician owns professional assertions; the beneficiary or authorized representative releases; the authority decides public exceptions and permits.

**Authorized Action need.** Requirement checking is derived support. CORDON may stage cure requests to the correct owner. It must refuse generic exception resolution or any action that silently substitutes one actor’s assertion for another’s.

### A3 — Select the filing or repair route

**Questions answered:** 3 and 4.

**Manager capability.** Select whether to continue preparation, route a source repair, seek a professional judgment, request a permit, respond to an authority, stage release or preserve a fallback.

**Minimum operator-visible information bundle**

- available route choices and the decision owner for each;
- readiness and refusal conditions for the named next action;
- consequence of each choice for deadline, concession, capacity and field readiness;
- mandate and signing-authority scope;
- member-specific rights preserved; and
- next handoff and evidence needed.

**Backstage evidence.** Draft iteration history and abandoned response strategies remain backstage unless needed for review.

**External authority outcomes.** The selected coordination route does not decide the source correction, professional conclusion, permit, filing certification or public review result.

**Authorized Action need.** An authorized cooperative role may select the coordination path and authorize preparation. Release remains a separate Action available only to the beneficiary or authorized representative within mandate.

### A4 — Explain application readiness and public result

**Questions answered:** 2–4 and 6.

**Manager capability.** Explain why the named filing action is ready or held, what actor can cure the hold, what a received public result decided and what it did not decide.

**Minimum operator-visible information bundle**

- named action and action-specific readiness result;
- satisfied and unsatisfied consequential conditions;
- actor and authority for every unsatisfied premise;
- active clock and consequence of release, waiting or non-response;
- received public outcome, scope, amount or conditions when applicable;
- resulting route changes and unaffected work; and
- bounded non-implications, such as concession not proving field readiness or cash.

**Backstage evidence.** Validation traces, complete application dossier and authority-document parsing remain backstage.

**External authority outcomes.** Public review, admissibility, ranking, concession, request for information and permit decisions remain external outcomes.

**Authorized Action need.** CORDON may generate a cited readiness or outcome explanation and prepare the next response. It must not represent a readiness calculation as certification or a public result.

### A5 — Stage or release an application action

**Questions answered:** 4 and 5.

**Manager capability.** Assemble the known contents, run action-specific checks, stage human review and, only for an authorized actor, release a filing or response.

**Minimum operator-visible information bundle**

- exact action: prepare, repair request, permit request, file, supplement, respond or withdraw;
- beneficiary, land, intervention and proceeding scope;
- current source facts and required professional assertions;
- signer or releaser and mandate basis;
- readiness result, unresolved refusal conditions and deadline;
- exact contents to be released and intended atomic effect; and
- expected receipt or authority response distinct from release.

**Backstage evidence.** Dossier files, form rendering, portal mechanics, validation logs and transmission payloads remain backstage but attached to the staged action.

**External authority outcomes.** Receipt, admissibility, ranking, concession, permit or rejection remain authority outcomes.

**Authorized Action need.** CORDON may prepare, validate and stage. It may release only when the acting beneficiary or representative has real authority and the action’s refusal conditions are clear. It must refuse missing authority, missing required professional judgment, expired or incompatible scope, and attempts to mark release as public acceptance.

### A6 — Hand off the application result

**Questions answered:** 5 and 6, with feedback to 1 and 4.

**Manager capability.** Hand a released filing, repair request or received authority outcome to the next owner and propagate only the bounded consequence to Funding, Field Work or Payments.

**Minimum operator-visible information bundle**

- what was released or received, by whom and when;
- receipt or outcome scope and authority;
- resulting entitlement, condition, request, hold or deadline;
- affected downstream named actions;
- evidence the recipient must use;
- unresolved issue and cure owner; and
- unaffected routes preserved.

**Backstage evidence.** Full submission and response packages remain backstage except for recipient-required references.

**External authority outcomes.** The authority’s decision remains its own outcome even after CORDON propagates it.

**Authorized Action need.** CORDON may route the handoff, update action-specific readiness and selectively reopen affected work. It must not translate a filing receipt into admissibility, a concession into dispatchability, or an application result into payment settlement.

## 4. Field Work

### W1 — Inspect readiness or field-result change

**Questions answered:** 1, 4, 5 and 6.

**Manager capability.** Inspect a changed duty, order, concession, approved change, self-funded authorization, access fact, permit, resource condition, field result or aftercare result and see which named field actions it affects.

**Minimum operator-visible information bundle**

- affected member, land, route, intervention and named field action;
- changed readiness premise or received execution/aftercare result;
- target scope and responsible field manager;
- affected reservations, commitments, schedule and downstream claims;
- active legal, biological, commercial or evidence clock;
- scoped hold and work that may continue; and
- next decision owner and consequence.

**Backstage evidence.** Sensor or observation ingestion, raw crew evidence, inventory feeds, source synchronization and non-consequential event history remain backstage.

**External authority outcomes.** Permit, compulsory acceptance, funded-work control, private acceptance and biological observations supplied by their owners remain received outcomes.

**Authorized Action need.** CORDON may recompute named-action readiness, flag affected commitments and stage rescheduling. It must not revoke a permit, acceptance or contractual outcome on its own.

### W2 — Compare execution routes, schedules and readiness

**Questions answered:** 2 and 4.

**Manager capability.** Compare voluntary owner delivery, forced or ARIF execution, municipal or other public-asset work, private funded work, self-funded work and local in-kind recovery where each is legally available.

**Minimum operator-visible information bundle**

- available route, executor, contracting authority and acceptance route;
- named action and required authority, target, access, technical design, permits, materials, lawful water, capacity, finance and evidence;
- current committed versus tentative resources;
- compatible and conflicting clocks;
- expected execution and aftercare consequences;
- evidence requirements that must shape procurement and work before spend; and
- fallback or rescheduling consequence.

**Backstage evidence.** Full supplier catalogs, all work instructions, detailed resource calculations and evidence schemas remain backstage until needed for the named action.

**External authority outcomes.** Public execution assignment, permit decisions, official acceptance and contract acceptance remain owned by their assigned actors.

**Authorized Action need.** Readiness and schedule comparison are derived support. CORDON may recommend a feasible route or schedule; it must not reserve, contract or dispatch through comparison alone.

### W3 — Select execution scope and commit cooperative resources

**Questions answered:** 3 and 4.

**Manager capability.** Select the cooperative-controlled execution route, target and schedule, and reserve or commit resources while respecting public, member, professional and contracting authority boundaries.

**Minimum operator-visible information bundle**

- selected member, land, intervention and physical scope;
- route and executor with actual authority;
- approved technical design and required public outcomes;
- resource availability, reservation, firm commitment and schedule distinctions;
- evidence and acceptance route;
- impact on other committed work and fallback; and
- authorized decision maker and reason for any override.

**Backstage evidence.** Detailed optimization, supplier negotiations and internal scheduling traces remain backstage unless required for review.

**External authority outcomes.** Technical judgment, permit, member authorization, supplier acceptance and public assignment remain outcomes of their owners.

**Authorized Action need.** An authorized field or cooperative manager may reserve or commit cooperative-controlled resources and select a schedule. CORDON must refuse commitments outside authority, beyond mandate, lacking a mandatory public outcome, or incompatible with a continuing legal duty or firm commitment.

### W4 — Explain dispatchability, performance and acceptance

**Questions answered:** 4–6.

**Manager capability.** Explain whether the named action is dispatchable, what was actually performed, which exceptions occurred, who accepted which result and what remains open.

**Minimum operator-visible information bundle**

- named action and action-specific readiness result;
- material satisfied and unsatisfied dispatch conditions;
- contemporaneous performed scope, exceptions and custody facts;
- executor’s performance assertion kept separate from beneficiary, technician, public-control and contractual acceptance;
- aftercare assignment and early survival or defect exposure;
- resulting claim eligibility or rework need; and
- next owner and clock.

**Backstage evidence.** Full photos, measurements, delivery records, chain-of-custody files and inspection documents remain backstage but reachable through the bounded result.

**External authority outcomes.** Compulsory legal completion, public control of funded work, private acceptance, defects and warranty determinations remain separate outcomes of their assigned actors.

**Authorized Action need.** CORDON may generate the explanation from recorded facts and stage requests for missing proof or acceptance. It must not infer acceptance from performance evidence or installation from procurement.

### W5 — Dispatch and record authorized field work

**Questions answered:** 4 and 5.

**Manager capability.** Dispatch a named field action when its exact route is ready and record contemporaneous execution, exceptions, materials, custody and proof within the cooperative’s authority.

**Minimum operator-visible information bundle**

- exact dispatch target, physical/material scope and executor;
- authorized dispatcher and authority basis;
- route-specific readiness result and refusal conditions;
- committed materials, services, capacity and lawful resource basis;
- work instructions, evidence obligations and acceptance owner;
- clocks, stop conditions and exception-routing rules; and
- atomic intended effect: dispatch issued, work record accepted from executor, or rework assigned.

**Backstage evidence.** Detailed work packs, supplier documents, telemetry, photos and raw execution records remain backstage and preserved as required evidence.

**External authority outcomes.** Contractor performance, official inspection, public acceptance and private acceptance remain externally owned even when CORDON records them.

**Authorized Action need.** CORDON may dispatch only for the responsible cooperative, member or public field manager within actual authority and only after named-action gates pass. It may record received execution evidence and assign internally controlled rework. It must refuse dispatch when authority, target, mandatory permit, access, design, committed resources, finance, clock compatibility or later-required evidence is materially missing.

### W6 — Hand off accepted work and aftercare exposure

**Questions answered:** 5 and 6.

**Manager capability.** Hand the correct bounded field result to Payments, Land and the aftercare owner without conflating installation, official compliance, payment eligibility, cash or biological establishment.

**Minimum operator-visible information bundle**

- performed and accepted scope, with each acceptance owner identified;
- evidence required for the next claim, compliance or warranty action;
- defects, excluded quantities, rework or residual duty;
- aftercare owner, protocol, review date and early survival exposure;
- payment-claim consequence kept separate from cash; and
- next action, owner and clock.

**Backstage evidence.** Full execution and acceptance packages remain backstage except for required handoff references.

**External authority outcomes.** Official compliance, funded-work acceptance, contractual acceptance and biological review remain separate received outcomes.

**Authorized Action need.** CORDON may issue the bounded handoff, start internally controlled aftercare coordination and selectively reopen Land, Payments or Field Work from a later outcome. It must not mark bounded establishment from installation acceptance or mark cash from an accepted work result.

## 5. Payments

### P1 — Inspect entitlement, claim or financial-result change

**Questions answered:** 1, 4 and 6.

**Manager capability.** Inspect a changed award rule, entitlement, accepted quantity, guarantee, claim result, liquidation, order, settlement, audit, revocation or recovery and identify exactly which financial and operational decisions it affects.

**Minimum operator-visible information bundle**

- beneficiary-specific programme and claim stage;
- changed rule or received financial outcome and consequential date;
- affected admissible spend, evidence, claim action, cash expectation or recovery exposure;
- creditor identity and member-specific right preserved;
- active submission, guarantee, audit or recovery clock;
- downstream field or aftercare work that is and is not affected; and
- next decision owner.

**Backstage evidence.** Accounting-source ingestion, bank-feed mechanics, invoice extraction, rule parsing and unchanged financial calculations remain backstage.

**External authority outcomes.** Public instruction, liquidation, payment order, bank settlement, audit, revocation and recovery remain separate outcomes of their assigned bodies.

**Authorized Action need.** CORDON may recompute claim readiness and affected exposure from received outcomes. It must not create entitlement, liquidation, order, settlement or revocation.

### P2 — Compare permitted claims, admissible spend and cash exposure

**Questions answered:** 2, 4 and 6.

**Manager capability.** Compare permitted advance, interim or final claim paths where the governing instrument allows them, engineer admissible procurement and evidence, and distinguish expected public accounting from actual cash.

**Minimum operator-visible information bundle**

- permitted claim stages and prerequisites under the actual award;
- beneficiary, entitlement, approved scope and ceiling;
- spend and quantity admissibility indication with unresolved exceptions;
- guarantee, procurement, execution and evidence conditions;
- claimable, requested, admitted, ordered and settled amounts kept separate;
- timing, liquidity and recovery exposure; and
- consequence of each claim option for continuing field and aftercare work.

**Backstage evidence.** Invoice-line detail, calculation workbooks, procurement files, evidence validations and cash forecasts remain backstage unless material to the comparison.

**External authority outcomes.** Admitted amount, public accounting decision, payment order and bank receipt remain external outcomes.

**Authorized Action need.** Comparison and admissibility preparation are derived support. CORDON may prepare a claim option and evidence plan; it must not treat the calculated claimable amount as an admitted or payable amount.

### P3 — Select claim strategy and authorized release path

**Questions answered:** 3 and 4.

**Manager capability.** Select the permitted claim stage and timing, arrange required guarantee or cure work, and identify the beneficiary or representative who can release it.

**Minimum operator-visible information bundle**

- allowed claim alternatives and their prerequisites;
- beneficiary-specific entitlement and creditor identity;
- current accepted scope, admissible evidence and unresolved exceptions;
- liquidity benefit, delay consequence and recovery risk;
- signer or releasing representative and mandate basis;
- effect on later claim rights and field obligations; and
- next owner and clock.

**Backstage evidence.** Scenario calculations, draft claim forms, bank or guarantee correspondence and unused alternatives remain backstage.

**External authority outcomes.** Guarantee issuance, public review, liquidation, order and settlement remain owned externally.

**Authorized Action need.** The manager may select the coordination strategy. Claim release is available only to the beneficiary or authorized representative. CORDON must refuse release by an unauthorized cooperative role or any strategy that exceeds the award’s permitted stages or accepted scope.

### P4 — Explain claim readiness and where the money is

**Questions answered:** 4–6.

**Manager capability.** Explain why a claim is ready or held, what amount has reached each public-accounting stage, whether cash actually settled and what audit, revocation or recovery exposure remains.

**Minimum operator-visible information bundle**

- claim stage and action-specific readiness;
- satisfied and missing evidence, guarantee or acceptance conditions;
- requested, admitted, liquidated, ordered and bank-settled amount with dates;
- source of truth for actual receipt;
- exception, reduction, audit, revocation or recovery owner;
- effect on remaining entitlement and open work; and
- next action and clock.

**Backstage evidence.** Full financial dossier, authority accounting records, bank documents and reconciliation traces remain backstage, with basis references visible.

**External authority outcomes.** Only bank or treasury evidence proves settlement. Public instruction, liquidation and order do not.

**Authorized Action need.** CORDON may generate a cited explanation and route a discrepancy for resolution. It must not infer cash from a public order or erase recovery exposure because cash arrived.

### P5 — Stage or release a payment claim

**Questions answered:** 4 and 5.

**Manager capability.** Assemble the claim and admissibility evidence before release, route required review, and release only for an authorized beneficiary or representative.

**Minimum operator-visible information bundle**

- exact claim stage, beneficiary, programme and award scope;
- requested amount and accepted quantities supporting it;
- required procurement, execution, acceptance, invoice, payment and guarantee evidence;
- readiness result and refusal conditions;
- authorized releaser and mandate basis;
- exact contents and intended atomic effect: claim released; and
- expected receipt and later authority decisions kept distinct.

**Backstage evidence.** Claim package files, invoice details, validation logs, portal payload and transmission record remain backstage but preserved for review and audit.

**External authority outcomes.** Claim receipt, admissibility, instruction, liquidation, order and settlement remain external outcomes.

**Authorized Action need.** CORDON may prepare, validate and stage the claim. It may release only for the beneficiary or authorized representative within mandate. It must refuse missing authority, unsupported amount, missing required acceptance or evidence, incompatible claim stage, or any instruction to record release as payment.

### P6 — Hand off settlement, shortfall or recovery exposure

**Questions answered:** 5 and 6, with feedback to 1.

**Manager capability.** Hand the exact financial result to the beneficiary, finance, Funding and Field Work, preserving remaining entitlement, shortfall, audit and recovery exposure and keeping aftercare active when appropriate.

**Minimum operator-visible information bundle**

- received authority or bank outcome and its owner;
- amount at each relevant stage and actual settlement evidence;
- beneficiary and creditor identity;
- remaining entitlement, reduction, shortfall or overpayment;
- audit, revocation or recovery status, owner and clock;
- field, warranty and aftercare work unaffected by payment; and
- next action, responsible actor and selective-reopening consequence.

**Backstage evidence.** Reconciliation files, full bank records, public-accounting documents and audit evidence remain backstage except for the basis references the receiver needs.

**External authority outcomes.** Settlement, audit, revocation and recovery are recorded exactly as received and remain owned by the competent bank, treasury or authority.

**Authorized Action need.** CORDON may propagate the received result, reconcile it to the claim, route a shortfall or recovery issue and reopen only affected funding or payment work. It must not close aftercare because payment settled, or reopen accepted field work unless the financial outcome actually changes it.

## Minimum authorized Action envelope

The 30 capabilities require a small authorization envelope rather than one mutation for every verb or responsibility.

1. **Detect and selectively recompute.** Identify a material received change, derive affected work and update only dependent answers. This does not change the underlying external fact.
2. **Prepare and stage.** Assemble a named package, recommendation, reservation, response, dispatch or claim for review. Staging has no release, commitment, dispatch, acceptance or payment effect.
3. **Route and request.** Send an authorized request or handoff to the actor who owns the next decision, carrying scope, evidence and clock. Routing does not transfer authority.
4. **Record an authorized cooperative decision.** Record mandate acceptance, pursuit, coordination selection, capacity reservation/commitment/release, schedule selection or override only when the acting cooperative role owns that decision.
5. **Release for an authorized principal.** Release an application, response or payment claim only for the beneficiary or representative whose real mandate covers the exact action.
6. **Dispatch authorized field work.** Issue a bounded dispatch only for the responsible field manager and only when the named action’s refusal conditions pass.
7. **Record a received bounded result.** Preserve who decided, performed, accepted, ordered, settled or reviewed what; then propagate only its actual consequence.

Read, compare, calculate, recommend and explain are necessary capabilities but do not themselves require state-changing Actions. They must not be inflated into mutation contracts.

## Universal refusal conditions

Any staged or executable Action must refuse when one or more of these material conditions applies:

- actor authority or mandate is absent, expired or narrower than the target scope;
- target member, land, intervention, proceeding, resource or claim scope is ambiguous;
- the action attempts to take a reserved member, professional, contractual, bank or public-authority decision;
- a mandatory route-specific premise is missing or materially conflicted;
- the requested effect collapses preparation, release, execution, acceptance, accounting, settlement or establishment;
- the action would overwrite an independent route, clock, creditor right, fallback or unaffected work;
- a staged, recommended or pending result is being represented as completed; or
- the requested mutation is generic workflow or status editing rather than a bounded real-world operation.

Every successful Action records only its atomic effect, actor, authority basis, target scope, consequential time, evidence reference and next handoff.

The manager can reach basis evidence when a consequential answer must be defended. The evidence does not become a parallel operating universe, and external decisions do not become CORDON Actions.

## Cross-responsibility handoff contract

Every handoff must answer seven things without requiring a generic dossier, task or case abstraction:

1. What bounded effect has actually occurred?
2. Which member, land, route, intervention or claim scope does it cover?
3. Who produced or decided it under what authority?
4. What minimum evidence must the receiver rely on?
5. Which named action is now possible, required or held?
6. Who owns that action, and what clock applies?
7. Which later change would selectively reopen this result?

A handoff can therefore carry an achieved effect and evidence without claiming that the entire member, land or recovery path is complete.

## Addressability and actionability test against the rough later context

This is a compatibility test only. It does not select a surface.

### Legal and jurisdiction addressability

The Land capabilities require the manager to retrieve the current applicable proposition by land, jurisdiction, responsible actor, route and consequential date; inspect material conflicts; and trace the affected work. This supports jurisdiction drilldown and legal-matrix queries without requiring all legal-corpus detail to be operator-visible or choosing any map, matrix or navigation behavior.

### Disease, zone, order and tree addressability

The Land and Field Work capabilities require official condition, area, order, target and execution scope to be locatable at the grain that changes a decision. Individual-tree addressability is required only when a valid external identity or validated reproducible inventory makes the tree an actionable target. Arbitrary detections do not create an operator identity. The requirement is addressability of the consequential fact, not a map-shaped data model.

### Authorized AIP actionability

The Action envelope supplies exact actor, authority, target, scope, refusal, atomic effect, evidence and handoff semantics. A later AIP harness could invoke those bounded operations without gaining generic authority, changing an external outcome or mutating an opaque global status. This tests actionability only; it does not select AIP architecture, automation, tools or interaction design.

## Deletion checks and verdict

The ledger is minimal at the capability level because deleting any one of the six capability classes from a responsibility causes a concrete operator loss:

- without **inspect**, the manager cannot identify material change or affected work;
- without **compare**, the manager cannot distinguish concurrent routes, requirements or consequences;
- without **select**, the manager cannot exercise the cooperative’s real choices while preserving reserved decisions;
- without **explain**, the manager cannot justify the bounded answer, authority, hold or consequence;
- without **stage/execute**, the manager cannot turn a ready route into an authorized real-world effect; and
- without **hand off**, the achieved effect cannot reach its next owner or selectively reopen downstream work.

The minimum information bundles preserve route independence, proposition-specific authority, named-action readiness, preparation/decision/execution/acceptance separation, public-accounting/cash separation, installation/establishment separation, member-specific rights and unaffected work. Backstage evidence remains available for substantiation, while external authority outcomes remain received facts rather than CORDON Actions.

**Verdict:** the five responsibilities require 30 operator capabilities and the seven-operation authorization envelope above. These are sufficient to proceed to minimum operator-visible representation and Action-contract derivation. They do not yet justify any Ontology type, Function, Interface, Foundry/AIP mapping or operator surface. Gates 6–7 remain closed.
