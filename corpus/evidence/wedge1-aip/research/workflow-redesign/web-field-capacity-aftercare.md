# Puglia Xylella recovery: field capacity, supply, inspection, irrigation, and aftercare

**Research date:** 20 August 2026  
**Scope:** Public web evidence only. Italian- and English-language searches covered ARIF, Regione Puglia/BURP, the Puglia open-data portal, EmPULIA, ANAC-indexed results, ARIF TUTTOGARE, irrigation bodies, nursery/RUOP materials, grant implementation acts, trade/professional guidance, and field studies. No local repository or prior report was read.

## Bottom line

Public evidence measures the **administrative and phytosanitary control system** much better than it measures the **commercial field-delivery system**.

1. **Compulsory removal has a statutory clock and a named public chain.** InnovaPuglia returns parcel/owner data within 3 working days; the Osservatorio issues the order within 7 working days; publication runs for 7 days; the owner has 3 days after publication to choose voluntary removal; voluntary or ARIF removal is due within 10 days. ARIF removals require two ARIF phytosanitary assistants; owner removals are checked by inspectors/agents.[S2][S4]
2. **Monitoring capacity is partly measurable.** ARIF publicly reported 122 technicians, 61 two-person teams, and five laboratories for the 2022 campaign.[S1] The open monitoring file contains 688,631 plant records dated 2020 through 30 June 2023, including 187,264 records dated 2022.[S6] The defensible derived workload is **3,070 recorded plants per 2022 team** if the 61-team announcement is treated as the campaign denominator. It is not a plants/team/day rate because public records do not expose active field days, travel time, repeat visits, or team continuity.
3. **Private grant-work demand is much larger and differently governed.** The 2020 replant call received 8,133 individual applications plus 26 collective applications, requesting removal/replant support for 3,829,991 trees on 31,900 ha against €222.08 million requested.[S7] By 31 May 2023, commitments covered 1,402 beneficiaries (€41.35 million), but only 108 of 191 advance requests had been processed and 99 beneficiaries had received €1.95 million.[S7] This is dossier/payment throughput, not contractor output.
4. **Plant demand is visible; supply capacity is not.** The 2025 SRD01.01B intake proposed 2,235,199 new trees over 3,220 ha in 365 projects.[S10] Public RUOP services expose registration and passport rules, but no public producer-level inventory, annual output, order book, or lead-time series.[S12][S13] The clearest supply stress signal is operational: grant rules allow up to an 18-month extension when a beneficiary produces a supply contract for enough certified plants with delivery due within that extension.[S9]
5. **Water access is structurally uneven.** The legacy Ugento–Li Foggi network reports 33 collective schemes, 12,741 commanded hectares, about 10,300 users, roughly 100 deep wells, 3,357 L/s aggregate pumping capacity, 201,265 m³ of storage, and a 16 April–30 September standard season.[S15] These are system assets, not proof that a given replant parcel has a connection, pressure, summer allocation, or affordable water.
6. **Public aftercare evidence stops at installation/compliance.** Final-payment rules require invoices, traceable payment, RUOP plant passports, and a joint beneficiary/technical-director declaration that the works were completed properly and that resistant/tolerant trees planted are at least equal to trees removed.[S9] They do not publish survival inspections, mortality/fallanza rates, irrigation logs, replacement obligations, defect cure times, or responsibility outcomes after payment.

### 2. Compulsory removal capacity: what is actually public

The compulsory chain is public and time-bounded:

1. Laboratory result enters the official chain.
2. InnovaPuglia supplies cadastral and owner data to the Osservatorio within **3 working days**.
3. The Osservatorio issues eradication/containment orders within **7 working days**.
4. The municipality publishes the order for **7 days**.
5. The owner has the publication period plus at most **3 days** after it ends to choose voluntary removal.
6. Voluntary removal is due within **10 days** of communication.
7. ARIF removal is due within **10 days** after owner communication, or within 10 days after owner silence.
8. Refusal triggers forced ARIF removal, notice to Prefect/police, cost recovery from the owner, and administrative enforcement.[S2][S4]

Responsibility is split, not outsourced end-to-end:

- **Owner/holder:** may perform voluntary removal; keeps clean wood; bears forced-removal costs after refusal.
- **ARIF:** public fallback executor; performs forced removal; manages removal indemnity instruction/liquidation.
- **Osservatorio Fitosanitario:** competent authority; orders, official controls, sanctions, second-level control.
- **InnovaPuglia:** cadastral/identity data handoff and system support.
- **Municipality:** publication/noticing channel.
- **Inspector/agent or two ARIF assistants:** witnesses/checks field completion.
- **Prefect/police:** access/refusal escalation.

**Public capacity finding:** the clock and minimum verifier staffing are measurable, but the execution fleet is not. Searches found no public current roster of ARIF removal crews, contractor framework lots, excavators/grinders, geographic depots, dispatch queue, completed removals/day, missed 10-day clocks, or forced-entry waiting time. The 2024 100% target is not a result.[S5]

### 4. Nursery and plant-supply capacity

#### What is measurable

- Final payment requires a plant passport from the producing professional operator registered in RUOP. Accepted categories include CAC and “certified”; the “virus-free” score is retained only when 100% of plants are certified.[S9]
- Planting of Leccino and FS-17 is registered through a public regional service.[S12]
- RUOP registration is publicly available as a service, and the Osservatorio conducts official nursery and movement controls.[S12][S4]
- The 2025 ARIF Restinco program can grant at most **100 plants per non-commercial intervention/project**, below 70 cm, for eligible public/non-profit/non-economic projects.[S11] It is an environmental/community program, not a material source for commercial grant hectares.
- If certified plants cannot arrive by the project deadline, a beneficiary may seek an extension of at most **18 months**, supported by supply contracts covering at least the eligible quantity and specifying delivery within the extension.[S9]

#### What is not publicly measurable

No retrieved public artifact supplied:

- number of RUOP olive nurseries actively producing Leccino or FS-17;
- current nursery inventory by cultivar, pot/age/height, CAC/certified class, and location;
- annual certified-stock output or propagation capacity;
- reserved versus uncommitted inventory;
- median quote-to-delivery time, cancellations, substitutions, or price series;
- lot rejection, plant-passport nonconformity, varietal mismatch, or virus-free failure rate;
- ARIF Restinco annual output or total allocation budget.

The professional field report gives a credible qualitative supply constraint: high FS-17 demand reduced availability of mature plants, leading some growers to install smaller plants requiring different management.[S14] The grant’s 18-month supplier-contract extension is the strongest official sign that lead time can bind.[S9] Neither source yields an industry-wide lead-time distribution.

### 5. Contractor, machinery, and technician capacity

No public award or lot specification was located for a current, Xylella-specific commercial framework covering private orchard clearance, ripping, planting, staking, drip installation, or aftercare. ARIF’s public procurement portal exposed a JavaScript shell and filters, but its server-rendered page contained no procedure rows; query-string searches did not return Xylella records.[S23] EmPULIA and general web/ANAC searches returned unrelated laboratory and health procurements, not a field-delivery lot.

The strongest capacity evidence is indirect:

- ARIF’s compulsory pathway appears primarily **public self-performance/owner choice**, not a published standing private removal framework.[S2][S4]
- In June 2025, the Region extended mandatory adult-vector treatment from 30 June to 10 July after Confagricoltura cited unavailable authorized products, unavailable licensed contract operators, and strong winds.[S18] This is direct evidence that short regulatory windows collide with contractor and input capacity.
- Professional guidance distinguishes “family” orchards from professional orchards by irrigation, mechanization, and skilled-labour availability. It reports that a 5 × 2.5 m, 800 trees/ha FS-17 system creates heavy manual-pruning demand and cites lack of specialist labour; a 4 × 1.5 m, 1,666 trees/ha system is designed for fully mechanized pruning.[S14]
- The same guidance states that high-density (400–600 trees/ha) systems require irrigation and that non-irrigated/extensive systems need larger, more lignified planting stock with stronger roots.[S14]

No defensible public crew-day standard was found for felling, stump removal, shredding, planting, drip installation, pruning, or replacement planting.

### 6. Irrigation capacity and parcel access

The former Ugento–Li Foggi district publishes unusually concrete physical capacity:[S15]

- 33 collective irrigation systems;
- 12,741 ha of commanded area;
- about 10,300 served users;
- system size range about 50–2,300 ha;
- 20 storage basins totaling 201,265 m³;
- 970,639 m of network, including 337,509 m asbestos-cement, 69,044 m steel, and 314,016 m PVC;
- about 100 wells, average depth roughly 100 m;
- 3,357 L/s aggregate pumping capacity;
- normal season 16 April–30 September; winter requests possible.

The page itself identifies parcel fragmentation as a cause of time and cost burden, and deep pumping as energy-intensive.[S15] Since 1 January 2024, the new Consorzio di Bonifica Centro-Sud Puglia has absorbed Arneo, Ugento–Li Foggi, Stornara e Tara, and Terre d’Apulia.[S16] Its published plan structure covers North and South Salento, but the retrieved material does not provide a current parcel-level serviceability, outage, pressure, allocation, queue, or tariff dataset.[S17]

**Operational interpretation:** commanded hectares are not irrigable hectares for a particular beneficiary. A field decision still needs connection status, hydrant distance, meter/contract status, summer allocation, pressure, outage history, water quality/salinity, pumping energy, and backup supply. The new SRD01.01B pipeline explicitly anticipates efficient irrigation investments, confirming that irrigation is part of project scope rather than an assumed public utility.[S10]

### 7. Field evidence, final checks, defects, and rework

The grant control packet measures installation and documentary conformity:[S9]

- final balance request within 30 days of the completion deadline;
- electronic invoices and supplier releases;
- traceable payment evidence;
- plant-purchase invoices and, where available, VAT register;
- RUOP plant passports and category evidence;
- joint declaration by beneficiary and licensed agricultural technical director that works are complete and properly executed;
- planted resistant/tolerant trees at least equal to removed eligible trees;
- registration of Leccino/FS-17 planting;
- completion communication and other land/antimafia documentation;
- administrative controls on every aided application and possible on-site controls.

The rules allocate defect consequences clearly:

- fewer trees removed than planned → contribution reduced;
- fewer trees planted than removed and aided → final-payment application inadmissible;
- more trees planted than conceded → no extra premium;
- material irregularity/nonconformity → reduction, exclusion, revocation, recovery, and legal interest;
- an unapproved/inadmissible variant leaves the beneficiary bound to the conceded project; non-delivery can revoke all aid.[S9]

But the public control design does not publish an **operational defect ledger**. It does not expose field-photo requirements, geotag coverage, failed inspections, cure notices, reinspection time, nursery replacement claims, dead-tree replacement, irrigation commissioning defects, or survival at 3/6/12/24 months.

### 8. Aftercare practice and responsibility

Professional evidence shows why installation is not completion:[S14]

- small FS-17 plants need more skilled establishment than mature, lignified stock;
- orchard density and cultivar must match irrigation and machinery;
- an 800 trees/ha layout can create avoidable specialist manual-pruning demand;
- early canopy management affects entry into production and structural durability;
- young orchards face serious injury from cicada oviposition, weevil, olive leaf moth/margaronia, bark midge, and scolytids;
- planting at late summer/autumn can reduce cicada exposure;
- biological systems may use row mulch initially and mechanical cultivation from about year three.

The public grant chain places immediate execution responsibility on the beneficiary and technical director. The nursery is responsible for compliant/passported stock. ARIF/Region controls admissibility and payment. The Osservatorio controls official phytosanitary compliance. **No retrieved instrument assigns a funded, measurable survival warranty or multi-year aftercare service to any one actor.** This is the largest responsibility gap.

## Operational bottlenecks

1. **Administrative concurrency, not just field labour.** The first call generated 226 appeals/reviews; the Region identified unavailable information systems, many appeals, MASAF–Region co-management, and staff shortage as delays.[S7]
2. **Qualification-data defects.** Missing IAP/direct-farmer status in AGEA farm files drove many score disputes.[S7]
3. **Supplier lead time.** An 18-month extension tied to certified-plant contracts is allowed; mature FS-17 shortage has already pushed growers toward smaller stock.[S9][S14]
4. **Contractor/input/weather compression.** The 2025 adult-vector deadline moved by 10 days because products and licensed contractors were unavailable and wind prevented operations.[S18]
5. **Water is parcel-specific.** Large commanded-area figures mask fragmentation, deep-well energy cost, aging network segments, and unknown parcel connection/pressure.[S15]
6. **Design-to-operating-model mismatch.** Density/cultivar choices can create labour and machinery demand the farm cannot support.[S14]
7. **Inspection bottleneck is unquantified.** Every removal needs a verifier, while ARIF removals need two. No concurrent capacity or inspection-day standard is published.[S2][S4]
8. **Payment evidence arrives after physical work.** The public state model is strong at invoice/passport/quantity verification but weak at pre-delivery scheduling and post-delivery survival.[S9]
9. **No shared exception ledger.** Public documents contain variants, withdrawals, missing delegation, inadmissibility, and later ranking scrolls, but no standardized reason/time-to-cure dataset.[S20][S21]
10. **Aftercare has no single accountable owner.** The beneficiary and technical director attest installation; the nursery certifies stock; the public bodies check compliance. None publishes a survival warranty or replacement SLA.

## Compulsory removal capacity versus private grant-work capacity

| Dimension | Compulsory eradication/containment | Private grant replant/recovery |
|---|---|---|
| Trigger | Official positive and DDS order | Beneficiary application and concession |
| Executor | Owner voluntarily or ARIF; forced ARIF fallback | Beneficiary’s private nursery/contractors/technician |
| Public clock | Explicit 3/7/7/3/10-day chain | Project deadline; final balance within 30 days; extensions/variants possible |
| Verification | Inspector/agent or two ARIF assistants | Administrative check on all aided applications; possible in-loco checks; beneficiary + technical-director attestation |
| Capacity denominator | Positive plants/orders | Applications, hectares, trees, conceded budget |
| Public machine/crew data | Not found | Not found |
| Failure consequence | Forced removal, cost recovery, sanction | Reduction, inadmissible balance, revocation/recovery |
| Aftercare | Not relevant after destruction except site/vector compliance | Essential but not publicly measured after installation |

Do not combine the two into a single “field capacity” estimate. ARIF’s statutory 10-day removal duty does not prove that private grant beneficiaries can source millions of trees, irrigation, land preparation, and aftercare on the same timetable.

## Interview and accesso-civico targets

### Priority 1: ARIF / Osservatorio — operational event extracts

Request machine-readable, de-identified 2023–2026 event tables:

- `order_id`, subspecies/regime, order date, notification start/end, owner choice date;
- executor type (`owner`, `ARIF`, `forced_ARIF`), scheduled date, actual removal date;
- number/species of trees, parcel count, comune, crew size, inspector count;
- machine type, travel/depot, treatment-before-removal flag;
- delay reason, refusal/access/police flag, rework/revisit, completion evidence;
- inspector/assistant anonymized ID and active field days.

Ask specifically for achieved distributions against the 3/7/10-day clocks and the 2024 100% outcome target, not only totals.

### Priority 2: official laboratory award and operations

Request DDS 144/2023, DDS 33/2024, contract/letters, and monthly 2024–2026 laboratory reports with:

- contracted ceiling and unit price per lab;
- allocated, received, accepted, rejected, repeated, positive, and completed samples;
- receipt-to-result and positive-to-confirmation timestamps;
- capacity/day, weekend operation, backlog, courier cutoffs, and data-upload latency;
- quality nonconformities and corrective actions.

### Priority 3: grant delivery and field checks

From ARIF/Regione/AGEA request a de-identified application-event table:

- concession, advance request/payment, supplier contract, start, completion, balance request/payment;
- hectares, planned/removed/planted trees, cultivar, density, nursery RUOP number hashed;
- contractor/technical-director pseudonymous identifier;
- irrigation investment and water-source class;
- variant, extension, inspection, defect, cure, reduction, revocation reason and dates;
- geotagged evidence availability (not owner names/images unless lawfully releasable).

This would distinguish **dossier delay**, **plant shortage**, **contractor delay**, **water/connection delay**, and **field failure**.

### Priority 4: nursery supply

Ask the Osservatorio and certification bodies for aggregate, non-commercially sensitive quarterly data:

- active RUOP olive producers by province and cultivar authorization;
- CAC/certified batches produced, passported, sold, rejected, and destroyed;
- available/reserved stock by age/size class;
- median/P90 order-to-delivery and cancellation/substitution rates;
- inspection nonconformities and corrective-action closure time.

Interview five nursery types: large national propagator, Puglia RUOP producer, cooperative supplier, ARIF Restinco, and a nursery serving high-density projects.

### Priority 5: contractors and technical directors

Interview 10–15 firms/technicians across Lecce, Brindisi, and Taranto. Capture:

- crew composition and machines;
- ha/day and trees/day by operation and orchard condition;
- travel radius, seasonal calendar, quote lead, backlog, minimum job size;
- subcontracting, weather loss, waste/wood handling;
- technician caseload and actual site visits;
- first-year callbacks, replant/fallanza responsibility, and warranty terms.

### Priority 6: Consorzio Centro-Sud and ARIF irrigation

Request GIS/service tables for:

- commanded versus actually connected/served parcels;
- hydrants, pressure zones, pipe material, meters, wells, storage, outages;
- application/contract dates, connection queue and refusal reasons;
- monthly delivered volume and restrictions by district;
- tariff, energy surcharge, arrears, water-quality/salinity measures;
- emergency/winter requests and response time.

Interview operations managers for North Salento, South Salento, and ARIF Lecce/Brindisi rural aqueducts.

### Priority 7: survival cohort

Design a de-identified cohort sample from paid projects at 3, 6, 12, and 24 months. Measure:

- live/dead/missing tree count and cultivar;
- irrigation presence/function and watering history;
- canopy establishment, pest injury, staking/shelter failure, weed competition;
- replacement date, payer, supplier/contractor warranty, inspection outcome;
- survival by stock age/size, density, water source, installer, nursery, and planting month.

Without this cohort, “replanted trees” remains an installation count, not restored productive capacity.
