# Cooperative/OP governance and execution practice for collective agricultural and Xylella applications

**Research date:** 20 August 2026  
**Lane:** public-web evidence only  
**Focus:** the private operating layer between a collective application and work on each member's land  
**Evidence rule:** statements marked **PUBLIC FACT** are supported by a cited public artifact. Statements marked **INFERENCE** are reasoned implications, not observed practice. **GAP** means the searched public corpus does not answer the question.

## Bottom line

The Puglia Xylella replanting measure is not a single cooperative grant. It is an **aggregated application with member-level legal and financial liability**:

1. The OP or oil cooperative submits the collective container and may choose only some eligible member adhesions after private “trattative intercorse.” An unselected adhesion is treated as an individual application.[S1][S2]
2. The members remain the beneficiaries. Each receives a concession, gives any advance guarantee, submits or supports the final-payment evidence, and carries revocation exposure and the 15-year land-use obligation.[S1][S2][S8]
3. The collective body receives a mandate to contribute to or perform removal and replanting under terms agreed privately when the mandate is given. The public rules do **not** publish those terms, do not state whether the OP is principal contractor or agent, and do not allocate supplier default, cost overruns, dead-tree replacement, warranty claims, or routine aftercare.[S1][S2]
4. Public comparable programmes show the contractual controls that the Xylella public layer omits: a written mandate; a partner-by-partner financial plan; explicit authority to contract; supplier-quote and conflict checks; a dedicated account; a choice between central payment and member reimbursement; exit/substitution rules; attribution of sanctions; and continuation rules if a partner fails.[S17][S19][S20][S21][S22]

The operating redesign should therefore treat a “collective application” as a **federation of member work packages joined by a cohort agreement**, not as one indivisible dossier. The missing private mandate/agreement is the controlling document. It must be obtained from practitioners before any workflow is treated as complete.

## Scope and method

I searched in Italian and English for:

- Xylella collective applications, mandates, agreements, rankings, concessions, advances, balances, variants, implementation reports, disputes, consultants, and webinars;
- public OP/cooperative statutes, internal service/conferment rules, assembly controls, member sanctions and withdrawal;
- Italian PIF/PIT/filiera/distretto/PEI-AGRI cooperation agreements and contracts;
- supplier selection, quote requirements, conflicts, outsourcing, dedicated funds, member reimbursement, overruns, penalties, defects, aftercare, withdrawal, exclusion, replacement, and project failure.

The search followed direct programme names and the collective applicants named in the official ranking (A.P.OL., ATEPROL, APROL Lecce, Oro di Puglia, named oil cooperatives). It then followed the strongest comparable regimes: MASAF Xylella district contracts, Toscana integrated supply-chain projects, the national PEI-AGRI operational-group agreement, Sicilia PIF 2026, and OP operational-programme rules.

No local repository, session history, or prior research report was read. No software, ontology, Foundry/AIP, challenge criteria, or screens were researched.

## Sourced findings

### 1. What “collective” means in the Puglia Xylella call

**PUBLIC FACT.** The OP/cooperative is not the aid beneficiary. Each adhering landholder is the beneficiary and receives a concession.[S1][S8]

**PUBLIC FACT.** The collective body presents the collective application and contributes to removal and replanting under conditions agreed “d’intesa tra le parti” when the mandate is conferred. The mandate must exist no later than collective submission; evidence of it is required at payment or final balance, on pain of loss and revocation.[S1]

**PUBLIC FACT.** The collective body sees the adhesions associated with it and can select only some members of its social base for whom, “in virtù delle trattative intercorse,” it intends to exercise the mandate. A valid adhesion not selected in the collective application becomes an individual aid application.[S1][S2]

**INFERENCE.** There are therefore two distinct decisions:

1. **public eligibility/ranking** of a member and cohort; and
2. **private willingness-to-serve** by the OP/cooperative after negotiations.

The second decision can exclude a member from collective execution even when the member is formally eligible. The public corpus does not reveal the bargaining variables.

### 2. How cohorts are selected

#### Public programme selection

**PUBLIC FACT.** The original measure reserved €19.8 million for individual operations and €19.8 million for collective operations.[S2][S3]

**PUBLIC FACT.** Collective applications were ranked using aggregate cohort characteristics: olive specialisation; aggregate economic size; age/location of damage; share of IAP/CD members; and commitment to virus-free certified planting material.[S2]

**PUBLIC FACT.** The official ranking lists 26 collective applications. Cohorts ranged from 4 to 126 adherents. Requested collective aid totalled €22,599,153.02. The highest-ranked A.P.OL. cohort had 10 members and requested €659,870; ATEPROL had 13 and requested €1,185,384.60; the largest listed cohort, San Giorgio, had 126 and requested €1,786,240.80.[S4]

**PUBLIC FACT.** The 31 May 2023 report says 8,133 applications were submitted, including 1,029 adhesions and 26 collective applications. Initially, 23 collectives with 823 adhesions were admitted to technical-administrative review; later all 26 with 879 adhesions were admitted. Thus, not every adhesion filed was carried forward inside a collective cohort.[S6]

#### Private cohort selection

**PUBLIC FACT.** The OP/cooperative can select only some adhesions after negotiations.[S1]

**GAP.** No public source found states:

- whether selection is first-come, board-discretionary, geographic clustering, minimum hectares, creditworthiness, technical feasibility, expected margin, member seniority, or willingness to pre-finance;
- whether a member receives a written reason for exclusion;
- whether directors abstain when their own farms or relatives are candidates;
- whether rejected members can appeal internally;
- whether the board or assembly approves the cohort.

**INFERENCE.** A collective ranking based on aggregate properties can create internal gaming pressure. Adding or removing one member can change the cohort’s aggregate size, IAP/CD share, damage-age score, and total request. The OP therefore needs a frozen cohort snapshot and a recorded rule for changes.

### 3. How negotiations work

**PUBLIC FACT.** The Xylella FAQ acknowledges “trattative intercorse” but gives no agenda, price, or approval process.[S1]

**PUBLIC FACT.** Comparable OP practice makes negotiation more observable. Terre dell’Etruria prepares the annual production programme through product assemblies attended by board representatives, commercial managers, technicians/agronomists and producer members. Quantities are agreed so the cooperative can meet commercial commitments.[S23]

**PUBLIC FACT.** Sicilia PIF requires the agreement to state sale/purchase quantities, price parameters, quality, list or production-cost references, duration, reciprocal liabilities, penalties, and a ten-year term.[S20]

**INFERENCE.** A robust Xylella negotiation likely needs at least four bargains that the public call leaves private:

1. scope per parcel and permitted technical design;
2. member cash requirement and timing;
3. procurement/contracting model and price basis;
4. aftercare, mortality, warranty and failure allocation.

### 4. Fairness and conflicts

#### Between applicants

**PUBLIC FACT.** Public ranking criteria and scores are published. Equal scores and programme thresholds are handled at the public layer. Administrative disputes were numerous: the 2023 report records 24 hierarchical appeals and 202 self-review requests, mostly over missing IAP/CD status in farm files.[S4][S6]

**PUBLIC FACT.** Sicilia PIF explicitly says participation must observe transparency and equal opportunity.[S20]

#### Within a cooperative/OP

**PUBLIC FACT.** OP operational-programme rules require an assembly resolution proving every producer had the opportunity to benefit from the operational fund and participate democratically in decisions on its use and member contributions.[S21]

**PUBLIC FACT.** For suppliers, OP rules require two quotes below €50,000 and three at or above €50,000 (regions may require more). Quotes must be comparable, from distinct independent suppliers without links or potential conflicts. The lowest quote is the default; a higher-price choice needs a technical/economic justification. A December 2024 clarification reiterates three comparable quotes generally and prohibits a supplier choice that creates a director’s personal conflict.[S21][S22]

**PUBLIC FACT.** Bovinitaly’s statute places contribution-setting with the assembly, while the board administers admissions, recesso, exclusion and sanctions. The rules provide written notice and judicial opposition rights for exclusion.[S24]

**GAP.** No Xylella applicant’s public site yielded the board resolution, member resolution, conflict declaration, scoring sheet, or service charter used to select its Art. 6 cohort.

**INFERENCE.** Democratic OP-fund requirements do not automatically prove democratic selection for this separate Xylella mandate. The control must be checked in the actual mandate, internal regulation and approving resolution.

### 5. Who contracts suppliers and contractors

#### Xylella Art. 6

**PUBLIC FACT.** The OP/cooperative is mandated to perform or contribute to removal/replanting for selected members.[S1][S2]

**PUBLIC FACT.** For final balance/adaptations, the beneficiary and works director/qualified technician sign; for a member adhesion, the collective body’s legal representative also signs.[S7][S8]

**GAP — decisive.** The public Xylella corpus does not say whether:

- the OP signs one master works/supply contract in its own name;
- the OP signs as disclosed agent for each member;
- each member signs directly with nursery and contractor;
- the OP provides labour/equipment itself and purchases only plants;
- the technician is retained by the member, cohort or OP;
- invoices are addressed to the member or OP;
- title and risk in planting material pass to the member at delivery or after acceptance.

No public tender, request for quotes, award notice, supplier framework, nursery contract, planting contract, bill of quantities, defect protocol or completion certificate for a named Art. 6 collective applicant was located.

#### Comparable practice

**PUBLIC FACT.** Sicilia PIF authorises the capofila to sign, in the name and on behalf of mandants, all contracts necessary to manage and execute the project. Yet each direct beneficiary remains responsible for its operation, receives its own aid, and bears its own failure damages.[S20]

**PUBLIC FACT.** OP programme rules permit the OP/AOP to present supplier quotes on behalf of members. If work is outsourced, the assembly (or board, later ratified) must authorise it and a written contract/protocol/convention must define services, objectives, termination and controls. The OP remains responsible for management, control and supervision.[S21]

**PUBLIC FACT.** The PEI-AGRI model gives the capofila representative power and allows it to sign project acts, but ties activities and budget to named partners.[S19]

**INFERENCE.** The Xylella collective model could lawfully use more than one contracting architecture. A workflow must not assume “the cooperative contracts” from the existence of a mandate alone. It needs explicit fields for `contracting_principal`, `invoice_addressee`, `payer`, `asset_recipient`, `warranty_holder`, and `claim_controller` per work package.

### 6. Who pays and who pre-finances

#### Xylella Art. 6

**PUBLIC FACT.** Each member beneficiary, not the cooperative, must supply the guarantee for an advance. Concession acts specify a guarantee of 110% of the requested advance.[S1][S8]

**PUBLIC FACT.** No progress-payment (SAL) application is allowed. Apart from an advance, the member requests only final balance after completion and supplies purchase/execution evidence.[S7]

**PUBLIC FACT.** The contribution is forfait-based rather than conventional cost reimbursement, although final payment still requires plant-purchase and execution evidence.[S1][S7]

**GAP.** The public rules do not state whether the cooperative temporarily pays contractors, whether members pay deposits, whether the cooperative obtains working-capital credit, whether guarantee fees are pooled, or how the contribution is routed to the cooperative if it paid the supplier.

**INFERENCE.** The legal beneficiary bears the public-law funding gap, but the private mandate may move cash operationally. “Beneficiary” and “payer” must be separate concepts.

#### OP operational programmes

**PUBLIC FACT.** The operational fund is financed partly by EU aid and partly by OP funds or member contributions. The OP must pre-finance the amount required to complete the fund while awaiting EU balance. EU support is generally 50% of eligible actual spend.[S21]

**PUBLIC FACT.** A dedicated account receives member contributions, OP contributions, EU advances, and temporary OP/member advances. It pays OP-incurred supplier costs or reimburses member-incurred costs. A member reimbursement can be offset against sums the member owes only with written authorisation and accounting evidence.[S21]

#### PEI-AGRI / PIF / district

**PUBLIC FACT.** The PEI-AGRI model permits two patterns: the capofila receives payment into the GO account and distributes it according to each partner’s supported costs within a specified number of days, or each partner claims and receives directly.[S19]

**PUBLIC FACT.** Sicilia PIF uses member-level claims and receipts; each beneficiary retains autonomy for its grant.[S20]

**PUBLIC FACT.** District contracts can advance up to 40% against an unconditional guarantee and otherwise pay by progress. Individual beneficiary spending evidence passes through the proponent.[S14]

### 7. Costs, overruns and unbudgeted work

**PUBLIC FACT.** Xylella public aid is a fixed/forfait contribution. The public documents do not promise to cover actual overrun.[S1][S2]

**PUBLIC FACT.** District-contract changes, including cost increases and new interventions, cannot increase the aid granted to that district contract.[S14]

**PUBLIC FACT.** In the PEI-AGRI model, each party bears costs it incurs autonomously outside the approved financial plan.[S19]

**PUBLIC FACT.** In Sicilia PIF, each beneficiary bears consequences of non-performance of its own operation. The capofila is not liable for failures exclusively attributable to another direct beneficiary.[S20]

**GAP.** No public Xylella mandate allocates:

- difference between contractor price and forfait aid;
- quantity growth after site inspection;
- inflation or nursery-price changes;
- extra site preparation, irrigation, access or disposal costs;
- additional professional/permit costs;
- cost of correcting member-supplied cadastral or title errors;
- unrecoverable VAT or guarantee fees.

**INFERENCE.** Absent a private pooling clause, the safest presumption for workflow design is **no cross-subsidy**: each member work package carries its own approved aid, cash calls, committed cost, forecast overrun and final variance. That is a conservative data model, not evidence of actual practice.

### 8. Defects, warranties and aftercare

**PUBLIC FACT.** The Xylella programme requires regular execution, compliant varieties and traceable planting material. It imposes a 15-year destination/use obligation on the member beneficiary.[S2][S7]

**PUBLIC FACT.** Technical/economic adaptations can be made during work by the beneficiary and technical director; for collective adhesions the OP representative also signs the supporting report.[S7]

**PUBLIC FACT.** The OP outsourcing comparator says the OP remains responsible for supervising outsourced activity.[S21]

**GAP — strongest negative finding.** No searched public Xylella artifact assigns:

- inspection/acceptance authority between contractor, technician, OP and member;
- latent-defect claims;
- nursery warranty and phytosanitary rejection;
- replacement of dead trees during establishment;
- irrigation, pruning, fertilisation, pest control or weed management after planting;
- response times, service levels, retention, performance bond or defects-liability period;
- ownership of manuals, as-builts, plant passports, test reports or warranty documents;
- who bears loss from drought, vandalism, disease, member neglect or contractor workmanship.

**INFERENCE.** Public-law “regular execution” is not a private defect warranty. Passing the grant’s final inspection does not settle who must correct later plant mortality. The workflow needs a separate aftercare/warranty plan and claim register.

### 9. Member withdrawal, exclusion or failure

#### Xylella Art. 6

**PUBLIC FACT.** Before collective submission, a valid adhesion omitted by the collective becomes individual.[S1][S2]

**PUBLIC FACT.** After concession, beneficiary change is a formal variant and must preserve eligibility and ranking position. Adaptations and specified failures are handled at beneficiary level; the 2023 concession act says certain non-compliance causes revocation “limitatamente ai soggetti interessati.”[S7][S8]

**GAP.** No public rule explains the full post-submission split if one member withdraws, loses title, refuses a cash call, blocks site access, fails a permit, becomes insolvent, or causes the cohort score to fall. It is not public whether remaining members must absorb shared mobilisation/design cost or whether the OP may substitute another member.

#### Comparable rules

**PUBLIC FACT.** Toscana’s model makes the capofila liable for harm caused by failure to perform its duties and makes participants liable to one another for their own breaches. Its exit clause expressly tells parties to regulate effects on costs already incurred.[S17]

**PUBLIC FACT.** The PEI-AGRI model requires written cure. Persisting breach can lead to unanimous exclusion and indemnity. The excluded partner still pays its share of costs and obligations already incurred and can owe additional damages. A withdrawing partner must have grave justified reasons, pays costs incurred in its interest and existing commitments, and may owe greater damages. A replacement must take the same role and activities; the managing authority must approve the composition change.[S19]

**PUBLIC FACT.** Sicilia PIF requires the constituent agreement to commit remaining parties, after recesso/exclusion/termination, to complete project objectives and, where possible, redistribute activities and costs. However, the entire PIF loses funding if minimum participation, mandatory interventions, agreement duration, or project objectives cease to hold. Already paid aid must be returned with legal interest on total abandonment.[S20]

**PUBLIC FACT.** Under DM 7775 district contracts, a replacement after recesso/exclusion can be requested only if no aid has yet been disbursed and, for an already signed contract, within three months. Any variation must preserve admissibility and economic-financial balance.[S14]

**PUBLIC FACT.** Terre dell’Etruria requires a departing member who received operational-programme aid before the binding period expires to repay the full aid unless the member joins another recognised OP.[S23]

**PUBLIC FACT.** Bovinitaly’s OP members face a three-year minimum relationship and twelve-month notice. The board can exclude for arrears, grave breach, two years of unpaid contributions, prolonged non-conferment or serious harm; the member can oppose exclusion in court.[S24]

### 10. Real implementation shows the consequences of split liability

**PUBLIC FACT.** By 31 May 2023, all 26 collective applications with 879 adhesions had been admitted to review, but concessions and advances were still made to individual beneficiaries. The report recorded €41.35 million committed to 1,402 beneficiaries, 191 advance requests for €3.65 million, 108 admitted for €2.07 million, and 99 beneficiaries liquidated for €1.95 million.[S6]

**PUBLIC FACT.** The same report attributes delay to numerous appeals, initial absence of an information system, and MASAF–Region co-management. It does not report private execution performance by cooperative, supplier, work cohort, plant survival, aftercare, or defects.[S6]

**INFERENCE.** Public monitoring counts applications and money at beneficiary level, but not the collective body’s service delivery. This is why the operating layer remains invisible even in an unusually detailed implementation report.

## Operating model implied by the evidence

The following is a **design inference**, not a claim that all OPs currently work this way.

### A. Objects that must remain separate

1. **Collective application** — public container and aggregate ranking.
2. **Member adhesion** — member eligibility, land, requested area and aid.
3. **Private mandate/service agreement** — authority, fees, cash calls, execution and exit rules.
4. **Member work package** — parcels, scope, price, permits, supplier, technician, milestones.
5. **Supplier contract/work order** — legal principal, invoice addressee, warranty and acceptance.
6. **Funding account** — member aid, guarantee, advance, balance, member/OP pre-finance.
7. **Aftercare plan** — owner, tasks, dates, evidence, mortality and warranty claims.
8. **Cohort-change event** — nonselection, withdrawal, substitution, failure or expulsion and effect on ranking/admissibility.

### B. Decision rights that require explicit agreement

| Decision | Public Xylella rule | Missing private decision |
|---|---|---|
| Select member into cohort | OP may select after negotiations | criteria, conflict/abstention, reason, appeal |
| Freeze scope | member land and eligible operation | who approves quantities/design and later changes |
| Choose supplier | not specified | quote process, evaluator, related-party rule, award record |
| Sign contract | mandate exists | OP as principal/agent vs member as principal |
| Pay deposit/invoices | member is aid beneficiary | actual payer, routing, reimbursement, cash-call timing |
| Accept work | regular-execution evidence | technical acceptance, member sign-off, deemed acceptance |
| Claim defects | not specified | warranty holder, response time, retention/bond, escalation |
| Maintain planting | member has 15-year use obligation | whether OP provides aftercare and at what fee |
| Handle member failure | beneficiary-specific remedies partly visible | shared-cost split, substitution, cohort rerating, litigation |

## Failed hypotheses and bounded negatives

### Hypothesis 1: “The formal Xylella call defines the collective contract.” — **FAILED**

The call and FAQ expressly defer operating terms to a private mandate agreed by the parties.[S1][S2]

### Hypothesis 2: “Because the OP submits collectively, it receives and allocates the grant.” — **FAILED**

The member is the beneficiary, receives the concession and must give its own guarantee.[S1][S8]

### Hypothesis 3: “Every eligible member who files an adhesion is included.” — **FAILED**

The OP may select only some after negotiations; unselected adhesions become individual. Official totals also show fewer adhesions inside final collective cohorts than adhesions filed.[S1][S6]

### Hypothesis 4: “Public procurement notices will reveal contractor selection.” — **NOT SUPPORTED / bounded negative**

Searches combined `Xylella`, `reimpianto`, `espianto`, `cooperativa`, `OP`, `gara`, `appalto`, `capitolato`, `fornitore`, the names of ranked collectives, and the named programme. They returned public-authority phytosanitary procurements, the aid call and rankings, but no procurement/award artifact for a named collective Art. 6 applicant. Private cooperatives are not necessarily public-contracting authorities; absence from public procurement portals therefore does not prove no competitive process.

### Hypothesis 5: “Published OP statutes resolve project failure.” — **PARTLY FAILED**

Statutes resolve membership, contributions, arrears, recesso and exclusion, but do not allocate this project’s supplier contract, overrun, warranty or aftercare. The project mandate/internal regulation is still needed.[S23][S24]

### Hypothesis 6: “Programme implementation reports measure cooperative delivery.” — **FAILED**

The 31 May 2023 report measures applications, appeals, concessions, advances and payments. It does not report supplier performance, completion by collective, cost variance, defects, survival or maintenance.[S6]

### Hypothesis 7: “A public court dispute reveals the mandate terms.” — **FAILED within searched corpus**

Searches for Xylella collective-application appeals and cooperative replant litigation found public-law eligibility/replant cases and generic cooperative-member exclusion cases, but no published decision construing an Art. 6 collective mandate, supplier defect, member cash call or cohort split. This is a bounded search result, not proof no dispute exists.

### Hypothesis 8: “Webinars/videos expose the practical playbook.” — **FAILED within indexed public web**

Italian and English searches for webinar, video, transcript, consultant, workshop, and collective application returned programme pages, FAQs, public meetings and general Xylella agronomy. No transcript or recording was located that disclosed a cooperative’s member selection, pricing, supplier contract, pre-finance or aftercare process.

## Exact interview questions and document requests

Ask these verbatim. Request the document immediately after the answer.

### Cohort formation

1. “Please walk me through the last collective Xylella cohort from the first member expression of interest to the final SIAN collective submission. At each step, who could say yes or no?”
2. “Of all valid adhesions linked to your cooperative, how many did you include, how many did you leave out, and what written rule determined that split?”
3. “Did geography, contiguous parcels, minimum hectares, member seniority, IAP/CD status, creditworthiness, expected service margin, plant design, or ability to pre-finance affect selection?”
4. “Who approved the final cohort: staff, president, board, product assembly, or full assembly? Please provide the resolution and minutes.”
5. “Did excluded members receive a reason and an internal review or appeal route? Please provide one anonymised notice.”
6. “When a director, employee or close relative was also an applicant, what abstention or conflict procedure applied? Please provide the declaration or minutes.”

### Negotiation and mandate

7. “What exactly was negotiated in the ‘trattative intercorse’ before you exercised the mandate: hectares, design, contractor, price, fees, cash timing, warranty, aftercare, or something else?”
8. “Please provide the exact mandate/service agreement signed with one member, including annexes, fee schedule and privacy/delegation forms.”
9. “Was the mandate revocable after collective submission? If yes, on what notice, cost and consequence for the public application?”
10. “Could the cooperative change scope, supplier or price without a fresh member signature? Which clause authorised it?”
11. “What authority did the member give you: submit only, procure, sign contracts as agent, pay invoices, receive grant money, inspect work, pursue warranty claims, or all of these?”

### Contracting and procurement

12. “For the last completed member parcel, who was the legal customer on the nursery invoice and who was the legal customer on the contractor invoice?”
13. “Did the cooperative sign one master contract, member-specific work orders, or did each member contract directly? Please provide a redacted example of each document used.”
14. “How many quotes did you obtain for plants, removal, soil preparation, planting, irrigation and technical services? Please provide the comparison table and award decision.”
15. “What rule applied if the cheapest supplier was not selected?”
16. “Were any suppliers owned by, managed by, or related to a director, employee or member? How was that conflict recorded and approved?”
17. “Who selected and retained the works director/technical professional, and whose interest did that professional contractually represent?”
18. “Did the supplier contract identify parcel-level quantities and prices so costs could be split if one member withdrew?”

### Payment and pre-finance

19. “Who paid the first euro before public aid arrived: the member, cooperative, bank, supplier credit, or a mix?”
20. “Did each member provide the 110% guarantee, and who paid guarantee fees and interest?”
21. “Did grant money ever pass through the cooperative’s account? If so, under what written authorisation and reconciliation rule?”
22. “Were contractor payments pooled and then allocated, or invoiced per member? Please provide an anonymised cash ledger from commitment through final balance.”
23. “Did you deduct service fees or member contributions from grant or product-conferment payments? Please provide the member’s written compensation authority.”
24. “What happened when a member could not make a cash call on time? Was work paused, financed for them, removed from the cohort, or cross-subsidised?”

### Cost and overrun allocation

25. “What was the original budget, final cost and grant for three completed member work packages, including cooperative fee, technician, guarantee and finance cost?”
26. “Who bore costs above the forfait contribution?”
27. “How did you allocate common mobilisation, surveying, design, machinery and administration costs: hectares, trees, hours, equal share, actual use, or another basis?”
28. “Who could approve an overrun, and what written member consent was required?”
29. “If one member failed after common costs were committed, did that member, the cooperative, the contractor or remaining members absorb the stranded cost?”

### Completion, defects and aftercare

30. “Who signed practical completion and who could reject defective or non-conforming work?”
31. “What percentage of contract price, if any, was retained until establishment or warranty expiry?”
32. “What nursery warranty applied to cultivar, certification, plant health and mortality? Who held and exercised that warranty?”
33. “What contractor warranty applied to planting depth, staking, irrigation and establishment? Please provide the defects clause and one closed claim.”
34. “Who replaced dead trees in year one and year two, and who paid when cause was disputed?”
35. “Who owns the 15-year aftercare obligation operationally: member alone, cooperative service, or contractor maintenance package?”
36. “What evidence do you keep for watering, pruning, fertilisation, pest control, mortality and replacements after final payment?”
37. “If the member neglects aftercare and aid is revoked, can the cooperative recover any amount or terminate service?”

### Withdrawal and failure

38. “Give one real example of a member who withdrew, lost eligibility, changed land title, denied site access, became insolvent, or failed to complete. What happened to the other members?”
39. “Did you substitute another member after submission? Which authority approved it, and did you recalculate the collective score?”
40. “Which costs and obligations survive member withdrawal under your mandate?”
41. “Can you exclude a member from the project without excluding them from the cooperative? What notice, cure period and appeal apply?”
42. “If one member’s failure causes a collective criterion to fail, who bears resulting grant loss?”
43. “If the cooperative itself cannot perform, can members take over supplier contracts, technical files, warranties and applications?”
44. “Please provide an anonymised withdrawal, default, cure, substitution or revocation file.”

### Performance and governance evidence

45. “For the entire cohort, please provide counts for selected, submitted, conceded, advanced, started, completed, paid, withdrawn, revoked, defective and under-aftercare members.”
46. “Please provide planned versus actual hectares, trees, cost, grant, completion date and one- and two-year survival by member.”
47. “What complaints did members raise, and who adjudicated them? Please provide the complaint register and resolution times.”
48. “What did the board learn and formally change after the first cohort? Please provide the later resolution or revised mandate.”

## Practical conclusions for workflow redesign

1. **Do not collapse collective and member status.** A collective can remain alive while one member is rejected, changed or revoked.
2. **Record both public and private authority.** Public eligibility does not prove the OP accepted the mandate; an adhesion is not the same as a service commitment.
3. **Freeze cohort metrics at each gate.** Show how every add/remove event changes aggregate score, funding and admissibility.
4. **Make contracting architecture explicit.** Never infer principal, payer or warranty holder from “capofila” or “mandatario.”
5. **Keep money member-granular.** Track aid, guarantee, pre-finance, invoices, OP advances, fees, reimbursement, overrun and recovery separately.
6. **Require conflict evidence before award.** Supplier independence, director abstention and higher-quote justification are first-class records.
7. **Separate public completion from private acceptance.** A grant balance can be payable while defects and aftercare remain open.
8. **Make exit computable.** On any member failure, calculate sunk shared cost, reassigned activity, cohort-score impact, aid at risk, replacement eligibility and surviving obligations.
9. **Treat aftercare as an owned workstream.** The 15-year obligation requires a named owner, schedule, evidence and escalation even though the public call does not specify a cooperative service.
10. **Label unsupported terms as OPEN, not assumed.** The private mandate is required evidence for supplier, payment, overrun, defects, aftercare and withdrawal answers.
