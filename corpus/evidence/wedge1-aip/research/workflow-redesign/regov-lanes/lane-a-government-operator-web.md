# Regione Puglia as operator of the *Xylella fastidiosa* emergency

**Scope and cut-off.** This is a decision-grain operating inventory, not a general history of the epidemic. It treats the Sezione Osservatorio Fitosanitario as Puglia's Regional Plant Health Service (RPS), ARIF as its delegated/support executor, and the municipalities, Prefects, Carabinieri Forestali, laboratories, payment bodies and EU institutions as connected actors. Sources were checked on the live web through 25 August 2026. “Annual demarcation update” is a misleading shorthand: the legal clock is **without delay after official confirmation**, while the Region also periodically consolidates zone geometry. The current EU rule is Commission Implementing Regulation (EU) 2020/1201, as amended; the consolidated text used here is [EUR-Lex](https://eur-lex.europa.eu/legal-content/EN/TXT/HTML/?uri=CELEX:02020R1201-20241017). The national allocation of powers is D.lgs. 19/2021, especially [Article 6](https://www.normattiva.it/uri-res/N2Ls?urn:nir:stato:decreto.legislativo:2021-02-02;19~art6), [Article 33](https://www.normattiva.it/uri-res/N2Ls?urn:nir:stato:decreto.legislativo:2021-02-02;19~art33), [Article 34](https://www.normattiva.it/uri-res/N2Ls?urn:nir:stato:decreto.legislativo:2021-02-02;19~art34) and [Article 55](https://www.normattiva.it/uri-res/N2Ls?urn:nir:stato:decreto.legislativo:2021-02-02;19~art55).

## 1. Decision inventory

### 1.1 Who owns the operating system

D.lgs. 19/2021 makes the regional plant-health services the territorial authorities that apply EU and national plant-health law. Their enumerated duties include official surveillance, responsibility for official analysis, demarcation (after National Phytosanitary Committee opinion), action plans, findings of violations, official prescriptions including destruction, annual information collection, audits of delegated bodies, RUOP/authorisations and notification of newly found pests to the central service ([Article 6](https://www.normattiva.it/uri-res/N2Ls?urn:nir:stato:decreto.legislativo:2021-02-02;19~art6)). In Puglia that authority is the Osservatorio Fitosanitario.

ARIF is not the policy owner. Puglia's DGR 570/2023 calls it an **organismo delegato** and assigns support functions for monitoring, prevention, removal, communication and financial-contribution instruction; the same act says the Osservatorio remains the delegating authority and must structure collaboration and oversight ([DGR 570/2023](https://burp.regione.puglia.it/documents/20135/2171631/DEL_570_2023.pdf/4e74cce4-d505-5e48-b255-e133d3f93d61?t=1687174345077&version=1.0)). ARIF itself says its monitoring follows the Osservatorio's plan and that the plan defines zones, actions, vector work, removal procedures, indemnities, roles and schedule ([ARIF monitoring page](https://www.arifpuglia.it/attivita/monitoraggio-xylella/attivita-di-monitoraggio/)).

## 2. EU-facing obligations and audit findings

### 2.1 The regional evidence that must reach Brussels

1. **Outbreak notification.** Official confirmation, host, subspecies, location, scale and measures must pass from the Osservatorio to the national service and EU notification system. The Commission says a positive finding is reported within eight working days ([Commission developments](https://food.ec.europa.eu/plants/plant-health-and-biosecurity/plant-health-rules/control-measures/xylella-fastidiosa/latest-developments-xylella-fastidiosa-eu-territory_en)).
2. **Demarcation notification.** New/updated/lifted areas and any use of Art. 5–6 derogations require the geometry, act, date and scientific justification. The Commission publishes the Union list from Member-State notifications ([EU Art. 4](https://eur-lex.europa.eu/legal-content/EN/TXT/HTML/?uri=CELEX:02020R1201-20241017); [current Union list](https://food.ec.europa.eu/document/download/77ec3d12-c080-4273-b604-5cb5164a4c78_en?filename=ph_biosec_legis_list-demarcated-union-territory_en.pdf)).
3. **Annual survey/results return.** By 30 April, Italy must report prior-year measures and results under Arts. 2, 4, 5, 7–18 and 32; demarcated-area plant and vector surveys use Annex V templates ([EU Art. 35/Annex V](https://eur-lex.europa.eu/legal-content/EN/TXT/HTML/?uri=CELEX:02020R1201-20241017)). The Osservatorio therefore needs data at the grain of zone-version × survey stratum × site type × host × method × result, not a regional total.
4. **Traceback/forward communication.** For eradication outbreaks, origin and associated moved host plants must be investigated and results communicated to Commission and affected Member States (EU Art. 11). Nursery lot/passport/movement data are therefore part of emergency evidence, not merely commercial regulation ([EU regulation](https://eur-lex.europa.eu/legal-content/EN/TXT/HTML/?uri=CELEX:02020R1201-20241017)).
5. **Auditability of delegation.** D.lgs. 19/2021 makes the RPS responsible for auditing delegated official-control/official-activity bodies ([Art. 6(q)](https://www.normattiva.it/uri-res/N2Ls?urn:nir:stato:decreto.legislativo:2021-02-02;19~art6)). Puglia must therefore evidence not only what ARIF did, but the authority, instructions, supervision, exceptions, corrective action and performance of the delegation.

### 2.2 DG SANTE audit lineage and findings

The Commission's live audit database returns Xylella-specific Italy audits in 2014 (two), 2015 (two), 2016, 2018, 2019, 2021 and 2022. The latest dedicated report located is **DG(SANTE) 2022-7401**, audit June 2022, published 3 March 2023 ([record](https://ec.europa.eu/food/audits-analysis/audit-report/details/4583); [report](https://ec.europa.eu/food/audits-analysis/audit-report/download/16010)). Earlier relevant reports are [2021-7280](https://ec.europa.eu/food/audits-analysis/audit-report/download/15549), [2019-6731](https://ec.europa.eu/food/audits-analysis/audit-report/download/14845) and [2018-6485](https://ec.europa.eu/food/audits-analysis/audit-report/download/14049).

**What the audits say, in operating terms:**

- **2018: removal system failure.** In the 2017 campaign's 20-km containment strip, only **10.7% of more than 3,000 positive olive trees** had been removed by the audit. The Commission attributed delay to protected-area authorisations, court appeals, difficulty identifying owners and shortages of administrative capacity; the buffer/barrier function was compromised ([2018 report](https://ec.europa.eu/food/audits-analysis/audit-report/download/14049)).
- **2019: backlog persisted despite a new 33-day workflow.** Of 366 infected trees confirmed in the infected zone and 165 in the 20-km strip, only 160 and 52 respectively had been felled by audit time. The new procedure allowed up to 13 days for public notice, 10 for owner removal and another 10 for ARIF. A prosecutor's seizure of CNR-Bari laboratory material/equipment from 3 May to 5 June 2019 stopped molecular confirmation and added to the positive/ambiguous ELISA backlog ([2019 report](https://ec.europa.eu/food/audits-analysis/audit-report/download/14845)).
- **2021 audit: improved but still considerable delay and weak compliance.** The audit said Apulian removal delays were significantly reduced but still created pronounced spread risk, especially in vector flight season. It also found a high number of non-compliances with mandatory vector controls. At national level, lack of coordination and proficiency testing among authorised regional laboratories created a false-negative risk, and the then-current national contingency plan was obsolete/not legally binding ([2021 report](https://ec.europa.eu/food/audits-analysis/audit-report/download/15549)).
- **2022 audit: major acceleration, with remaining survey/data defects.** Organisational changes yielded removal on average **19 days after final lab result and 31 days after sampling**; area totals averaged 26–34 days, with observed ranges up to 70 days. Felling orders were prepared within five days of confirmed result; municipal publication, owner election/removal and ARIF substitution were explicitly managed. DG SANTE concluded most confirmation-to-removal intervals were within the technically/legal minimum ([2022 report](https://ec.europa.eu/food/audits-analysis/audit-report/download/16010)).
- **2022 compliance result.** Carabinieri Forestali completed 6,664 checks in demarcated areas with **92.5%** correct implementation and 1,865 checks outside them with **91.7%** compliance; both control volume and compliance had nearly doubled from earlier figures. But surveys omitted some risk factors (symptomatic plants and previously sampled-plant information), pooling added 5–10 days to positive confirmation, and monumental olives in Polignano a Mare were not sampled in 2021 as required ([2022 report](https://ec.europa.eu/food/audits-analysis/audit-report/download/16010)).
- **Systemic EU judgment.** In Case C-443/18 the Court of Justice held that Italy failed to ensure immediate removal of infected plants in the containment strip and failed to ensure annual monitoring at appropriate times; it rejected the Commission's broader allegation of a general/persistent failure because causation and each alleged infringement were not proven ([judgment](https://eur-lex.europa.eu/legal-content/EN/TXT/?uri=CELEX:62018CJ0443)). This is the precise finding—not a holding that every aspect of Italy's response was unlawful.

## 3. Documented operational pain, with numbers

### 3.1 Positive-to-felling backlog and the clock problem

The historical failure was not “lack of a map”; it was loss of elapsed time at each handoff: sampling → screening → confirmatory lab → owner/parcel resolution → DET → public/PEC notice → owner election → crew/access/vector treatment → felling/destruction. The numbers above show the progression from 10.7% removal of >3,000 positives in 2018, to 160/366 and 52/165 removed by the 2019 audit, to a 2021 average of 31 days sampling-to-felling under the redesigned process ([2018](https://ec.europa.eu/food/audits-analysis/audit-report/download/14049); [2019](https://ec.europa.eu/food/audits-analysis/audit-report/download/14845); [2022](https://ec.europa.eu/food/audits-analysis/audit-report/download/16010)). A common operating picture must retain each timestamp and blocking reason; “open/closed” destroys the diagnostic value.

### 3.2 Appeals and legal constraints

Growers' challenges led the Lazio TAR to suspend eradication measures and refer the validity question to the CJEU; a separate criminal seizure blocked removals until July 2016. The CJEU then upheld the validity of removing host plants around infected plants under the precautionary/proportionality principles ([C-78/16 and C-79/16](https://eur-lex.europa.eu/legal-content/EN/TXT/?uri=CELEX:62016CJ0078)). DG SANTE's 2018 audit still found court appeals, owner identification and protected-land procedures delaying removals ([2018 report](https://ec.europa.eu/food/audits-analysis/audit-report/download/14049)). D.lgs. 19/2021 later gave explicit publication/access/public-force machinery ([Art. 33](https://www.normattiva.it/uri-res/N2Ls?urn:nir:stato:decreto.legislativo:2021-02-02;19~art33)).

Recent litigation remains operationally relevant. A July 2025 TAR interim order reportedly suspended removal of a vineyard within a 50-m eradication radius pending merits; only press accounts were located, so the case number, ultimate outcome and elapsed delay are **UNVERIFIED** ([Telenorba report](https://telenorba.it/2025/07/25/xylella-il-tar-blocca-leradicazione-di-un-vigneto/)). The live system therefore needs a parcel/order-level “stay/appeal” state linked to the court order, not a narrative note.

### 3.3 Laboratory and inspector/crew capacity

- The 2019 CNR seizure created a complete confirmatory-testing interruption for about a month ([2019 audit](https://ec.europa.eu/food/audits-analysis/audit-report/download/14845)).
- In 2021/22, sample dispatch was deliberately paced to available daily laboratory capacity; pooling increased throughput but added **5–10 days** on average to positive confirmation ([2022 audit](https://ec.europa.eu/food/audits-analysis/audit-report/download/16010)).
- The 2022 audit warned that Italy's national contingency plan did not ensure regions would make the minimum resources available for future outbreaks ([2022 audit](https://ec.europa.eu/food/audits-analysis/audit-report/download/16010)).
- **Current numeric headcount, crew capacity, per-lab daily assay capacity and inspection vacancy data were not found in the public sources reviewed: UNVERIFIED.** These are essential capacity inputs and should be requested rather than inferred from sample totals.

### 3.4 Compliance and enforcement coverage

The strongest official compliance measure found is the 2021 CUFAA result: 92.5% in demarcated areas and 91.7% outside, based on 8,529 checks. It shows substantial improvement, but not universal compliance, and says nothing by itself about representativeness because sites were risk-selected ([2022 audit](https://ec.europa.eu/food/audits-analysis/audit-report/download/16010)). The public record reviewed does not expose a current linked series of obligations issued, inspections, breaches, notices cured, sanctions final, coercive removals and amounts recovered: **GAP**.

### 3.5 Budget cliff versus actual capacity cut

DGR 570/2023 showed autonomous-budget Xylella allocations of **€3.62m (2023), €4.02m (2024), €2.92m (2025)**, €10.56m total ([DGR 570/2023](https://burp.regione.puglia.it/documents/20135/2171631/DEL_570_2023.pdf/4e74cce4-d505-5e48-b255-e133d3f93d61?t=1687174345077&version=1.0)). DGR 1593/2024 later presented plan resources of approximately **€10.624m (2024), €2.77m (2025), €1.95m (2026)**—a nominal drop of 73.9% and 81.6% from the exceptional 2024 figure—and added/reprogrammed €1.513m in bound surplus ([DGR 1593/2024](https://burp.regione.puglia.it/documents/20135/2560952/DEL_1593_2024.pdf/4501bf7f-c5ec-face-57d4-a559581af1c2?t=1733751415831&version=1.0)). The Region simultaneously described the three-year plan as “over €15m” and a concentration of human/economic resources in a narrower surveillance strip ([regional release](https://press.regione.puglia.it/-/la-giunta-regionale-approva-l-aggiornamento-del-piano-d-azione-di-contrasto-alla-xylella-f.-2024-2026)).

**Interpretation:** the published profile is an allocation cliff, but it is **not proven to be a like-for-like cut to field monitoring**, because 2024 includes exceptional/reprogrammed resources and chapters combine several activities. Treat “monitoring budget cut” as **PARTLY VERIFIED** until line-item commitments, carry-forwards, ARIF contracts and lab procurements are reconciled by year.

### 3.6 Grant/payment backlogs and control friction

ARIF reported in July 2022 that 965 Article 6 applicants had completed positive technical-administrative instruction for **€21.95m**, while it was also processing 4,646 2018 and 3,848 2019 damage-indemnity applications—nearly 8,500 legacy cases ([ARIF release](https://www.arifpuglia.it/comunicati-stampa/importante-boccata-dossigeno-per-circa-1-000-aziende-agricole/)). The Region later acknowledged that DURC and antimafia certificates objectively slow grants and that it sought massive rather than one-by-one INPS DURC requests ([regional status release](https://press.regione.puglia.it/-/reimpianti-ulivi-in-zona-infetta-da-xylella-lo-stato-di-attuazione-dell-avviso-pubblico)). Successive “16th advance,” scorrimento, concession and liquidation acts into 2025–26 show a long-lived queue, not a single award event ([Measure 2.B](https://www.regione.puglia.it/web/rigenerazione-olivicola/misura-2.b)).

### 3.7 Fraud/irregularity allegations: do not promote them to fact

Press reports in late 2025 attributed to an internal AGEA cross-check an alleged €18.8m paid among roughly 2,500 positions with no detected plants and many closed/deceased files. No public AGEA report, GdF case release, charge, audit decision or judgment supporting those exact figures was located. The numbers are therefore **UNVERIFIED allegations**, not findings. The operational implication is still valid: benefit, parcel, crop, enterprise/death status, duplicated aid and field evidence require cross-system controls. In June 2026 press reported a Region–GdF extension of preventive controls to Xylella measures, monitoring and aid, but an official signed protocol text was not located in this search ([BariToday](https://www.baritoday.it/economia/controlli-fondi-concessi-danni-xyellla-accordo-regione-puglia-guardia-finanza.html)).

### 3.8 Corte dei conti: bounded negative

A dedicated Corte dei conti performance audit of Puglia's Xylella operating chain was **not located** in the live Corte catalog/searches performed. The Court's Puglia parification materials contain broad recommendations on slow EU-fund payment and regional monitoring, but those are not Xylella-specific and should not be cited as if they audited this emergency ([2024 parification document](https://www.corteconti.it/Download?id=5d0e84f9-78c0-4583-abab-714d15e614a4)). This is a **NOT FOUND**, not proof that no such audit exists.

## 4. What the common operating picture must answer every day

The authority does not need one dashboard. It needs a linked clock-and-evidence picture that answers these live questions:

### Outbreak and legal geography

1. Which samples became officially positive today, which still need second-test/subspecies confirmation, and how old is each lab queue item?
2. For each positive, what exact **zone-version act** and Annex III status applied at the time? Is the parcel under eradication, containment, buffer or free-area rules?
3. Has the 50-m infected zone and the correct buffer been computed, reviewed and published? Which cadastral parcels intersect even partially?
4. Is there a newer positive that requires immediate enlargement, merger or replacement of a demarcated area? Has Rome/Brussels been notified?
5. Which areas are candidates for reduction/lifting, and do they actually meet every negative-survey/vector/confidence/time condition?

### Survey and laboratory control

6. Against the approved statistical design, how many epidemiological units, plants, nurseries, public sites and vector sites are planned, visited, sampled, tested and unresolved by zone/risk/host/method?
7. What confidence/design prevalence has actually been achieved—not merely planned—and which risk strata are under-covered?
8. Are symptomatic plants, previously sampled plants, nurseries, transport corridors, monumental groves, abandoned orchards and the first 400 m represented correctly?
9. What is each laboratory's daily capacity, backlog, indeterminate/retest rate, turnaround distribution and proficiency status? Is sampling being throttled to avoid a felling-delaying lab surge?
10. Can every result be traced to collector, plant coordinates, chain of custody, method, controls, report and any confirmatory/subspecies result?

### Removal clock and enforcement

11. For every positive and every required surrounding plant, what is the elapsed time from sample → confirmed result → DET signed → Comune/PEC notice → owner election → access → pre-treatment → removal/destruction?
12. Who owns/holds each affected parcel; what evidence supports that identity; did the Comune complete seven consecutive days of publication; is PEC proof present?
13. Did the owner elect voluntary removal, ARIF, or remain silent? Which 10-day clock is running? Which ARIF crew and inspector are assigned?
14. Which jobs are blocked by protected/monumental status, inaccessible terrain, wrong owner, third-party obstruction, weather, contractor/crew shortage, court stay or missing Prefect support?
15. Was vector treatment applied before felling where required? Were roots removed/devitalised and branches/leaves destroyed correctly? Is completion evidence sufficient for EU audit and indemnity?
16. Which breaches are cured, contestable, sanctioned, coercively executed, referred to police/prosecutor or awaiting cost recovery?

### Vector control and public land

17. What vector stage and density are observed by altitude/climate area, and what treatment window should be opened, extended or closed?
18. Which private farms, abandoned parcels, municipal greens, road/rail margins, canals and demesne are obligated, exempt, inspected, compliant or overdue?
19. Are Comune/Province/infrastructure-manager work plans funded and complete? Does aerial evidence agree with field inspection?
20. Is low compliance clustered by actor, land class or municipality, and is enforcement aimed where spread risk is greatest?

### Nurseries and movement

21. Which RUOP operators and production sites are authorised, suspended or due for inspection? Which structural/vector-control deficiencies are open?
22. Which lots of specified plants are awaiting pre-movement testing/passport/movement communication, and does the route cross infected/buffer boundaries lawfully?
23. For a nursery positive, what lots, suppliers, customers and Member States are in traceback/forward; which plants remain untraced?
24. Are planting permission, plant movement permission and grant eligibility being kept as three separate decisions?

### Money, capacity and accountability

25. For each felling indemnity and Article 6 grant, what are eligibility, instruction, DURC/antimafia, concession, advance, works, inspection, final payment, recovery and appeal states—and who owns the next action?
26. Do parcel/crop/beneficiary/death/enterprise/payment records agree across regional systems, ARIF and the relevant paying body? Are duplicate or incompatible aids blocked?
27. What money is appropriation, commitment, contract, certified work, liability, payment or recovery? How much field/lab/crew capacity does each euro buy this month?
28. Are ARIF/lab/Carabinieri/Comune delegations meeting service levels, and has the Osservatorio performed the legally required audit/corrective action?
29. Is the 30-April EU return complete at Annex V grain; do outbreak/demarcation notifications reconcile to regional acts, lab reports and current GIS?
30. What would an EU auditor be unable to prove from today's evidence package?

## Bottom line

The Osservatorio's decisive product is a **legally versioned, evidence-complete, clocked work order**: an official result becomes the correct zone/regime, the correct plants and owners, valid notice, a voluntary or ARIF job, controlled destruction, enforcement/payment state and an EU-reportable evidence package. ARIF is the execution engine, not the legal decision maker. The historical operational failure was elapsed time and lost handoffs; the modern risk is fragmentation across lab, GIS/cadastre, municipal notice, crew/access, vector compliance, nursery movement, grants and EU reporting. A common operating picture must preserve those separate decisions and clocks while linking them to the same plant, parcel, zone-version, actor and source act.
