## Bottom line

The consultant preserves the accepted architecture and identifies several genuine precision defects. The recommendations are mostly surgical and should be incorporated.

The report does not receive authority to close Gate 2. Owen’s remaining one-by-one boundary review still controls Gate 2 acceptance. No new broad research, sixth function, noun, Ontology, platform or interface work is justified.

## Verdict labels

- **ADOPT** — corrects a demonstrated workflow defect without changing the accepted architecture.
- **PARTLY ADOPT** — the underlying concern is valid, but the proposed framing, terminology or scope must be narrowed.
- **DEFER** — belongs to a later gate or requires a separate decision.
- **REJECT** — conflicts with current authority or verified workflow evidence.

## Effect on accepted and standing decisions

The recommendations do not reopen:

- the accepted six recurring questions;
- the five responsibility lanes;
- the recursive event grammar;
- the separation of public eligibility, adhesion, cooperative mandate acceptance and concession;
- the Land/Field/authority compliance split;
- proposition-specific source authority and targeted holds; or
- municipalities as contextual actors rather than a separate lane.

They refine how those accepted decisions operate.

The paused Funding-population decision remains necessary. Cross-line capacity allocation is separate from population evaluation:

- the complete event-driven population determines which lines are publicly viable, unresolved or not assessable;
- the cooperative separately allocates scarce shared capacity among otherwise viable lines; and
- capacity allocation never rewrites public eligibility, concession, compulsory duty or individual fallback.

## Gate effect

Gate 2 remains in executive review. Gate 3 noun identification and every later gate remain closed.
