# Operator reality: a large Puglia olive OP under Xylella

Status: independent reconstruction, not direct observation  
Date: 24 August 2026  
Scope: the operating world around a large recognized olive producer organization (OP), from member-land diagnosis through compulsory response, recovery investment, execution, payment and early establishment  
Evidence rule: Class 3 hypotheses come first, Class 2 inferences second, and fetched Class 1 facts and verbatim excerpts last. No source list establishes completeness.

## Executive verdict

CORDON has the right administrative separations, but it is still more like a carefully governed public-programme control system than the desk of an OP operations manager. The real unit of work is likely not a `Parcel`, `Proceeding`, `Intervention`, or abstract decision scene by itself. It is a rolling **member commitment plus executable field package**, assembled into a geographic and seasonal batch, with named people, a bill of quantities, supply lots, access, water, crews, invoices, guarantees, defects, and a next call to make. Public eligibility is only one constraint on that package.

The strongest redesign implication is to put three practical controls ahead of ontology completeness and portfolio optimization:

1. **cohort conversion:** which interested members have become legally and commercially executable packages;
2. **resource-backed schedule:** which packages can actually run in the next 2–12 weeks with plants, water, machinery, crews, technician and cash; and
3. **exception-to-call:** the exact person the manager or case handler must call today, what document or decision is missing, and what batch is blocked meanwhile.

CORDON should retain its hard distinctions between member choice, OP choice, public decision, physical performance, acceptance, payment order, cash and survival. It should challenge its assumptions that a complete legal/opportunity population, plant-level identity, a formal capacity optimizer, a persistent five-line rail, and a dense hidden occurrence graph are the first things an operator needs.

# Part I — Class 3: hypotheses from model memory, not verified by fetched evidence

Everything in this part is **[Class 3 — unverified]**. These are targets for direct observation, not product facts.

## 1. What the operations manager probably manages in practice

A large OP is unlikely to have one clean Xylella team or one integrated case system. The manager probably coordinates a mixed operation:

- member relations and cooperative governance;
- CAA and farm-file repair;
- agronomic design and technical sign-off;
- grant application and PEC traffic;
- nursery and contractor sourcing;
- machinery and crew scheduling;
- invoice, guarantee and payment-claim assembly;
- mill, quality and commercial planning; and
- disputes, deaths, ownership gaps, access failures and substitutions.

The manager’s recurring questions are probably more concrete than CORDON’s six-question grammar:

1. Which members are real, responsive and still committed?
2. Which land can we enter, clear, plant and irrigate without discovering a new veto?
3. How many hectares can we execute this planting window, with which crew and which plants?
4. Which beneficiary will fail next because a PEC, title, DURC, antimafia check, guarantee, invoice or signature is stale?
5. Which supplier promise is firm, and what is the substitute if it fails?
6. Which package can be closed for payment this month without exposing the OP to rework or recovery?
7. Which young orchard is dying, who owes replacement, and is water, planting quality, browsing, drought or disease the cause?
8. What does the board need to decide, and what can staff settle without escalation?

The manager likely shifts constantly between population and exception grain. Morning work may begin with a spreadsheet of 300 members, then collapse into ten phone calls about four parcels, then return to a contractor’s weekly route and a bank guarantee expiry list.

## 2. The likely human network

### OP internal roles

The “operations manager” may not be one person. Decision rights may be split among:

- president or legal representative;
- board or executive committee;
- general director;
- programme/project manager;
- member-relations staff;
- agronomists and field technicians;
- procurement/contract administration;
- accounting and treasury;
- quality, traceability and mill liaison;
- privacy/administration staff; and
- external consultants who carry more operational memory than internal staff.

A system built for one omnipotent manager may fail because no one person can accept mandates, commit spend, direct crews, approve invoices and communicate public claims.

### Member-side actors

The apparent member may not be the person who can decide or sign. Common practical variants probably include:

- elderly owner with an adult child handling WhatsApp and PEC;
- tenant farmer with operational control but no authority to uproot;
- several co-owners who disagree or cannot be found;
- deceased applicant with unresolved succession;
- company with a changed legal representative;
- member whose CAA, accountant and technician each hold different pieces of the truth;
- non-farming owner who wants clearing but not productive replant;
- member who joins to preserve an option but delays when a guarantee or co-financing becomes real; and
- member who changes OP, CAA, lease, IBAN or legal form mid-route.

## 3. The likely operating calendar

### Annual and seasonal constraints

The public deadline is rarely the only deadline. The operating calendar likely includes:

- compulsory vector-control windows;
- nursery propagation and order lead times;
- soil moisture and access conditions for heavy machinery;
- felling and stump/root removal windows;
- planting windows and heat avoidance;
- irrigation-system commissioning before planting;
- water-contract activation and seasonal delivery schedules;
- contractor availability around harvest, pruning and other crop work;
- technician workload around CAP and CSR filing peaks;
- bank guarantee issuance and renewal time;
- PEC response windows and public integration requests;
- invoice/payment timing and VAT cash effects;
- labour availability and legal onboarding;
- mill commissioning, harvest queues and storage capacity;
- aftercare inspections timed to reveal survival; and
- replacement planting before warranty or programme clocks expire.

A parcel can be administratively ready and operationally impossible because the nursery cannot supply the selected lot, a pump has no connection, a contractor is in harvest work, or the land is too wet for an excavator.

### Daily control rhythm

The real “Now” view may be assembled from:

- Excel or Google Sheets trackers;
- Outlook folders, PEC inboxes and portal receipts;
- WhatsApp groups and voice notes;
- shared-drive member folders;
- printed cadastral maps and marked-up PDFs;
- technician notebooks and phone photos;
- SIAN, regional EIP/PMA, RUOP/Xylella portals and municipal pages;
- accounting software and bank portals;
- contractor calendars; and
- one or more personal notebooks that contain the actual promises.

The most valuable information may be outside formal systems: “Mario’s son will sign Friday,” “the excavator can do that comune only next week,” “the nursery has 4,000 FS17 but will not hold without deposit,” “the water hydrant is broken,” or “do not invoice until the variant is accepted.” These are not authoritative facts, but they determine whether work moves.

## 4. The likely work package

A field package probably needs more than an `Intervention` scope and generic readiness. At minimum it may bind:

- member/beneficiary and signatory;
- authority to enter, uproot, contract and claim;
- parcels and actual accessible work polygons;
- tree/stump estimate or measured quantity;
- treatment, felling, removal and disposal method;
- monumental/protected-tree exceptions;
- soil preparation and drainage;
- orchard design, cultivar mix, spacing and pollination plan;
- plant quantity, size/specification, nursery, lot and passport;
- irrigation source, entitlement, delivery point, design and commissioning;
- fencing/tree guards and wildlife risk;
- contractors, equipment, crew and named supervisor;
- health-and-safety plan and access instructions;
- schedule dependencies and weather contingency;
- accepted unit prices, taxes and payment milestones;
- public eligible cost versus real delivered cost;
- member contribution, advance, bridge finance and guarantee;
- evidence checklist by phase;
- inspection/acceptance and defect route;
- aftercare owner, watering plan and replacement terms; and
- downstream harvest/mill compatibility assumptions.

The package is versioned. A changed parcel, cultivar, plant count, supplier, layout or price may be a harmless field adaptation, a contractual change, a grant variant or an impermissible change. The operator needs that classification before committing spend.

## 5. Likely recurring failure modes

### Member and land

- CUAA/tax identity mismatch across membership, farm file and application.
- Parcel operated in reality but absent or stale in the fascicolo.
- Crop/tree count copied from an old declaration and not field-real.
- Title expires before the maintenance commitment ends.
- Co-owner, lessor or heir consent arrives late.
- Access is through another parcel and was never documented.
- Boundary, road, wall, utility, canal or neighbour conflict reduces executable area.
- Member is responsive until a guarantee, private contribution or long maintenance duty is explained.
- Informal family representative has no signing authority.

### Application and authority

- CAA fixes one source but another portal still holds the old snapshot.
- PEC reaches an unattended mailbox.
- OTP or digital signature fails at release.
- Integration request is answered with the right document but wrong scope, format or signer.
- A programme-office interpretation changes through FAQ or case handling rather than a clean amendment.
- Collective application succeeds while individual adherents split into admitted, held, dead, withdrawn or individually routed populations.
- Staff infer a favourable result from a ranking, concession proposal or payment order before the formal act exists.

### Supply and field execution

- Plant reservation is treated as capacity without a deposit, delivery week or accepted substitution.
- Passport/lot evidence is detached from the exact parcel delivery.
- Delivered plant count, size or cultivar differs from the order.
- Irrigation is designed after planting rather than before dispatch.
- No water delivery, low pressure, broken hydrant, illegal/expired well or missing power kills establishment.
- Stumps, rock, slope or narrow access change the machinery and price.
- Felling contractor, earthworks contractor, irrigation installer and planter each assume another actor owns cleanup or evidence.
- Photos prove presence but not quantity, location, date or accepted quality.
- Contractor invoice categories do not match the programme cost schedule.
- A late public variant turns already-purchased work into contested spend.
- Warranty is vague, so nursery, installer, member and aftercare contractor dispute mortality.

### Money

- The beneficiary, payer, invoice addressee, creditor and bank-account holder are not the same party.
- Individual guarantees make a nominal collective route financially fragmented.
- The OP fronts supplier deposits but has no clean recourse against a member who withdraws.
- A public advance arrives after the cash need it was meant to solve.
- Bank guarantee expiry precedes public release.
- Liquidation is mistaken for settlement.
- Partial admitted amounts are not allocated back to actual supplier/member liabilities.
- VAT or ineligible overrun creates a private gap not visible in the grant amount.
- Recovery/audit risk survives long after field completion.

### Establishment and value recovery

- “planted” becomes the success metric, masking mortality and stalled aftercare.
- Survival sample design is too weak to attribute a problem.
- Watering, weed control, staking, pruning and replacement duties are not assigned in one contract.
- The selected orchard system is agronomically viable but mismatched to member machinery, labour, mill intake or market route.
- The OP restores hectares that members will not maintain or harvest economically.

# Part II — Class 2: inferences from fetched evidence

Everything in this part is **[Class 2 — inference]**. Each inference is derived from one or more Class 1 facts listed later, but it is not directly stated by those sources.

## 6. Reconstructed real operating model

### 6.1 The OP is an execution aggregator without becoming the beneficiary

The Article 6 material separates individual beneficiary rights from collective execution. An OP or oil cooperative receives adhesion records, selects which adherents to include, holds a mandate to perform uprooting and replanting, and organizes fragmented land. The individual remains the beneficiary and may need an individual guarantee. A non-selected adhesion can continue as an individual route.

**Operational consequence:** the OP runs two coupled ledgers:

1. a **collective production ledger** for cohorts, procurement, crews, delivery and evidence; and
2. a **beneficiary liability ledger** for mandate, guarantee, concession, claim, admitted amount, recovery and cash.

A software design that models only one collective Proceeding or one member route will lose the many-to-one/many-back-to-one reconciliation. The OP needs to see whether one defective parcel blocks only one beneficiary, one supplier invoice, one batch, or the entire collective claim.

### 6.2 The cohort is a first-class operating object even if it is not a legal object

The regional material explicitly describes OPs as responsible for organizing work across a fragmented production system. Contractors, plants, irrigation installers and inspections are economically scheduled in batches. A cohort can be geographic, cultivar-specific, supplier-specific, deadline-specific or evidence-specific.

**Operational consequence:** CORDON’s `Cooperative Pursuit` and `Intervention Capacity Commitment` do not fully capture the temporary execution batch. The manager needs a selectable batch with:

- included executable packages;
- excluded/held packages and reasons;
- total quantities;
- supplier/crew commitments;
- route and delivery sequence;
- evidence closure; and
- impact of removing one member.

This can begin as an application projection, not necessarily an Ontology type. But it must behave as a durable planning unit if orders, deposits, dispatches or claims refer to it.

### 6.3 “Ready” is dominated by source repair and counterpart commitment, not rule evaluation alone

The formal route requires a validated farm file, exact olive-tree counts per parcel, PEC, signature/OTP, tenure/owner authorization and member/OP mandate. The real bottleneck is likely getting these facts current in the systems that own them and getting the relevant human to sign. Rule logic can identify a defect but cannot cure a stale fascicolo, absent co-owner or unread PEC.

**Operational consequence:** every blocker needs a **cure transaction**:

- who contacts whom;
- channel;
- requested artifact or decision;
- due date;
- last response;
- whether work can continue in parallel; and
- batch consequence.

CORDON rejects Task/Queue/WorkItem semantics. That is principled but risky. A “Now” projection is insufficient if it cannot preserve the ordinary follow-up promises by which staff move a case. The design needs a minimal correspondence/follow-up substrate or an explicit integration with the OP’s CRM/email practice. Calling everything a next-owner derivation may erase operational accountability.

### 6.4 Mass public controls create population bottlenecks

Regione Puglia itself identifies contributory regularity and antimafia certification as objective sources of delay and requested mass DURC handling. It also reports needing assistance and software to speed instruction. These are not isolated member exceptions; they are programme-office throughput constraints.

**Operational consequence:** the manager needs control-level population views:

- awaiting DURC request/result;
- antimafia threshold and status;
- programme-office batch/desk;
- evidence submitted versus authority acknowledged;
- ageing since the last authority effect; and
- likely next scorrimento or concession population.

CORDON’s selective reopening is aligned, but a purely object-by-object decision scene underplays shared external queues. “Owner and clock” should include a population bottleneck and escalation route, not imply that the OP can cure it.

### 6.5 Nursery lot and passport are operationally load-bearing

Fetched regional material requires plants to be passported, traces to be kept by lot/recipient, and nursery movements to be recorded. It also conditions nursery authorization on inspections, testing and RUOP standing.

**Operational consequence:** Gate 5’s decision not to include Plant Trade Unit/Lot in the initial core should be treated as a high-risk provisional cut. A plant lot connects:

- order and reservation;
- supplier and RUOP/passport authority;
- cultivar/specification;
- test/inspection status;
- delivery and invoice;
- parcel/batch allocation;
- acceptance/rejection; and
- mortality/warranty investigation.

Individual Plant identity is usually less useful than **lot × delivery × planting block** identity. Individual identity should remain for monumental trees, ordered removals, sampled official plants, exceptions and replacement defects. For ordinary new orchards, plant-level objects create high cost and weak decision value.

### 6.6 Water is not a conditional permit footnote; it is a production capacity

OP-issued material already treats irrigation advice and water-use management as a core member service. Secondary reporting describes funded young plants at risk because irrigation contracts or infrastructure were unavailable. Even without accepting every reported detail, establishment plainly depends on a committed water source, delivery and irrigation system.

**Operational consequence:** before dispatch, the manager must distinguish:

- water right/contract;
- source type and legality;
- delivery point and pressure/flow;
- infrastructure condition;
- installation/commissioning;
- seasonal allocation or outage;
- emergency tanker fallback;
- who pays; and
- who waters after acceptance.

A generic “resource condition” or “water permit outcome” is too coarse. Water capacity should be a named readiness profile and cohort constraint. A map may show network proximity, but only the provider’s contract and operational confirmation establish availability.

### 6.7 The OP’s ordinary programme, technical service and market machinery already exist

Assoproli and APOL describe field assistance, transformation assistance, environmental work, irrigation DSS, phytosanitary monitoring, harvest timing, delivery to mills, traceability, certification, training and residue management. Aproli describes production programming, cost reduction, price stabilization and direct commercialization.

**Operational consequence:** CORDON should not behave as a new standalone operating system imposed on an empty organization. It should attach Xylella recovery to existing OP structures:

- technician territories and visit logs;
- member communications and AgroAPP/other apps;
- traceability IDs;
- cooperative and mill network;
- quality and certification programmes;
- programme-operation reporting; and
- existing commercial planning.

The current Wedge endpoint excludes productive maturity and market realization. That boundary is sensible for delivery, but orchard-system choice must consume downstream constraints before planting. The manager must know whether spacing, cultivar mix and projected harvest fit available machinery, cooperative mills, quality route and member economics.

### 6.8 Compulsory removal is an enforcement and access operation

ARIF’s procedure separates official finding and injunction, owner election, voluntary removal, ARIF removal, silence, refusal, forced removal, Prefecture/law-enforcement involvement, inspection and indemnity. Wood disposition, vector treatment and difficult-root exceptions have separate rules.

**Operational consequence:** “compulsory route” needs an operational fork beyond duty/compliance:

- notification effective but person unreachable;
- voluntary execution chosen;
- ARIF execution requested;
- silence default;
- access appointment;
- refusal/obstruction;
- force/police support;
- difficult location/root devitalization authorization;
- wood retained/moved;
- inspector witnessed; and
- indemnity package complete.

The OP may not control this route, but it must know when compulsory execution competes with, precedes or physically changes a voluntary recovery package.

### 6.9 Death, succession and role changes are ordinary state transitions

The regional programme created a specific pre-concession succession procedure requiring the deceased person’s CUAA and the successor’s PEC. ARIF indemnity guidance also anticipates several co-owners and owner/tenant delegation.

**Operational consequence:** Party identity alone is not enough. The system needs a robust **continuity-of-standing** pattern: deceased/ceased party, successor candidate, evidence, effective authority, impacted mandates, bank/IBAN change, CAA/fascicolo change, and which decisions survive. This is more material than a rich generic event history.

### 6.10 Finance is a route feasibility decision before it is a payment state

The collective beneficiary can request an advance but may need an individual guarantee. ISMEA guarantees are requested through a financing bank after merit review; Confidi can participate. Public processes separate concession, advance liquidation and final balance. VAT and cost overruns can remain private.

**Operational consequence:** the manager needs, before OP commitment:

- who fronts deposits and interim invoices;
- guaranteed amount, issuer, cost and expiry;
- member credit/guarantee refusal;
- approved public amount versus cash need;
- VAT and ineligible cost;
- payment terms with supplier/contractor;
- assignment/mandate of payment, if any;
- OP exposure if member withdraws; and
- fallback if public timing slips.

A portfolio that maximizes expected Xylella-loss reduction but ignores bridge cash, guarantee lead time and counterparty credit is not feasible.

### 6.11 Labour legality and safety are procurement facts

Official labour material treats regular contribution, safety and sub-contracting as compliance and inspection concerns. The Article 6 notice also requires workplace-safety compliance.

**Operational consequence:** contractor capacity is not “one crew available.” It includes:

- legal employer/subcontract chain;
- DURC and insurance;
- training and equipment qualification;
- safety documentation;
- seasonal labour source;
- travel/accommodation where relevant;
- incident and stop-work responsibility; and
- whether the OP may direct work without becoming an employer in fact.

CORDON should not become an HR system. It should gate dispatch on a small verified contractor package and surface subcontract changes.

### 6.12 Mills are not merely out-of-scope downstream actors

The OP’s recognized purpose and existing services span production programming, harvest, delivery, transformation, quality and traceability. Xylella also affected mill throughput and triggered separate compensation.

**Operational consequence:** the mill network influences recovery choices now. A large OP must decide whether restored production can be harvested and milled at the desired quality, whether cooperative mills will survive the production trough, and whether new orchard systems concentrate harvest into capacity peaks. CORDON does not need to manage milling, but it needs a **downstream compatibility check** at design approval.

## 8. What formal procedures do not carry

Formal systems establish authority, eligibility, filing, inspection and payment effects. They do not reliably carry:

- confidence that a member will sign;
- whether a family representative is actually reachable;
- supplier willingness to reserve without deposit;
- contractor’s real weekly capacity;
- access width and ground condition;
- water pressure today;
- likelihood that a programme officer will accept a proposed adaptation;
- quality of a crew’s prior work;
- whether the member will perform aftercare;
- whether a mill will still operate when production returns; or
- the OP’s reputational cost of selecting or deferring members.

CORDON should preserve these as **operational assertions with owner, date and confidence**, never as authoritative truth. Some expire quickly. A phone promise should not become “capacity committed,” but excluding it entirely forces staff back to private notebooks. The practical representation is a short dated commitment ladder: indicated → quoted → reserved → contracted → dispatched/received, with the counterparty and expiry visible. The legal names can remain route-specific.

## 9. Current CORDON comparison

### Strong alignments

- **No global case status.** One member-land package can be legally compliant, publicly unconceded, physically planted, financially unpaid and biologically failing at the same time.
- **Member participation, OP acceptance, OP pursuit and public concession are independent.** Article 6 collective mechanics support this separation.
- **Preparation, decision, performance, acceptance and settlement are separate.** The public and ARIF materials repeatedly rely on distinct acts, forms and controls.
- **Payment order is not cash.** Advance liquidation and bank settlement are separate effects.
- **Installation is not establishment.** Water and aftercare make this operationally material.
- **Authority is proposition-specific.** Osservatorio, ARIF, CUFAA, Comune, CAA, technician, beneficiary, bank and inspector do not inherit one another’s powers.
- **Evidence requirements must shape work before dispatch.** Passport, lot, inspection, delegation, safety and payment evidence support this.
- **Compulsory and voluntary routes can coexist.** Removal/enforcement can physically precede or disrupt a voluntary recovery package.
- **Map geometry is not a legal verdict.** Provider contract, title, official act and current inspection remain decisive.
- **Selective reopening is correct.** Death, variants, plant-lot problems and payment defects should reopen bounded lines, not reset everything.

### Where CORDON overstates the operator world

- It overstates the value of a complete, continuously evaluated opportunity population before proving that the OP has the data and staffing to act on it.
- It overstates the availability of defensible expected-loss inputs for a formal capacity optimizer. Operators first need a truthful executable schedule and transparent hard constraints.
- It overstates individual Plant identity for ordinary replant populations. Lot, delivery and planting block are more likely to carry supplier, evidence and defect decisions.
- It overstates the value of a persistent five-responsibility rail. Operators likely organize by batch, deadline, counterparty and exception, with responsibilities appearing as attributes.
- It overstates how much “next owner” can be derived. Ordinary follow-up is a social commitment that must be captured or integrated, not inferred from formal authority alone.
- It overstates the usefulness of dense receipt non-implications, premise fingerprints and platform-version detail to the manager. These matter for audit and safety but should stay collapsed unless an exception occurs.
- It overstates the need for one complete three-pane desktop picture before proving the real OP’s screen, mobile, printing and field practices.
- It risks treating a trusted legal corpus as the operational center. Most staff time may be spent making people, documents, supplies, water and invoices line up under already-known rules.

### Where CORDON understates the operator world

- It understates cohort/batch construction and weekly routing.
- It understates bills of quantities, procurement, price, deposits and supplier substitution.
- It understates plant-lot/passport traceability and nursery operational risk.
- It understates water delivery, infrastructure and aftercare as capacity.
- It understates land access, co-ownership, succession and representation continuity.
- It understates private liabilities, VAT/ineligible gaps, bridge cash and individual guarantees inside collective delivery.
- It understates contractor legality, subcontracting, safety and machinery readiness.
- It understates downstream harvest, mill and commercial compatibility at orchard-design time.
- It understates correspondence, phone promises, PEC triage and escalation as the mechanism of movement.
- It understates the OP’s existing apps, technical programmes, traceability and cooperative/mill network.

# Part III — Class 1: facts from sources actually fetched

## S1. Collective execution does not transfer beneficiary identity

# Closing decisions

## 1. Confirmed design alignments

1. Keep public eligibility, member adhesion, OP inclusion/pursuit, mandate acceptance and public concession separate.
2. Keep compulsory removal, voluntary funded recovery and self-funded recovery as independent routes that can share physical planning but not authority.
3. Keep preparation, filing/release, authority outcome, dispatch, performance, inspection, acceptance, liquidation, cash and survival separate.
4. Keep named-action readiness. Replace any generic “ready” label with the exact filing, contract, dispatch, claim or aftercare action.
5. Keep authority proposition-specific. Especially preserve Osservatorio versus ARIF versus CUFAA versus CAA versus technician versus beneficiary versus bank.
6. Keep payment order distinct from cash and installation distinct from establishment.
7. Keep evidence requirements upstream in procurement and field instructions.
8. Keep selective reopening and explicit preservation of unaffected work.
9. Keep map geometry as orientation and candidate scope, never as title, water availability, duty, eligibility or acceptance.
10. Keep the OP manager as coordinator, not a synthetic holder of member, professional or public authority.

## 2. Material missing operator decisions or facts

1. **Executable-package conversion:** has interest become signed authority, source-correct land, finance, design, procurement and field access?
2. **Execution cohort/batch:** which packages share supplier, crew, geography, deadline and evidence closure, and what happens if one drops?
3. **Bill of quantities and delivered-cost gap:** quantities, unit rates, VAT, eligible amount, private overrun and party liable.
4. **Plant order/lot/passport:** firm reservation, deposit, test/passport, delivery, allocation, rejection, substitution and warranty.
5. **Water capacity:** legal source, provider contract, hydrant/well, pressure/flow, system commissioning, outage and emergency fallback.
6. **Orchard-system viability:** cultivar mix, density, pollination, machinery, harvest timing, mill route and member maintenance economics.
7. **Access and constructability:** route, width, slope, rock, stump/root, utilities, roads, canals, neighbours and weather.
8. **Standing continuity:** co-owners, owner/tenant, death, succession, changed representative, lease term, member/CAA/IBAN changes.
9. **Counterparty commitment ladder:** indicated, quoted, reserved, contracted, received/performed, with expiry and evidence.
10. **Private cash feasibility:** deposit source, bridge finance, individual guarantee, expiry, member contribution and OP recourse.
11. **Contractor package:** subcontract chain, DURC, insurance, safety, equipment, operator qualifications and real weekly capacity.
12. **Evidence-to-liability reconciliation:** which delivery, work measurement, invoice and acceptance support which beneficiary and claim amount.
13. **Correspondence/follow-up:** last contact, promise, requested cure, next call and escalation. Formal next owner alone is not enough.
14. **Aftercare service level:** watering, weed control, staking, inspection, mortality threshold, cause review and replacement duty.
15. **Programme-office shared bottleneck:** mass checks, desk/batch, acknowledgement and ageing, not just isolated Proceeding status.
16. **Existing OP system link:** member CRM, technician territories, AgroAPP or equivalent, traceability, accounting and mill/cooperative network.

## 3. Complexity to cut because it adds marginal realism only

1. Do not instantiate every replanted tree. Use parcel/planting block plus plant lot and quantity. Create individual Plants only for official samples/orders, monumental trees and identified defects/replacements.
2. Do not expose 32 semantic links or hidden occurrence machinery to operators. Project one package, batch, decision, evidence and liability view.
3. Defer a formal expected-loss portfolio optimizer. First ship hard-constraint scheduling, transparent inclusion/deferral reasons and manual board approval.
4. Do not build a custom scenario comparator until a real OP supplies two capacity decisions that spreadsheets cannot support safely.
5. Replace the persistent five-responsibility rail as the default with a configurable `Now / batches / exceptions / money / young orchards` operational entry. Preserve responsibilities as filters and completeness checks.
6. Keep legal corpus ingestion, premise fingerprints, tool versions and compatibility manifests backstage. Surface only stale/conflict consequence and cure owner.
7. Do not create generic representations for every possible permit or actor. Add a conditional dependency only when an active package triggers it.
8. Do not require authenticated bank-transaction integration in release 1. Capture beneficiary-level settlement evidence and reconcile it; automate only after access exists.
9. Do not build a universal correspondence/task system. Integrate the OP’s existing email/CRM if possible; otherwise add only dated cure commitments tied to a package.
10. Do not extend Wedge 1 into mill operations or full maturity. Add one downstream compatibility checkpoint before orchard commitment and keep the rest outside.
11. Do not put a dense map in every decision. Population finance, guarantee and document repair are list/batch problems.
12. Do not equate formal completeness of the opportunity universe with operational completeness. Limit release 1 to named programmes the OP actually pursues and show the declared coverage boundary.

## 4. Discoveries that would materially change the build

1. **The OP’s real execution unit is a contractor/nursery batch, not a member-land Intervention.** This would require a durable batch/order/allocation model and a schedule-first home.
2. **The OP fronts or guarantees member costs.** This would make private liability, credit limit, deposit, recourse and treasury exposure core, not a Payments detail.
3. **Water failure is the main cause of replant mortality or non-dispatch.** This would elevate water entitlement/delivery/commissioning into a core capacity object or relationship.
4. **Plant passports and supplier lots are routinely reconciled to claims and warranties.** This would reverse the Gate 5 lot exclusion and reduce ordinary individual-Plant use.
5. **Most case movement happens through one CAA/consultant’s private spreadsheet and PEC inbox.** This would make integration/correspondence capture more valuable than additional ontology or AIP reasoning.
6. **The board, not operations staff, approves member inclusion or supplier/financial exposure above low thresholds.** This would change all four manager Actions and the assumed primary user.
7. **Collective claims are submitted/paid at member grain despite shared invoices and work.** This would require a strong allocation and reconciliation substrate.
8. **The OP already has an AgroAPP/ERP/CRM with usable member, field and traceability records.** This would turn CORDON into a governed decision overlay rather than a new operational system.
9. **Field technicians need phone/offline capture for the evidence that controls payment.** This would trigger the declared move away from desktop-first Workshop or require a bounded OSDK/mobile evidence companion earlier.
10. **Orchard design is routinely changed to fit mill, harvest or market constraints.** This would require downstream compatibility inputs before pursuit/capacity commitment, despite maturity being out of scope.

## 5. Exactly which new direct/private evidence to acquire from a real OP

Acquire the following as redacted copies plus a 60–90 minute screen-share walkthrough for each. Do not accept an interview summary alone.

1. **One live collective Article 6 cohort from first interest to current state:** the OP’s master tracker, adhesion list, selected/non-selected members, mandates, SIAN receipts, concession acts, current blockers and staff annotations.
2. **One executed member package:** signed member/owner authorities, fascicolo snapshot used, cadastral scope, technical project, BoQ, variants, plant order, passports, delivery notes, contractor work orders, field evidence, invoices, inspection/acceptance and payment-claim package.
3. **One failed or withdrawn package:** all correspondence and internal notes showing the first detectable failure, who decided to stop, sunk cost, supplier/batch impact and fallback.
4. **One succession/co-owner/tenant repair:** pre- and post-repair records, CAA actions, legal authority evidence, time elapsed and which OP commitments were suspended or preserved.
5. **One nursery procurement cycle:** request for quote, competing offers, cultivar/specification, availability promise, reservation/deposit, lot/passport/test data, substitutions, delivery allocation by parcel and any rejection/warranty case.
6. **One contractor procurement and weekly schedule:** bid comparison, contract, subcontractors, DURC/insurance/safety pack, machinery/crew capacity, route plan, dispatch changes, measured quantities and invoice approval.
7. **One water pathway for a new orchard:** source right/contract, provider correspondence, connection/hydrant/well, irrigation design, power/pump/filtration, commissioning, watering schedule, outages/tanker fallback and actual cost.
8. **One advance and one final-balance pathway:** beneficiary guarantee application, bank/Confidi exchanges, expiry/renewal, public claim receipt, integration requests, liquidation act, bank settlement evidence, OP accounting allocation and any shortfall.
9. **One young-orchard cohort through two survival checks:** planting blocks and lots, aftercare contract, watering/field logs, mortality method, defect notices, replacements and dispute resolution.
10. **The manager’s actual last four working weeks:** calendar, anonymized email/PEC subject log, WhatsApp categories, call notebook, spreadsheets, portal list, board papers and recurring meetings. Record every handoff and duplicate entry.
11. **One board or authorized-role decision on inclusion, capacity or spend:** agenda paper, alternatives, thresholds, conflict/fairness rules, minutes, override reason and delegated authority matrix.
12. **The OP’s existing systems inventory and exports:** membership/CRM, CAA interfaces, AgroAPP/DSS, technician visit system, traceability/certification, accounting, bank, supplier, mill and document storage. Include identifiers and update ownership.
13. **One mill/commercial planning packet used before orchard design:** member production forecast, cultivar/harvest assumptions, mill capacity/closure risk, machinery compatibility, quality route and how these affected a replant choice.
14. **One programme-office adverse interaction:** integration request, OP/CAA response, authority acknowledgement, internal escalation, time to cure and whether the issue affected one member or a collective batch.
15. **A structured population export for all active recovery work:** one row per member-land package with dates and amounts for interest, adhesion, mandate, OP inclusion, source readiness, concession, guarantee, plant reservation, water readiness, contract, dispatch, performance, acceptance, claim, liquidation, settlement, survival and replacement. Preserve unknowns; do not backfill.

### Observation protocol

During each walkthrough, ask the operator to perform the work rather than describe it. Record:

- the first screen/file opened;
- every identifier copied between systems;
- each phone/PEC/WhatsApp handoff;
- every judgment not stated in the formal rule;
- the actual person with decision authority;
- the point at which a promise becomes a commitment;
- the evidence checked before money or dispatch;
- the fields staff ignore;
- the workaround used when a portal or source is wrong;
- the population affected by one exception; and
- what the manager needs visible at 08:30 Monday morning.

## Final build posture

Do not redesign the full Ontology from this public reconstruction alone. Build the direct-evidence acquisition above as the next gate. Meanwhile, preserve CORDON’s authority and outcome separations, but treat batch, plant-lot, water, private cash, correspondence and downstream compatibility as the five highest-risk omissions. The release-1 product hypothesis should be a **cohort conversion and executable schedule instrument with beneficiary-level financial reconciliation**, not a general legal graph with a map and chatbot attached.
