## Parent adjudication

The pruning lane correctly rejects domain normalization as product architecture. Its 14-element result is a strong lower-bound proposal, not final authority.

Two prunes are rejected.

### Official Area remains a core candidate

The manager must:

- inspect current official condition, zone/regime and governing consequence;
- compare applicability across land, jurisdiction and date;
- select a jurisdiction/area in later map/legal views;
- explain why a Parcel or route is affected;
- detect and selectively propagate area-definition changes.

A geometry/property embedded only on Governing Instrument makes the Instrument—not the authoritative area—the spatial identity. It also complicates independent zone versioning, map selection and one Area affecting many Parcels and Instruments.

Deletion failure: the manager cannot independently select, compare or track an authoritative zone/regime across definition changes and land consequences.

Owen accepts `Official Area` for both administrative jurisdictions and regulatory/plant-health areas through `areaKind`. Geometry is its representation; defining Instruments remain linked basis. A governmental Party and territorial Area remain separate, and jurisdiction never implies competence.

### Individual Plant remains a conditional-population core candidate

Owen’s only persistent later-surface context includes a full tree view. The operating model also requires plant-grain removal, monumental status, survival, defect, replacement and cure where valid identity exists.

Deletion failure: when a real process preserves individual identity, the manager cannot select or revisit the same Plant across official condition, Intervention target, execution, inspection, survival and replacement. Parcel scope/count loses sameness.

Recommendation: keep the `Individual Plant` type, but instantiate only from an external identity-preserving process or validated reproducible observation-derived inventory. Arbitrary detections still create no Plants.

## Why the operator model replaces the normalized model under Palantir guidance

The replacement is not a retreat from domain-driven design. The full Gate 1–4 model establishes reality and prevents false collapse. Gate 5 now applies Palantir's additional product and Ontology guidance to choose the smallest operational representation.

### Start with the decision, not the screen—or the domain catalog

Palantir's operational-application guidance says to name the decision first, identify the information required for it, and show only the objects, links and properties that matter to that decision while leaving other information out ([Operational applications](https://www.palantir.com/docs/foundry/app-building/operational-apps/#1-start-with-the-decision-not-the-screen), [surface the right information](https://www.palantir.com/docs/foundry/app-building/operational-apps/#2-surface-the-right-information-at-the-right-time)).

The former model preserved nearly every domain fact as an object-backed type. The replacement begins with 41 operator decisions and 30 capabilities, then admits representation only where the cooperative manager loses a decision, supervised Action, explanation or selective-reopening capability.

### Decision-centric, not data-complete

Palantir describes the Ontology as representing enterprise decisions and joining the information, logic, execution and policy assurance that comprise them ([Why create an Ontology?](https://www.palantir.com/docs/foundry/ontology/why-ontology/)).

The former model made complete fact ownership the product boundary. The replacement keeps full-fidelity evidence available but makes the shared operator decision loop the core boundary.

### Model reality, then curate intentionally

Palantir's first principle remains “model the real world, not the source data,” but its design checklist also says to curate intentionally and keep object types focused ([Ontology best practices](https://www.palantir.com/docs/foundry/ontology/ontology-best-practices/)).

Gate 1–4 identifies the real entities and predicates. Gate 5 does not deny them. It chooses which must be first-class operator objects and which can remain qualified properties, links, event details, derived views, external outcomes or backstage evidence.

### DRY and one canonical workflow

Palantir's DRY guidance warns that duplicated object shapes and workflows create maintenance burden and context problems for humans and agents; it recommends a single canonical representation or a real abstraction ([Do not repeat yourself](https://www.palantir.com/docs/foundry/ontology/ontology-best-practices/#2-do-not-repeat-yourself-rule-of-three)).

`Operator Party` applies this deliberately. The manager uses Natural Person and Organization through the same identity/authority/handoff capability. `partyKind` preserves the concrete distinction. Holding remains separate because it is a different real entity and decision grain.

### Store each fact once; derive convenience

Palantir's structural guidance says to store each fact once and derive convenience rather than copying it ([Normalization and derived properties](https://www.palantir.com/docs/foundry/ontology/ontology-structural-guidance/#normalization-and-derived-properties)).

The replacement does not copy legal, scientific, public, supplier or bank detail into parallel operator types. It preserves the authoritative fact once below the operator projection and derives current applicability, readiness, affected work, financial stage and remaining exposure.

### Object-backed links are conditional, not the default product shape

Palantir recommends object-backed links when a relationship carries dates, roles, status or allocation, but also says workflows may expose only the direct relationship when detail is unnecessary ([Links and object-backed link types](https://www.palantir.com/docs/foundry/ontology/ontology-structural-guidance/#links-and-object-backed-link-types)).

The former model treated this as one backing object per independently fact-bearing predicate. The corrected model asks whether the manager must navigate or mutate that relationship independently. If not, a direct link, qualified property/history or backstage owner preserves the fact without expanding the core.

### Choose the right tool and avoid Action Sprawl

Palantir's Golden Hammer guidance separates Actions for human decisions, pipelines for data processing, Automations for event reactions and Functions for complex live computation. Its Action Sprawl guidance rejects many narrow Actions in favor of cohesive business operations ([Ontology anti-patterns](https://www.palantir.com/docs/foundry/ontology/ontology-anti-patterns/#anti-pattern-the-golden-hammer), [Action Sprawl](https://www.palantir.com/docs/foundry/ontology/ontology-anti-patterns/#anti-pattern-action-sprawl)).

The replacement keeps three manager-owned Actions and three irreducible Functions as the lower bound. External authority decisions remain outcomes; member/professional/beneficiary/executor operations selectively enter only when those actors genuinely use CORDON.

### Build bounded human-agent operations

Palantir's agent pattern gives agents a small set of governed Actions, commonly staged for human review, and keeps humans accountable for material decisions ([Extending the loop with agents](https://www.palantir.com/docs/foundry/app-building/operational-apps/#extending-the-loop-with-agents), [Why Ontology—Action](https://www.palantir.com/docs/foundry/ontology/why-ontology/#action)).

The replacement's seven-operation authorization envelope is therefore a better basis for the AIP harness than exposing every domain verb. It provides bounded target, authority, refusal, atomic effect, evidence and handoff semantics.

### Prefer pragmatic extension over theoretical perfection

Palantir explicitly calls its principles guides, recommends incremental improvement, and says to prioritize business value over theoretical perfection while defending semantic clarity ([Pragmatism and tradeoffs](https://www.palantir.com/docs/foundry/ontology/ontology-best-practices/#pragmatism-and-tradeoffs)).

The 39+10 model is a defensible normalized upper bound. The 16-element working core is the pragmatic operational lower bound. Conditional elements selectively reopen when a real operator decision proves their value.

## Event/history ruling — accepted

Owen accepts no independent event object types in the initial core. Consequential occurrences remain typed multi-value history details on Governing Instrument, Public Proceeding, Intervention, Cooperative Pursuit or Intervention Capacity Commitment.

Operator-visible histories must preserve:

- protocolled filing versions;
- partial/multiple field executions;
- separate inspection/acceptance occurrences;
- partial/returned/reversed cash transactions;
- selective reopening references;

without creating opaque arrays, Time Machine copies or source-shaped histories.

Every history detail preserves only occurrence reference, kind, actor, authority/basis reference, consequential time, bounded scope, outcome, optional supplied explanation and minimum evidence references. Changed decisions, reopening, next owner, next Action and clock consequence are derived live by the core Functions. Gate 6 selects struct arrays, linked hidden history resources, time series or another supported mechanism.

A focused event type selectively reopens only when the manager must independently select, compare, link or act on the occurrence, or when partial/reversal structure cannot be represented safely in history.

Cooperative Pursuit and Intervention Capacity Commitment own their own factual histories because they are independently mutable Action targets. Do not duplicate their decisions on Interventions. A shared portfolio-decision reference binds atomically changed Commitments. Action logs are supplemental audit, not the semantic owner.

## AIP Action-harness correction

The pre-correction eleven Actions are not the core harness.

Manager-owned Actions survive first. Other operations enter only when:

- the member, technician, beneficiary, executor or private acceptor actually acts through CORDON; or
- CORDON can stage a proposal for that actor with explicit review and authority.

Public eligibility, concession, permit, compulsory acceptance, funded-work acceptance, liquidation, order, settlement, audit, revocation and recovery remain external outcomes unless the competent authority itself uses CORDON.

The seven-operation capability envelope is the correct next abstraction. Exact Action types follow from real authorized transaction boundaries, not from the full decision list.

Owen accepts four initial mutating AIP tools: Accept Cooperative Execution Mandate, Decide Cooperative Pursuit, Commit or Rebalance Intervention Capacity and Dispatch Intervention. All require exact manager authority and may default to human review.

For member, technician, beneficiary, executor and private-acceptor operations, AIP may prepare/stage a package, route it to the real owner and record the received bounded outcome. Those capabilities do not take the reserved decision. An exact non-manager Action selectively reopens only when that actor genuinely transacts through CORDON. Public-authority decisions remain external unless the competent authority itself uses CORDON.

One generic Execute CORDON Action tool is rejected.

## Contextual authority and relationship-object balance

Owen accepts context-owned actor detail: Party remains identity-only; Holding, Parcel, Instrument, Proceeding, Intervention, Pursuit and Capacity Commitment own their scoped roles and authority. Direct Party links remain navigational.

This is not a ban on relationship objects. A relationship object enters when the manager must independently select, compare, mutate, reconcile or preserve its history, or when Gate 6 proves endpoint detail cannot safely carry the contract. Cooperative Pursuit and Intervention Capacity Commitment already pass. A lower type count is not itself a design objective.
