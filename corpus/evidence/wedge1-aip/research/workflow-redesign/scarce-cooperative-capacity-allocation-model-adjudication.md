## Bottom line

The consultant’s central recommendation is correct and improves the earlier boundary.

CORDON should materially facilitate scarce-capacity allocation. It should apply the cooperative’s approved policy to validated facts, hard constraints, uncertainty, action-specific readiness, resource interactions and member terms; compute feasible portfolios; identify bottlenecks; recommend a policy-compliant allocation; explain inclusions, deferrals and counterfactuals; and show alternatives.

The cooperative’s authorized role retains the decision right to commit, override, release and reallocate capacity. CORDON does not invent a universal substantive policy and does not make an unreviewable decision that binds or displaces a member.

This remains inside Funding/Question 3 with readiness inputs from Question 4. It is not a sixth function, independent product or interface decision.

## Correction to Connor’s earlier recommendation

The earlier recommendation reserved allocation too narrowly to cooperative governance and treated CORDON mainly as a recorder of readiness and reasons.

That recommendation is withdrawn.

The corrected boundary is:

1. the cooperative’s competent governance body defines objectives, permissible criteria, fairness safeguards, tie-breakers, override authority and review rights;
2. CORDON applies that approved policy to validated evidence and uncertainty, removes hard constraints first, computes feasible bundles and schedules, identifies binding resources, and recommends a policy-compliant portfolio with alternatives and counterfactuals;
3. the authorized cooperative role commits capacity or overrides the recommendation with a recorded reason;
4. reservations and commitments are scoped, time-bounded and stable until expiry, release or a pre-agreed reallocation condition; and
5. allocation never changes public eligibility, concession, duty, creditor identity or individual fallback.

## Open policy decision

The consultant’s proposed hierarchy is not automatically accepted:

1. prevent irreversible legal or contractual loss;
2. apply member-fairness safeguards;
3. maximize expected realized recovery;
4. optimize the resource bundle; and
5. use a neutral tie-breaker.

This is a strong recommended template, but Owen must decide whether it is the default policy structure CORDON should help a cooperative formalize. Local cooperative documents and direct observation may later refine the permissible criteria and governance roles.

## Executive resolution

Owen selected Option A.

- Non-discretionary legal, safety, instrument, firm-commitment, action-readiness and resource-feasibility rules apply first.
- Cooperative-approved fairness safeguards further constrain the feasible set.
- CORDON recommends the feasible portfolio with the highest expected realized Xylella-loss reduction.
- Projected loss avoided, completion probability, aid or rights protected, restoration and aftercare success, financing burden and uncertainty remain separately explainable.
- CORDON shows alternatives and sensitivity. It does not manufacture a unique ordering when the evidence is not robust.
- An approved neutral tie-breaker applies only to materially equivalent portfolios.
- The authorized cooperative role commits or overrides the recommendation.

The earlier open policy question is resolved by this section. Local cooperative evidence can refine fairness safeguards, input definitions and governance roles without changing the accepted objective structure.
