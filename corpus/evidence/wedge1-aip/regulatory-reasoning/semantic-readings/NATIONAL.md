# Italian national bounded semantic reading

Status: **supporting source reading — no current-authority effect**

Current authority: `../STAGE_A_AUTHORITY_SNAPSHOT.json`.

Jurisdiction: Italian national law and national implementing instruments
Sources currently read:

- D.lgs. 19/2021 Articles 5–11, 13 and 14 in force on 28 August 2026

## Reading posture

The national layer does not restate or replace Regulation 2020/1201. It establishes who exercises plant-health authority in Italy, how national and regional bodies coordinate, which plans and ordinances bind implementation, how official findings and laboratory work are organized, and how measures are executed and enforced.

The EU reading controls the substantive outbreak-response branches. The national reading asks, for each reached EU meaning:

```text
which Italian actor owns or coordinates it?
which decision or procedure gives it national effect?
which regional actor implements it?
which evidence, notice, access or enforcement route supports execution?
```

The source bodies are read as one institutional and procedural mechanism. Article numbers and prior candidate IDs do not define process families.

## D.lgs. 19/2021: institutional authority

### Article 5 — central plant-health service

The central service is the single national authority for coordination and supervision of plant-health activities and the national liaison authority under Regulation 2017/625 for plant protection.

Its operative functions relevant to the bounded mechanism include:

- coordination and official communication with the Commission, Member States and international plant-health organizations;
- operation of the national committee;
- adoption of plant-protection measures, technical standards, operating procedures and stricter prescriptions after committee opinion;
- adoption of plant-health ordinances conforming to committee-approved acts;
- adoption of the national survey programme, national control plan, emergency plans and action plans after committee opinion;
- collection of pest presence/spread data and annual reporting;
- official determination of national pest status;
- staff training and audit;
- maintenance of national registers and regional data-transmission rules.

The central service coordinates and adopts national instruments. It does not absorb the regional service’s territorial decision and implementation powers.

Article 5(2) is temporally versioned. The 2023 amendment changes the central-service staffing/organizational wording and named competence units. That change must be read against the original and intermediate versions before any temporal serialization.

### Article 6 — regional plant-health services

Regional plant-health services are the authorities responsible for territorial implementation of national and Union plant-health activities.

For the accepted EU mechanism, their statutory powers include:

- applying Union and national plant-health law;
- official surveillance of cultivated and spontaneous plants and products;
- responsibility for official plant-health analyses;
- defining demarcated areas after a Union quarantine-pest finding, following national committee opinion;
- investigating demarcated areas;
- preparing action plans for priority pests after hearing the national committee;
- establishing violations;
- prescribing all necessary official measures, including destruction of contaminated or suspected plants/products and other objects capable of spreading pests;
- collecting pest-status/reporting information;
- auditing delegated official-control bodies;
- applying national survey programmes, emergency measures, emergency plans and national action plans;
- notifying the central service of pests previously absent from the regional territory;
- developing and communicating prophylaxis and plant-health defence strategies.

This is the central national-to-Puglia seam. The Osservatorio’s territorial decisions must derive from these powers and the applicable regional organization. A regional implementation act may allocate execution or procedure within competence; it cannot convert a non-regional decision into its own authority.

### Article 7 — national plant-health committee

The national committee brings together the central director, regional service heads and the national reference institute, with scientific participation as needed.

It:

- defines national plant-protection lines;
- supervises application and corrective measures;
- approves plant-health measures, technical standards and operating procedures;
- approves the national survey programme, emergency plans and action plans;
- gives opinions on reference-laboratory designation;
- approves risk analyses and training programmes.

Committee-approved acts are implemented by ordinances of the central-service director published in the Italian Official Journal.

The committee’s approval, central ordinance and regional implementation are distinct legal events. One cannot be inferred from evidence of another.

### Article 8 — national reference institute

CREA-DC is the national plant-protection reference institute and the scientific support body for the national service.

Its reached functions include:

- scientific work on new plant-health risks and eradication/containment measures;
- plant-health risk analysis;
- diagnostic and confirmatory diagnosis support;
- pathogen characterization and taxonomy;
- coordination and audit of national reference, official and network laboratories;
- development and validation of analytical methods;
- comparative testing, follow-up and laboratory training;
- communication of technical information and reports.

Scientific support does not exercise the competent authority’s legal branch selection. Diagnostic confirmation and method validation supply legally required evidence.

### Article 9 — emergency secretariat

The emergency secretariat is the national technical-operational bridge between the national committee and territorial emergency units.

For declared emergencies, it coordinates:

- implementation of action-plan measures;
- communication plans;
- verification and audit of measure execution;
- submissions for European contribution;
- proposals and documents for committee decision;
- periodic reporting on emergency evolution.

These are coordination and assurance duties. They do not activate the underlying EU measure, and execution evidence does not replace the regional authority decision.

### Article 10 — territorial emergency units

For each emergency declared by the national committee, affected regional services establish a territorial emergency unit. The unit implements the action plan and central ordinances under the participants’ legal competences.

It coordinates and organizes:

- plant-health measures;
- communication plans;
- verification of execution;
- European-contribution requests;
- documents for the national committee;
- continuing information exchange with the national secretariat.

The territorial unit is a coordination structure, not a new source of substantive legal duties. Regional services remain the competent territorial authorities.

### Article 11 — regional organizational duties

Regions establish and regulate their regional plant-health services, organize technical/operational/administrative actions for immediate emergency response and guarantee the resources necessary to implement plant-health measures.

Regional autonomy permits organizational design. It does not authorize omission or reassignment of statutory regional plant-health responsibilities without legal basis.

## D.lgs. 19/2021: laboratory authority

### Article 13 — national reference laboratories

The Minister designates one or more national reference laboratories under Regulation 2017/625. Designation requires the Union-law conditions, is published, and may be revoked after committee opinion when requirements or duties cease to be met.

Reference laboratories coordinate official laboratories, comparative testing, method harmonization, information transfer, technical assistance, reagent validation, training and confirmatory diagnosis.

The designation act, continuing qualification, method scope and requested confirmatory work must remain distinct.

### Article 14 — official laboratories

Regional services obtain and designate official laboratories after verifying Regulation 2017/625 requirements and hearing the national committee. Designation is written and specifies tasks and conditions.

A region may designate:

- its own official laboratory;
- an already designated laboratory of another regional service by agreement, including for specific methods;
- an external laboratory that satisfies Article 37 requirements.

Samples from official controls and other official activities are analysed within this designated authority chain. A laboratory result is not legally official merely because the method is valid or the laboratory is technically capable.

## D.lgs. 19/2021 Article 20 — reached plant-health-assistant dependency

Article 20 is reached by the current DDS 45 field-work procedure and the regional delegation chain. It supplies five bounded meanings:

1. A plant-health assistant is a professionally qualified technician operating outside a regional plant-health service and answering technically to the territorially competent service.
2. The Central Plant Health Service may appoint assistants after National Plant Health Committee opinion; appointed assistants follow the territorial service manager’s directions and enter the national personnel register.
3. An assistant may perform official plant-protection activities except prescribing official measures or issuing phytosanitary certificates.
4. An assistant acts only on express assignment from the competent service manager and within the assigned functions.
5. An assistant may propose a plant-health measure or sanction to the inspector; proposal is not decision.

The article allocates bounded field execution and proposal authority. It does not transfer official confirmation, area, prescription, certificate, violation or sanction ownership from the competent service.

Article 24 supplies the reached register state. The Central Plant Health Service maintains the national personnel register in SIPP, divided into sections and carrying the source-native identity, qualification, function, organizational, operating-site and authentic-signature tuple. Regional services communicate updates for central validation. Current personnel status also requires that no Article 24(5) cancellation has taken effect after reassignment, non-plant-health duties or cessation.

## First national integration findings

1. The regional service is the direct territorial authority for demarcation, investigations, official measures, destruction, survey-plan application, new-pest notification and violations.
2. The central service owns national coordination, Union communication, national plans, survey programmes, standards and ordinances.
3. Committee approval and central ordinance publication are distinct from regional implementation.
4. The emergency secretariat and territorial unit coordinate implementation and verification but do not replace competent-authority decisions.
5. Scientific and laboratory bodies produce official or confirmatory evidence under designation; they do not select legal branches.
6. A correct authority graph must preserve this seam without multiplying each institutional role into a product state.

## D.lgs. 19/2021: emergency lifecycle

### Article 26 — emergency plans

The central service, supported by the national reference institute, prepares and maintains an emergency plan for every priority pest and other committee-designated pests. The plan covers survey methods, decision processes, responsibilities, procedures, protocols, minimum resources and surge resources for suspected or officially confirmed presence.

The Minister adopts the plan after national committee opinion. Monitoring and updating are continuing central-service duties. Simulation procedures are separately defined.

The adopted Xylella plan is therefore a binding implementing instrument under Article 26. A draft, technical workbook or regional proposal is not the national plan.

### Article 27 — national survey programme

Regional services conduct territorial surveys. The national programme is assembled through a defined annual cycle:

```text
central risk-based objectives and criteria
→ regional territorial risk analyses and next-year proposals
→ central proposed programme
→ committee opinion
→ central adoption and publication
→ regional execution
→ national and Union reporting
```

External public/research bodies may contribute additional information under the central service’s defined rules. Contributed data does not become an official finding merely by entering the information system.

The statutory survey programme and the Xylella-specific Article 2/10/15 survey duties intersect but are not identical. The national programme implements the recurring survey obligation; specific EU branches still control populations, confidence/prevalence, geography and methods.

### Article 28 — finding, suspicion and official confirmation

Anyone who learns of actual or suspected relevant pest presence or imminent danger must communicate it immediately to the competent regional service or the named forwarding authorities before public dissemination.

Professional operators immediately adopt precautionary measures against establishment and spread.

When the regional service suspects or detects a relevant pest through reports, surveys or official controls, it obtains official confirmation based on diagnosis by an Article 14 official laboratory. While confirmation is pending, it adopts immediate appropriate measures to prevent spread. Public and private subjects must follow the regional service’s necessary phytosanitary instructions, including plant/product/object elimination.

The source distinguishes:

- report or suspicion;
- provisional precaution;
- official laboratory diagnosis;
- official confirmation;
- subsequent legal response.

No missing confirmation result is a negative result, and no reported suspicion is automatically an official plant finding for EU Article 4.

### Article 29 — Union notification

After official confirmation:

- the regional service enters presence/suspicion and initial measures in the European system within five working days;
- the central service officially notifies the Commission within the following three working days.

The clocks have different actors and sequential anchors. The regional draft/entry does not itself constitute the central official notification.

### Article 30 — affected operators and public information

After confirmation, the regional service informs without delay professional operators whose plants/products/objects may be affected. For a priority pest, the national committee defines how the public is informed about adopted/intended measures and required operator/person actions.

This implements the awareness and affected-party communication layer. It is distinct from the legal service of an individual or mass measure under Article 33 and Law 241/1990.

### Article 31 — emergency response and action plan

After confirmation, the regional service:

- investigates origin and possible spread through plant/product/object movements without delay;
- immediately adopts urgent measures to eliminate spread risk;
- establishes a demarcated area under Regulation 2016/2031 Article 18;
- periodically surveys the area, modifies it where necessary and reports to the central service.

At the first useful meeting, the national committee defines and approves measures under the emergency plan. The regional service then has fifteen working days to propose an eradication or containment action plan, including implementation calendar and survey design. The central service declares the emergency, officially adopts the plan and notifies the Commission. It may activate the emergency secretariat, and the regional service establishes the territorial unit that implements the adopted measures.

Article 31(3) states that the established area is one in which eradication measures must be adopted, while Article 31(5) expressly contemplates a later eradication-or-containment plan. This requires authority adjudication with:

- EU Article 12 and effective Annex III;
- the timing and function of urgent measures;
- committee approval;
- the central adoption act;
- the regional area/action act.

The national wording cannot independently make containment available outside EU Article 12. Nor should the Article 31(3) phrase be silently erased from the initial-response logic.

## D.lgs. 19/2021: execution and enforcement

### Article 32 — primary and substitute execution

Regional services implement all necessary official plant-protection measures for plants, products and objects with confirmed or suspected introduction/spread risk.

Owners and holders of the land have the primary duty to implement the prescribed measures and bear their economic cost. Substitute execution arises only where implementation is omitted, incomplete or nonconforming with the prescribed terms or methods. The competent regional service then completes the measures, determines the necessary costs and has recourse against the primary obligor.

The primary duty and substitute remedy are distinct legal meanings:

```text
valid prescribed measure
→ owner/holder implementation and cost duty
→ evidence of complete conformity OR proven omission/incompleteness/deviation OR unresolved evidence
→ substitute execution and cost recovery only on the proven breach route
```

Completion evidence does not activate the primary duty. Missing evidence does not prove breach.

### Article 33 — emergency derogation, protected plants, notice and access

Official emergency plant-health measures, including destruction of contaminated plants, operate in derogation from otherwise applicable provisions—including protective constraints—only within the limits and criteria of the emergency measures and Union law.

For monumental or historic-interest plants not confirmed infected, removal may be ordered case by case by the competent phytosanitary authority after landscape authorization under D.lgs. 42/2004 Article 146, due within forty-five days.

Where Law 241/1990 Article 21-bis conditions apply, central or regional services determine the communication methods and terms. After the prescribed publicity:

- authorized inspectors, agents and support personnel may access land containing infected plants to implement the measures;
- if owners oppose intervention, the territorial service may seek public-force assistance from the Prefect.

The emergency derogation is not a universal exemption from every legal requirement. Protected uninfected plants retain the stated case-specific authorization route. Notice/publicity, access authorization, owner opposition and Prefect assistance are independent steps.

### Article 55 — sanctions relevant to the admitted mechanism

Article 55 contains a broad sanction catalogue. Only sanctions reached by the admitted outbreak-response mechanism enter this reading.

Relevant routes include:

- failure to report actual/suspected findings or imminent danger under Article 28(1);
- omission of prescribed removal by owners/holders, with possible coercive removal and cost charging;
- violation of a regional planting prohibition, followed by a fifteen-day removal/destruction obligation after notice and coercive execution on noncompliance;
- failure to execute regional, ministerial or ordinance phytosanitary measures;
- refusal or obstruction of official access/control;
- Law 689/1981 procedure where the decree is silent;
- regional competence to impose sanctions.

A sanction requires the legally defined violation and its evidence. The sanction or coercive remedy never activates the underlying duty. Primary execution, substitute execution, coercive removal, doubled obstruction sanctions and general nonexecution sanctions must not be collapsed.

## Current national mechanism from D.lgs. 19/2021

The statute implements the EU loop as:

```text
national readiness and survey programme
→ report/suspicion and precaution
→ official laboratory confirmation
→ regional urgent response and origin investigation
→ area institution and committee-approved measures
→ regional action-plan proposal
→ central emergency declaration/adoption/notification
→ regional and territorial implementation
→ owner/holder primary execution
→ substitute/coercive execution only on proven noncompliance
→ sanctions and reporting
```

It also establishes enduring structures—regional services, committee, secretariat, territorial units and laboratories—that support many processes rather than defining separate case families.

## D.lgs. 19/2021 temporal reading

### 13 March 2021 original state

The statute enters into force with the institutional, emergency, survey, finding, execution and sanction mechanism described above.

Article 5(2) originally places qualified central-service staff within the Ministry’s existing establishment and organizes units for:

- committee/secretariat work;
- emergency secretariat;
- import controls;
- production/territory controls;
- export controls and barrier removal;
- training and audit;
- communication;
- European/international liaison.

The substantive central powers in Article 5(4) remain separate and unchanged in the held versions.

### 23 April 2023 decree-law state

D.L. 44/2023 Article 23 replaces Article 5(2). The new text:

- permits staff within the MASAF and CREA establishments;
- reorganizes the units around import coordination, certification/marketing disciplines, export coordination, training/audit/communication and plant-protection-product/PAN work;
- retains committee and emergency-secretariat units.

The amendment act also changes inspector/agent personnel provisions and the staffing annex. Those provisions are background unless a later admitted access or inspection decision requires their exact qualification rule.

### 22 June 2023 conversion-era wording

The current Normattiva text states that Article 5 has been in force in its current wording since 22 June 2023. Compared with the 23 April text, some unit descriptions change wording, including certification/marketing and export-control phrases.

The exact interval is preserved as source fact. Whether the June wording creates independently executable semantic change is deferred to adjudication. A new timestamp is not automatically a new legal atom, but potentially changed competence wording cannot be ignored as editorial without reason.

The other held admitted D.lgs. articles show no identified substantive change between 13 March 2021 and 28 August 2026. That negative remains subject to direct source equality checks before serialization.

## 2022 national Xylella emergency plan

The ministerial decree adopts the plan from 18 March 2022 and repeals the 2018 emergency measure. The plan remains in force through 5 August 2026.

### Legal role

The plan implements D.lgs. 19/2021 Article 26. It defines national responsibilities, survey design, diagnostic practice and response sequencing for suspected or confirmed *Xylella* presence.

Its scientific and risk sections explain why particular survey populations, timing and methods matter. They do not convert every cited model, species observation or literature value into an independently coercive legal rule.

### Annual surveillance

Regional services conduct annual surveys under EU Article 2 and the national Article 27 programme. The plan requires risk-based coverage across cultivated, natural, urban, nursery and commercial environments and links suspicion/evidence to immediate confirmation measures.

For the general survey design, the plan states that from 1 January 2023 the design must detect 1% prevalence at 80% confidence. It then identifies target population, epidemiological units, method sensitivity and risk factors as design inputs.

The plan’s `0.55` sensitivity value is expressly a reference value that may be changed based on laboratory performance and sampler experience. It is not an immutable statutory constant.

The plan describes RiBESS+ as a sample-size tool but states that the tool does not determine sample distribution. Regional services must estimate plant populations and distribute samples through territorial grids and risk factors. The named software does not own the legal survey obligation.

### Official inspection and sampling

Officially appointed and trained personnel perform inspection and sampling. The plan preserves source-native sampling and chain-of-custody requirements:

- season and plant material appropriate to detection;
- single-plant symptomatic samples and bounded pooling for asymptomatic plants;
- unique plant/pool marking;
- sealed, linked sample identity;
- transfer by inspectors or delegates to an official regional laboratory, with cross-region transfer only where regional capacity is unavailable.

These are execution and evidence requirements. They do not activate the survey duty.

### Vector monitoring

The plan distinguishes:

- risk-area monitoring of potentially relevant xylem-feeding insects;
- adult-vector collection and molecular testing;
- annual demarcated-area monitoring of population density and life stages;
- juvenile monitoring for intervention timing;
- georeferenced positive-vector samples followed by plant investigation;
- evaluation of treatment effectiveness.

Biological observations guide timing. They do not replace competent regional adoption of the applicable control measures.

### Diagnosis

Annex IV methods control detection and subspecies identification. A positive result in a pest-free area requires national-reference-laboratory confirmation using another molecular method targeting a different genome region, on the same sample or appropriate extract.

The plan also points to a separate national diagnostic procedure. That procedure must be read as an incorporated technical authority, not inferred from the plan’s summary.

### Official response

After official confirmation, the regional service:

- begins origin investigation without delay;
- adopts urgent measures;
- prepares a notification draft within five working days;
- informs affected operators/owners and prescribes mandatory actions;
- investigates traceability and epidemiology;
- establishes the demarcated area unless Article 5’s exception applies;
- maintains updates to the national structures and Union systems.

The national committee approves urgent measures. The regional service submits an action plan within fifteen working days. The central service declares the emergency, adopts the plan and notifies the Commission.

The 2022 plan says this action-plan proposal is for eradication, while the statute permits an eradication or containment plan and the plan itself later recognizes Annex III containment. The source must therefore be read in the full authority context rather than turning the one word into a universal branch rule.

### Area state

The regional service officially establishes the demarcated area after epidemiological investigation and acquires the legal/operational evidence needed for area management. It publishes detailed maps and identifies affected authorities and populations.

The plan recognizes:

- 50 m infected zones;
- eradication and containment buffers;
- multiple infected-zone points within one eradication demarcated area;
- Article 5 reduced-buffer/no-immediate-area decisions;
- four-year lifting;
- public mapping and affected-party communication.

A published map is evidence and communication of the area. The exact regional constitutive act and temporal state remain necessary.

### Eradication and containment measures

The plan restates the then-effective Article 7 removal populations, precautions, vector treatment, root treatment, destruction routes, limited-destruction judgment and historical-value derogation.

It also recognizes that Annex III areas use Articles 12–17 containment and remove infected plants found through Article 15 monitoring.

National implementation does not make one case exemplar the process. The plan provides reusable routes that regional acts select and specialize.

### Vector measures

The plan directs periodic all-stage vector control and distinguishes juvenile vegetation management, alternative methods where mechanical access is difficult and authorized adult treatments. Regional annual monitoring informs timing and effectiveness.

The plan’s stated dates are biological/operational guidance unless a competent annual or regional act adopts exact enforceable windows.

### Continuing surveillance

The plan specifies eradication infected-zone, buffer-zone and containment survey designs and vector monitoring. Its 5 km value is the Article 4 containment buffer zone outside the infected-zone boundary.

M5 separately reduces Article 15(2)(a)’s minimum inward surveillance band inside the infected zone from 5 km to 2 km. The national-plan 5 km buffer and the post-M5 2 km surveillance band therefore coexist and do not create a width conflict.

### Movement boundary and awareness

The plan also implements movement controls, production-site authorization and inspection. Those remain later transaction families except where they define the outbreak-response boundary or Article 32 official checks.

Regional services must make area geography and measures publicly available and maintain targeted awareness. That communication duty is distinct from legal service of an individual measure.

### Execution and enforcement

The plan confirms regional competence to prescribe measures and permits use of external implementers under agreements. It also restates Article 32 owner/holder primary implementation and cost, followed by regional substitute execution and recourse only where performance is omitted, incomplete or nonconforming.

The plan’s earlier statement that the regional service acts directly or through third parties, including owners under supervision, must be reconciled with the statutory primary-duty sequence. It does not erase the owner’s legal duty or allow missing completion evidence to prove noncompliance.

### Plan version boundary

The plan is historical after 5 August 2026. Its biology and completed historical acts remain facts. Its procedures do not govern later decisions after replacement unless the successor expressly preserves or independently re-adopts them.

The plan also experienced an in-force EU amendment during its lifetime. Historical resolution must combine the effective plan text with the effective EU provisions rather than treating the plan’s 2022 wording as frozen higher law.

## 2026 national Xylella emergency plan

The 16 July 2026 ministerial decree adopts the replacement plan under D.lgs. 19/2021 Article 26, after national committee approval, and expressly repeals/replaces the 2022 decree. It enters into force the day after ministry-portal publication; the held authority state begins 6 August 2026.

### Legal purpose and authority

The plan governs suspected or confirmed *Xylella* nationally. It seeks rapid coordinated eradication or, where eradication is impossible, maximum containment of spread into surrounding pest-free areas.

The plan identifies the statutory authority chain explicitly:

```text
central service coordination and national adoption
→ committee approval and mandatory measures
→ regional service confirmation, area, plan and prescriptions
→ emergency secretariat coordination
→ territorial unit implementation and verification
→ CREA/reference-laboratory scientific and confirmatory support
```

This confirms the institutional reading from D.lgs. 19/2021. The diagram and role list do not create new institutions or substantive powers.

### Survey design

The 2026 plan retains annual regional surveys and the Article 27 national programme, but updates the design process:

- EFSA’s current statistical model is identified as RiPEST;
- target population, epidemiological units, method sensitivity and risk factors remain distinct inputs;
- regional services estimate territorial populations and select local risk factors;
- the model calculates sample size but does not distribute samples;
- regions distribute the sample through additional spatial units/grids.

The 2022 `0.55` reference sensitivity is no longer stated. Method sensitivity remains a derived input based on field and laboratory performance. No old default should be carried into the current plan by temporal inertia.

### Inspection and sample identity

The plan updates the source-native sampling method:

- sampling may occur year-round where plant tissue and biology permit;
- deciduous-host leaf sampling is avoided during low-detectability periods, but dormant lignified tissue may be used;
- every sampled plant is georeferenced or marked;
- every sample is uniquely identifiable from field collection through diagnostic result;
- sealed samples move promptly to a laboratory officially designated by the competent regional service.

The current text no longer assumes the laboratory must be physically located in the same region. Legal designation and competent-service relationship control.

### Vector investigation

Potential xylem-feeding vectors are surveyed to understand host relationships, life cycles and intervention timing. Both juvenile and adult stages matter for different operational decisions.

Incorporating M5 Article 2(4a), a confirmed infected vector in an area where the pest is not known triggers immediate survey, sampling and analysis of host and suspected plants within 400 m. That is a plant-investigation route, not automatic demarcation.

### Laboratory and diagnostic authority

Official-control diagnoses use designated laboratories in the national network. The 2022 national emergency plan identifies CREA-DC and CNR-IPSP as national reference laboratories for *Xylella*. It does not identify the Bari seat. The 12 April 2022 ministerial decree defines network competence.

For the first positive in a pest-free area not attributable to nearby demarcated-area expansion, a national reference laboratory confirms with another molecular method targeting a different genome region. Subspecies identification applies to each infected plant species in the demarcated area. DTU 39 defines diagnostic procedures.

The plan distinguishes official diagnosis, second-level confirmation, subspecies identification and operator self-control. Those evidence routes cannot be merged.

### Three-phase emergency flow

#### Phase 1

After official laboratory diagnosis and any required reference confirmation, the regional service:

- confirms the finding;
- investigates origin;
- informs central, reference and other regional services;
- informs potentially affected professional operators;
- adopts urgent measures;
- establishes the area where applicable;
- drafts the Union notification within five working days.

The central service validates and officially transmits within the next three working days. The committee determines public-information methods.

#### Phase 2

At the first useful meeting, the committee approves initial measures. The regional service submits the action plan within fifteen days. The committee approves the plan and mandatory measures. The central service declares the emergency through an ordinance adopting the plan and notifies the Commission.

#### Phase 3

The regional service establishes the territorial unit, which implements the action plan under each participant’s competence. The regional service monitors and modifies the area where necessary. The emergency secretariat verifies implementation.

This sequence preserves distinct findings, approvals, adoption, implementation and verification events.

### Traceability and epidemiology

The regional service investigates supply, movement, destination, mother-plant, propagation and passive-vector routes and communicates results without delay. Epidemiological investigation determines real spread and informs area institution.

The plan’s concentric-area/gridded approach is recommended method, not a substitute for competent survey design or legal geography.

### No-immediate-demarcation route

The plan requires the regional service to test EU Article 5(3) conditions. Where it decides not to establish an area, it communicates the decision/evidence and carries out annual surveys for at least two years.

EU M5 reduced its own minimum to at least one year. The 2026 national plan was adopted with knowledge of M5 and retains a two-year national requirement. This may be a valid stricter national implementation under competent authority rather than stale text. Adjudication must test rank, competence and intent; it must not silently reduce the national period to the EU floor or assume every longer period is deliberate.

### Area-state lifecycle

After traceability and epidemiological investigation, the regional service establishes the area without delay and applies the action-plan measures.

The plan preserves:

- 50 m infected-zone minimum;
- 2.5 km eradication buffer;
- 5 km containment buffer;
- multiple infected zones in one eradication area;
- owner/holder, land-use, protected-area, access and production evidence;
- detailed public mapping;
- Article 5 reduced/no-area routes;
- four-year lifting and area redefinition when one infected zone is revoked.

The plan therefore requires exact area-state versions. Its generic map/publication process cannot replace each constitutive regional act.

### Action-plan contents

Regional action plans must define roles, command, coordination, delegated official activities, laboratory/operator coordination, cross-region coordination, access routes, communications, five-year record retention, survey methods and training.

These are required plan contents. They are not separate legal process families unless they change independently in an operative action plan.

### Eradication

The current plan implements M5 Article 7:

- current removal populations;
- no removal for negative-tested Article 7(1)(e) plants;
- two-year no-infection route with continuing Article 10 surveys;
- removal precautions and flight-season vector treatment;
- root removal/devitalization;
- destruction location and protected transport;
- limited branch/foliage destruction and retained wood free of leaves/branches;
- protected historic/social/cultural/environmental plant retention conditions;
- case-specific removal of uninfected protected plants after landscape authorization.

Implementation occurs under D.lgs. Article 32. The plan also permits direct regional personnel or supervised third parties. Adjudication must preserve the owner/holder primary duty and the conditions for substitute/direct execution rather than reading the implementation sentence in isolation.

### Containment

Annex III infected zones use Articles 12–17. Removal is limited to infected plants found through Article 15 monitoring. The 50 m sampling/testing population remains distinct.

Containment availability still requires the effective Annex III list and competent-authority branch selection. The national plan does not itself place every Italian area into containment.

### Vector measures

The plan implements M5’s agricultural/non-agricultural distinction and continuing control regardless of individual removal. Juvenile methods, alternative difficult-access methods and authorized adult treatments remain distinct. Annual Article 10/15 surveillance informs timing and efficacy.

Biological timing descriptions do not independently create one nationwide calendar.

### Annual demarcated-area surveillance

The 2026 plan uses the current EU designs:

- eradication infected zone: 90% confidence, 0.5% prevalence;
- eradication buffer: 90% confidence, 1% prevalence with first 400 m higher risk;
- containment infected edge: at least 2 km, 90% confidence, 0.7% prevalence;
- containment buffer: at least 5 km, 90% confidence, 1% prevalence with first 400 m higher risk;
- vector monitoring across route-specific areas.

This supersedes the 2022 plan’s 5 km Article 15 edge-band wording from 6 August 2026. It does not answer whether the 2022 plan’s 5 km rule remained a valid stricter national requirement between 17 October 2024 and 5 August 2026.

### Movement, resources, training and communication

Movement and import controls remain later transaction programs except where they define the outbreak boundary and Article 32/33 checks.

Regions must fund implementation under D.lgs. Article 11. Central services may seek Union cofinancing. Training and public communication are continuing duties. Resource and communication provisions do not create entitlement, aid or case status.

### Plan replacement

The 2026 plan is the current national Xylella emergency plan from 6 August 2026. The 2022 plan is not a current procedural authority after replacement. ## Notice, protected-plant and sanction procedure

### Law 241/1990 Article 21-bis

A measure limiting a private person’s legal sphere becomes effective against each recipient through communication.

Where personal communication is impossible or especially burdensome because of recipient volume, the administration may establish suitable publicity forms for that measure. A nonsanctioning limiting measure may contain a reasoned immediate-effect clause. Cautionary and urgent limiting measures are immediately effective.

This creates distinct effectiveness routes:

```text
personal communication
OR suitable authority-defined mass publicity
OR reasoned immediate-effect clause for a nonsanctioning measure
OR cautionary/urgent immediate effect
```

Publication is not automatically valid notice for every measure. The competent act must establish the route and satisfy the statutory conditions.

### D.lgs. 42/2004 Article 146

Landscape authorization is an autonomous prerequisite for interventions affecting protected landscape interests. The regional authority or lawfully delegated authority administers the procedure, with the superintendent’s role and statutory clocks.

D.lgs. 19 Article 33 creates a specific emergency interaction: removal of an unconfirmed monumental/historic-interest plant may be ordered case by case only after landscape authorization, due within forty-five days.

That branch must not be merged with:

- removal of confirmed infected plants;
- the EU Article 7(3) retention derogation;
- general emergency derogation;
- ordinary owner execution.

### Law 689/1981

Where D.lgs. 19 does not provide otherwise, the general administrative-sanction process supplies:

- investigative acts and evidence collection;
- immediate contest where possible;
- notification within the applicable time where immediate contest did not occur;
- reduced payment within sixty days;
- report to the competent authority when reduced payment is not made;
- thirty days for defensive submissions and a hearing request;
- motivated injunction/payment order or motivated archive decision;
- opposition before the ordinary judicial authority under the current procedural law.

The regional service’s competence to impose D.lgs. 19 sanctions does not eliminate the contest, defence, decision and appeal sequence. A violation finding, sanction contest and final enforceable injunction are different legal states.

## Laboratory designation and official-result chain

### 2021 national-reference designation

The held ministerial decree designates two CREA-DC laboratories as national reference laboratories:

- Florence for insects/acari and nematodes;
- Rome for bacteria, fungi/oomycetes and viruses/viroids/phytoplasmas.

The decree operates under D.lgs. 19 Article 13 and Regulation 2017/625. It takes effect the day after publication on the ministry and national-service sites.

For *Xylella* bacteriology, the held designation supports the CREA-DC Rome route.

### 12 April 2022 laboratory decree

The ministerial decree defines the national laboratory system. It distinguishes:

- national reference laboratories;
- official laboratories;
- research laboratories;
- self-control laboratories;
- seed-commercialization laboratories;
- micropropagation laboratories.

Only national reference and official laboratories perform official-control/other-official-activity analyses and diagnoses on official samples. Research and self-control laboratories may use valid methods and must report relevant detections, but their results remain nonofficial until the competent service completes the official confirmation route.

An analysis is official only where the legal and evidence chain includes:

```text
official control or other official activity
+ sample taken under the competent official process
+ laboratory validly designated for the area/method
+ applicable official method
+ test report meeting the required standard
```

The decree also separates designation, audit, suspension and revocation. A laboratory’s historic designation cannot be assumed current after suspension or revocation.

### Official laboratories

Regional services designate official laboratories after verifying requirements and hearing the national committee. Public or private laboratories may act in the name and on behalf of the designating regional service through specific agreements. Cross-region designation is permitted under the stated audit/designation conditions.

Official laboratories:

- analyse official samples;
- participate in comparative testing and audits;
- issue and retain official test reports;
- operate only while designation and scope remain valid.

### Research and self-control detections

Research and recognized self-control laboratories must report quarantine, regulated, or previously unreported organisms to the competent regional services within twenty-four hours. That report triggers the regional confirmation process. It does not itself establish an official finding.

### Official methods

Union-prescribed methods control where they exist. Otherwise, the decree defines a priority order among EPPO/IPPC, EU-reference, official national technical documents, national-reference and validated internal methods. Urgent use of nonnormalized methods is bounded by the stated absence/unavailability conditions.

The current EU Annex IV remains directly controlling for the Article 2/7/15/18 routes that expressly require it.

### Ordinance 10/2025

The central-service ordinance requires official-control plant samples to be analysed exclusively by accredited official laboratories designated under D.lgs. Article 14. Where a region lacks an accredited laboratory, it designates and uses an accredited laboratory already designated by another regional service, with public-administration agreements as needed.

The ordinance produces effects from adoption on 27 May 2025 and was later registered/published. Historical cases must determine whether the relevant legal effect attaches at adoption or a later registration/publication event for the specific proposition; the source states production of effects from adoption.

### CNR-IPSP institutional and seat-specific status

Section 7.2 of the 2022 national emergency plan identifies CREA-DC and CNR-IPSP as national reference laboratories for *Xylella* under Regulation 2017/625 Article 101.

The separate 2021 ministerial designation decree names CREA-DC facilities only. It does not designate a CNR-IPSP seat. Regional acts and judgments establish use of CNR-IPSP Bari in the regional diagnostic chain, but do not constitute a ministerial designation of that seat.

The institutional CNR-IPSP role is supported. A seat-specific ministerial designation for Bari remains unresolved only where an operative proposition depends on that narrower fact.

## DTU 39 revisions

DTU 39 revision 1 was approved by the national committee on 9–10 December 2024 and adopted on 7 January 2025. It is an official national technical document for *Xylella* survey and diagnosis.

### Legal role

The document supplies technical instructions to official survey personnel. Its explanatory taxonomy, symptom images and literature summaries support recognition and survey design. They are not independent coercive measures.

Its operative scope includes:

- risk-site identification;
- visual inspection;
- plant and vector sampling;
- trapping/capture methods;
- sample composition, timing, conservation and transfer;
- extraction;
- screening and subspecies methods;
- diagnostic result routing.

### Survey and sampling

The DTU confirms that visual inspection alone cannot establish absence because of latency, asymptomatic infection and nonspecific symptoms. Statistically sound, risk-based sampling and laboratory analysis are required.

RiPEST supports sample-number design using target population, epidemiological units, risk factors, sampling effectiveness and diagnostic sensitivity. It does not determine legal scope or sample distribution.

The source-native sampling instructions include:

- symptomatic and asymptomatic plant material;
- single or multiple samples under the referenced standard;
- mature leaves/ramets appropriate to host and season;
- year-round sampling where valid material supports detection;
- prompt laboratory transfer and protection from heat/vector escape;
- vector traps/capture methods and replacement/check frequencies.

The stated timing and counts are technical instructions at DTU scope. They do not become universal regional enforcement calendars outside the survey or plan that incorporates them.

### Diagnostic methods

The DTU incorporates the current EU Annex IV methods and maps them to EPPO appendices for screening and subspecies identification. It also references current versions of EPPO PM7/24, PM7/141 and ISPM DP25.

“Latest version” is a dynamic reference that requires exact point-in-time edition resolution. A later protocol cannot be projected backward merely because the current DTU uses that phrase.

### Confirmation routes

For pest-free-area surveys, a positive result requires national-reference-laboratory confirmation using a different genome target on the same sample or appropriate extract. Samples from already demarcated areas do not require that confirmation route under the DTU.

A technically positive result still requires a legally official sample/laboratory chain for official confirmation.

### Inconclusive results

The DTU requires repetition using the same method. It then routes:

- repeated positive → confirmation with another genome target;
- repeated negative → “not detected/absent” sample result;
- repeated inconclusive → resampling or repeat analysis where possible, with aliquot retention;
- a further inconclusive → sample considered “not detected/absent,” with the territorial service deciding whether further investigation is warranted.

This wording must be preserved exactly. “Not detected” is a diagnostic disposition. It does not establish global biological absence or automatically satisfy every legal no-presence predicate.

### Revision 0 interval

DTU 39 revision 0 was approved by the national committee on 21–22 June 2023 and adopted on 13 July 2023. It applies through 6 January 2025 and is superseded by revision 1 from 7 January 2025.

Its legal role is the same bounded technical role: instructions to official survey personnel for risk-site identification, visual inspection, plant and vector sampling, sample handling, and screening/subspecies diagnostic routing. It does not independently create a coercive measure.

It incorporates the then-current EU Annex IV diagnostic framework and referenced EPPO/ISPM standards. Dynamic references require the edition applicable at the relevant time; revision 1 is not projected backward.

## National pest-free status and PNI role

### Central Ordinance 3/2023

The central-service ordinance declares the Italian territories listed in its Annex I pest-free from *Xylella* and requires regional services to maintain that status under Regulation 2020/1201. The list is revised based on annual official survey results.

The recitals state that the national territory is pest-free except for demarcated areas in Puglia, Tuscany and Lazio as supported at that time by regional acts and official surveys.

Pest-free status is therefore versioned national authority. It is not inferred from absence of a public positive row. Exact demarcated-area acts and annual survey evidence determine exceptions.

Annex I lists the whole territories of Abruzzo, Basilicata, Calabria, Campania, Emilia-Romagna, Friuli-Venezia Giulia, Liguria, Lombardia, Marche, Molise, Piemonte, Sardegna, Sicilia, Umbria, Valle d’Aosta and Veneto, together with the Autonomous Provinces of Bolzano and Trento. Lazio, Puglia and Toscana are listed except for their demarcated areas as communicated by the regional services and published through the national and Commission sources.

The list is available in the official English courtesy translation. The authentic Italian Annex I text is not held. The translation closes list membership but does not replace authentic Italian wording where a linguistic issue arises.

### PNI 2026 workbook

The held workbook extract contains Puglia survey-design rows for orchards/vineyards, spontaneous vegetation and production sites, with target populations, risk strata, sensitivities, confidence/prevalence and planned sample counts.

The national committee approved the 2026 surveillance programme on 13 November 2025, and the programme was published through the National Plant Health Service portal. The workbooks are therefore committee-approved, published survey-design material for the 2026 programme.

A numbered Central Plant Health Service implementing instrument is not held. That gap is limited to the exact formal instrument identity and adoption route; it does not negate the approved workbook contents or permit those design rows to create legal area status, compliance or completeness claims by themselves.
