## Reading posture

The Puglia layer implements and specializes the EU and national mechanism for the regional service. It does not begin from one plan, crop, case, determination or reviewed artifact.

The reading proceeds from current regional legislation through plans, delegation, area state and recurring case procedure:

```text
EU substantive branch
→ Italian competence and procedure
→ Puglia statutory authority
→ effective regional plan/procedure
→ exact area state
→ case branch and genuine delta
```

A regional source receives only the weight its legal role and effective interval support.

## L.R. 14/2007 — monumental olive protection

### Monumental identity and listing

The law protects and values monumental olive trees and groves. Monumentality is established through source-defined age/diameter, historical-anthropological and exceptional form/symbolic/location criteria. A grove may qualify where at least 60% of plants in the cadastral cultivation unit are monumental.

The technical commission validates reports, advises listing and gives binding opinions on proposed removal or relocation of listed monumental trees. The regional list is proposed, published with cadastral details, open to owner opposition and then definitively approved and republished.

Monumental legal status is therefore not established by visual age, a GIS layer or case assertion alone. It requires the applicable listing/recognition route.

### Protection and ordinary derogation

Definitively listed monumental groves receive automatic landscape protection. Damage, felling, uprooting and trade of listed monumental trees are prohibited.

Ordinary derogation under Article 11 requires the stated legal grounds, a binding commission opinion, documented absence of alternatives and a complete replanting project. Natura 2000/protected-area procedures remain separate. Authorizations have a two-year validity.

The law also defines relocation/replanting procedure, responsible costs, destination constraints and specific authorization. These ordinary landscape-development routes are not automatically the phytosanitary emergency route.

### Interaction with plant-health emergencies

For an uninfected monumental/historic-interest plant inside a plant-health removal population, D.lgs. 19 Article 33 requires a case-specific phytosanitary decision and landscape authorization. L.R. 14 provides the regional monumental status and ordinary protection context.

For infected or retained-value plants under EU Article 7(3), Article 13(2) or Puglia L.R. 4, the applicable phytosanitary branch must be resolved first. L.R. 14 cannot independently exempt a plant from directly applicable EU removal law.

The following are distinct:

- monumental-tree status;
- EU protected-value retention eligibility;
- competent phytosanitary retention decision;
- landscape authorization for removal of an unconfirmed protected plant;
- ordinary non-phytosanitary uprooting/relocation derogation.

### Sanctions

L.R. 14 has its own sanctions for violations of monumental-tree protection. Those sanctions do not replace D.lgs. 19 sanctions for failure to execute plant-health measures. The governing violation determines the sanction route.

## L.R. 4/2017 — regional Xylella mechanism

### Reporting, suspicion and confirmation

Anyone aware of *Xylella* presence informs the regional plant-health service immediately and supplies relevant information. The regional service records and immediately informs the central service.

On actual or presumed presence, the regional service takes measures needed to verify the report and informs controllers of potentially affected plants about presence/suspicion, consequences, risks and measures.

This regional statute supplements the national Article 28 sequence. A regional report/presumption is not an official plant finding until the official diagnostic route is complete.

### Area lifecycle

After confirmed presence, the regional service defines a demarcated area without delay. It comprises infected and buffer zones. A confirmed finding in the buffer causes immediate modification. The area may be revoked where investigations support eradication and no further-spread risk under current law.

The statute establishes the regional service as area decision owner. Exact area versions still require the constitutive and amending acts, source-native geometry and effective intervals.

### Regional survey process

The regional service conducts continuing official surveys under an annual regional monitoring plan. It uses current scientific/technical evidence, host/specified plant definitions and risk factors.

The plan may be implemented directly or by delegation. Authorized personnel may access land even without the owner/holder. Samples are taken at appropriate times and analysed only by laboratories identified by the regional service.

Annual area surveys must be statistically sound and risk-based. Pest-free areas focus on introduction risk. Buffer and pest-free areas also monitor confirmed/potential vectors. Positive-vector findings lead to sampling of specified plants at the finding location.

Monthly communication to the central service includes inspected sites/coordinates, samples/species/results and monitoring maps.

These are recurring survey, evidence and reporting duties. They are not a single case process.

### Eradication

The regional service orders and controls immediate removal of infected plants and other susceptible populations required by current law. Removal must follow risk and vector-cycle precautions. Where timely removal is impossible, the law describes interim isolation, pruning and vector-control measures.

Article 5’s current cross-reference to a lettered part of Article 3(2) no longer resolves in the coordinated text. The substantive eradication authority must be read with current EU/national law; the broken cross-reference remains a legal-text defect rather than being silently repaired by a guessed target.

### Containment

Containment may be used only where Union law permits it. In such areas the regional service removes infected plants found through the regional/EU monitoring process and samples contiguous specified plants.

The phrase “where Union law permits” preserves EU Article 12 and Annex III as higher availability conditions. Regional law does not make containment generally available.

### Destruction and vector measures

The regional service determines destruction/removal location and prescribes agricultural practices for pest/vector management. It must communicate those measures widely.

Vector measures are continuing territorial work. They are not implied complete merely because a removal case closes.

### Monumental and protected plants

The law preserves several distinct branches:

- noninfected listed monumental trees are not removed where current law’s conditions hold;
- infected monumental trees may receive alternative measures only where current law permits and after Osservatorio authorization;
- the Piana degli olivi secolari receives monitoring;
- buffer-zone monumental trees may receive promoted protective measures;
- the regional service's planting-request route is retained as deferred Article 18 source evidence only.

Every branch is explicitly subordinate to current higher law. “Sustain vitality by every means” and alternative pruning/grafting language cannot override mandatory EU removal when no derogation is available.

### Deferred Article 18 source evidence

The regional service monitors scientific evidence of resistance/tolerance and
promotes planting requests to the national committee. This source fact is
outside the Articles 1–17 implementation aperture. It creates no current
authorization proposition, workflow or downstream completion requirement.

### Research and movement boundaries

Research with live/infected material requires advance communication and result reporting. Any additional authorizations under national/Union law remain necessary.

Plant movement is permitted only under national and Union law. The movement provisions remain outside the complete outbreak-response transaction program except where they define a boundary or official check.

### Out-of-aperture economic consequences

The statute also contains benefits, compensation, funding and economic-restoration provisions. Those do not enter this Stage A aperture. Their presence does not change the meaning of the admitted phytosanitary clauses.

## First Puglia integration findings

1. The regional service/Osservatorio owns territorial surveys, confirmation process, area decisions, measure prescriptions, implementation oversight and violations under the national allocation.
2. Exact area-state acts are necessary; current statutes provide authority but not each effective geometry.
3. Containment remains conditional on EU Article 12 and Annex III.
4. Monumental status, retention eligibility, removal authorization and landscape authorization are separate.
5. Recurring surveillance, vector control and public communication cannot disappear behind removal-case links.
6. Regional plans and determinations must implement these statutes without becoming the definition of the whole regional universe.

## Regional organization and execution support

### L.R. 3/2010 and L.R. 19/2019

L.R. 3/2010 establishes ARIF as a regional instrumental public-law technical/operational entity.

L.R. 19/2019 inserts Article 3-bis and assigns ARIF additional functions **in support of the regional plant-health Osservatorio**. The admitted phytosanitary support function covers monitoring, prevention, removal and communication for Xylella management.

This does not transfer the Osservatorio’s statutory authority to:

- confirm findings;
- establish or revoke areas;
- select eradication/containment;
- prescribe measures;
- decide derogations;
- establish violations;
- authorize planting or retention.

Those remain with the competent plant-health service unless another exact source lawfully reallocates a specific decision.

The same amendment also assigns financial/benefit and productive-restoration functions. Those are outside this Stage A aperture and do not enlarge ARIF’s phytosanitary decision ownership.

L.R. 19/2019 also repeals former L.R. 4 Article 11 and its ARXIA route. Current implementation must not revive that superseded actor.

### DPGR 22/2021 Article 20

Section managers own and are responsible for their organizational structures, adopt acts, ensure technical-administrative legality, direct subordinate work and manage assigned resources.

Manager acts are published electronically for ten working days and archived. The source states explicitly:

> publication does not remove the obligation to notify interested persons.

Therefore:

```text
act publication
≠ individual/mass notice effectiveness by itself
```

The applicable L. 241 Article 21-bis route and act-specific notice clause still control legal effect against recipients.

### L.R. 6/2021 Article 3

The current L.R. 4 Article 7(3) exclusion from regional benefits and procurement applies only to noncompliance with Article 7(1)’s destruction/removal prescription. It is not a general consequence of every Xylella obligation.

That economic consequence remains outside the current aid-excluded aperture. Its narrow statutory trigger nevertheless prevents it from being misused as general phytosanitary enforcement.

## DGR 1075/2025 — current Puglia Action Plan 2025–2027

DGR 1075 approves the 2025–2027 plan, replaces the prior ARIF convention and authorizes the Osservatorio to modify the plan in response to surveillance, risk or legal change. The deliberation is dated 29 July 2025 and published in BURP on 25 August 2025.

The plan is the current regional operating plan in the selected source universe. It does not replace the exact determinations that create areas, prescribe work or authorize planting.

### Authority and governance

The plan identifies the Osservatorio as the competent regional authority under D.lgs. 19 Article 6 and Regulation 2017/625. ARIF supports and performs delegated official activities. InnovaPuglia supplies technical/data support. Official laboratories provide designated diagnostic work.

The attached convention preserves the decision/execution split:

- the Osservatorio defines annual survey plans, laboratories, priorities, prescriptions, methods and second-level controls;
- ARIF conducts delegated surveillance/vector work, supervises or procures removals and reports execution;
- ARIF may perform substitute/removal work only under the applicable prescription, noncompliance route and delegation;
- the Osservatorio retains measure, violation and delegation oversight;
- delegation may be revoked for inadequate execution or compromised independence/impartiality.

ARIF’s delegated request to the Prefect must be reconciled with D.lgs. Article 33’s statement that the competent territorial plant-health service may request public-force assistance. Delegation cannot be assumed to include a reserved statutory power without exact legal support.

### Area-state chain

The plan lists the current-at-adoption areas for pauca ST53, fastidiosa ST1 and multiplex ST26 and cites the constitutive/updating determinations. This proves that area state is a temporal chain of acts, not one generic ledger.

The plan itself contains mixed states:

- existing areas;
- recently established areas;
- an area described as still in the course of institution;
- historical area updates recited as context;
- summary maps.

Therefore its area tables/maps cannot be used as one constitutive snapshot without the exact effective determination for each area.

The plan states that regional DGR 18/2024 expanded the pauca containment area before M4 later added the named municipalities to Annex III. EU Article 12 availability before M4’s effective date requires separate adjudication; a regional act could not itself amend Annex III.

### Internal geography conflict

The glossary defines the containment zone as at least 2.5 km, while the plan’s recitals and operative surveillance sections apply M5’s 2 km edge band and cite the regional DGR 158/2024 update.

This is an internal source conflict:

```text
2.5 km glossary
vs.
2 km operative sections/current EU law
```

The glossary cannot silently govern. The operative provision, higher law, amendment history and area act must be adjudicated.

### Survey design

The plan creates a detailed annual regional survey design for:

- the pest-free area;
- pauca containment infected zone and buffer;
- fastidiosa and multiplex eradication areas;
- newly identified risk corridors and one-kilometre external bands;
- crop/vegetation populations and risk strata.

The source-native tables preserve target populations, epidemiological units, confidence/prevalence, relative-risk allocation and planned samples. The tables must remain coherent designs. They must not become one legal atom per cell.

The plan also reserves 30% of annual samples for new findings. That is a regional design decision, not a Union statutory percentage.

### Exact area links

Each survey design names the relevant area/subspecies and relies on the cited area acts. A case or survey result cannot resolve correctly through a generic `area-state-versions` label.

The semantic requirement is:

```text
survey/case date
+ applicable subspecies
+ exact state_version_id created by the effective regional act
+ route-specific plan design
```

This confirms that zero exact case-to-area links is a substantive topology defect.

### Regional Annex II narrowing claim

The plan states that citrus, peach, apricot and plum are not susceptible to pauca ST53 based on regional/experimental evidence and therefore are no longer surveilled or subject to eradication, despite their inclusion in Annex II for pauca.

The plan repeats this exclusion in the 50 m containment follow-up.

EU Annex II defines specified plants by subspecies, while M5 Article 7 provides a bounded two-year no-infection route that still requires annual Article 10 surveys. The regional plan’s complete surveillance/removal exclusion is therefore not accepted on assertion.

It requires authority adjudication of:

- exact taxon and subspecies/genotype distinction;
- Article 7(1)(e) and its two-year route;
- continuing Article 10/15 survey duties;
- Member State authority to adopt a supported stricter/narrower population interpretation;
- whether a regional plan can override the effective Annex II population.

Until resolved:

```text
regional ST53 exclusion = AUTHORITY_INTERPRETATION_REQUIRED
```

### Positive-finding surveillance

For pest-free/buffer findings, the plan combines:

- the 50 m infected-zone removal population;
- 400 m high-risk survey;
- iterative outward survey to define the area;
- later statistical design.

These are sequentially distinct. The 400 m survey does not itself expand the infected-zone removal population.

For pauca containment findings, the plan requires 50 m sampling/testing of specified plants, subject to the unresolved regional exclusion above.

### Eradication

The plan applies current Article 7 removal populations and protected-value derogations. It prioritizes infected plants but retains the complete source populations.

It also creates crop-specific routes:

- voluntary vineyard removal within 400 m outside the infected-zone perimeter;
- mandatory ARIF removal of marginal/abandoned almond trees within 400 m.

These are not self-evident EU Article 7 consequences. Article 7 removal operates in the infected zone. The plan’s 400 m crop routes combine crop, distance, condition, modality and compensation context.

They therefore require independent authority adjudication rather than synthetic keys such as `VINEYARD_VOLUNTARY_400M` or `ALMOND_MANDATORY_400M`.

Questions include:

- whether the plants lie inside or outside the legal infected zone;
- whether the measure is voluntary, mandatory, precautionary or compensation-linked;
- which competent authority adopted the extension;
- what evidence defines “marginal or abandoned”;
- whether the measure is authorized by EU/national law or exceeds the regional plan’s competence.

### Containment and destruction

Containment removal follows official identification and vector-flight timing. Destruction, retained wood, difficult-root authorization and devitalization remain distinct.

The plan’s statement that leafless/branchless wood may move is subject to exact source and current movement boundary. The notification/tarpaulin/road-control process is separate from the Article 9/16 destruction decision.

### Notice and owner execution

The plan sets a detailed procedure:

1. InnovaPuglia supplies cadastral/owner/orthophoto data within seven working days.
2. The Osservatorio issues the removal prescription.
3. The act is published for seven days and sent/published through named channels.
4. Municipalities attempt individual notice where possible.
5. The owner elects voluntary or ARIF execution during publication or within three days after it ends.
6. Voluntary or ARIF execution follows within ten days.
7. No response after the election period routes to ARIF removal.
8. Refusal routes to forced removal, Prefect/law-enforcement information, cost charging and violation contest.

This is a reusable procedure, not a case label.

The legal effectiveness of seven-day publication remains subject to Law 241 Article 21-bis and DPGR Article 20. The plan’s assertion that publication constitutes notice must be supported by the recipient-volume/impossibility or immediate-effect route in the prescribing act.

Silence becomes proven default only where valid notice, completed publication and deadline evidence exist. Missing response data cannot itself prove silence.

### Monumental/Piana route

The plan states that Piana owners may either remove or adopt capitozzatura/grafting alternatives. L.R. 4 permits alternatives only where current law allows and after Osservatorio authorization.

The plan’s broad owner-choice wording cannot replace:

- exact monumental status;
- EU retention/planting eligibility;
- competent authorization;
- continuing testing/vector duties;
- case-specific evidence.

### Vector work

ARIF monitors vectors under Osservatorio coordination. The Osservatorio uses monitoring to issue timing through acts/circulars for homogeneous areas.

The plan distinguishes:

- recurring juvenile control;
- alternative physical/chemical methods where mechanical access is difficult/impossible;
- adult treatment;
- exact territorial obligations;
- exclusions;
- recommendations.

The plan does not create one fixed annual date. Timing is completed by later competent acts/circulars.

Aerial imagery may support control of juvenile-measure execution. It is not conclusive legal evidence by itself.

### Planting and grafting

The plan interprets Article 18 as applying to new productive planting and exempts certain intra-area relocations for public utility/land improvement because risk is said to remain unchanged.

It then authorizes named crops/varieties in the pauca infected zone outside containment, including resistant/tolerant olives and species described as immune, low-susceptibility or low-frequency infection.

These propositions require exact authority adjudication. Article 18 defines specific authorization routes; a regional risk-equivalence statement does not automatically create another route. The current authority must be traced through the plan and exact planting determinations, including DDS 48/2024 and later amendments.

### Official laboratories

The plan lists laboratories described as designated and either accredited or still undergoing accreditation. Ordinance 10/2025 requires official-control plant samples to be analysed exclusively by accredited, designated official laboratories.

Any “in-course accreditation” laboratory’s official-result authority therefore requires exact designation, accreditation and event-time scope. The plan’s list is not sufficient by itself.

### Sanctions and communication

The plan applies D.lgs. Article 55(15) to nonexecution of mandatory measures and uses the Law 689 procedure. Carabinieri support controls under a separate agreement.

The portal, WebGIS and dashboards provide public communication, evidence and assurance. They do not create legal areas, findings, duties or completion merely by displaying data.

## DGR 1593/2024 — immediate predecessor plan

DGR 1593 approves the 2024–2026 plan on 25 November 2024 and publishes it in BURP on 9 December 2024. It replaces the 2023 ARIF convention and is itself modified/replaced by DGR 1075/2025.

### Legal transition

The plan responds to the 2024 discovery of fastidiosa ST1, multiplex ST26 and additional pauca ST53 findings. It is the first held Puglia action plan that expressly incorporates M4 and M5 into a multi-subspecies regional mechanism.

The national committee approved a plan on 15 July 2024, before M5 was adopted in September. The final DGR plan cites and implements M5 and was further updated for later 2024 surveillance. No dated post-M5, post-DDS-158 committee minutes or central-service act establish approval of the materially updated final plan. A recital that the plan was “shared” does not establish approval under D.lgs. 19 Article 31.

This is a bounded adoption-authority question, not a reason to reject every regional measure in the plan.

### Mechanism continuity into DGR 1075

DGR 1593 already establishes the reusable process later carried into DGR 1075:

- multi-subspecies area and survey design;
- 400 m high-risk survey outside 50 m infected zones;
- seven-day publication as the claimed notice route;
- three-day owner election after publication;
- ten-day voluntary/ARIF execution;
- ARIF default execution after silence and forced execution after refusal;
- regional vector monitoring and timing circulars;
- current Article 18 crop/variety list;
- official-control, sanction and communication routes;
- ARIF delegation with the Osservatorio retaining prescriptions and oversight.

DGR 1075 changes the current area population, survey parameters, funding horizon, implementation interfaces and some crop-specific measures. It does not originate the core notice/execution/governance process.

### Area-state interval

At DGR 1593 adoption, the held plan summarizes:

- pauca Annex III containment with a 2 km operative edge after DDS 158/2024;
- a separate pauca Bari eradication area;
- fastidiosa ST1 eradication area;
- multiplex ST26 eradication areas for Santeramo, Capurso and Noicattaro/Triggiano;
- Ginosa as still being constituted/updated across the Puglia–Basilicata boundary.

The plan’s summaries remain secondary to each exact area act. The source itself shows that some areas are complete legal states and others are in-progress survey/coordination states.

### 2 km / 2.5 km conflict begins here

DGR 1593’s operative surveillance section uses M5’s 2 km containment edge. Its glossary still says 2.5 km. DGR 1075 carries the same conflict forward.

This is not a new 2025 defect. The source conflict begins with the 2024 plan and must be resolved by rank, scope and operative context.

### ST53 population exclusion begins here

DGR 1593 introduces the claim that citrus, peach, apricot and plum are no longer surveyed or subject to eradication for pauca ST53 despite Annex II. It also excludes them from the 50 m containment sampling population.

DGR 1075 repeats this without adding a new authority basis. The relevant interpretation therefore begins no later than 9 December 2024 and remains unresolved for the full interval.

### Notice and execution interval

DGR 1593’s notice procedure uses seven-day municipal-albo publication and related public channels. It does not yet add DGR 1075’s instruction that municipalities attempt individual notice where possible or the SPID election portal.

Its owner/ARIF route is otherwise materially the same:

```text
valid prescription and claimed publication notice
→ owner choice through three days after publication
→ ten-day owner or ARIF execution
→ silence routes to ARIF
→ refusal routes to forced execution, costs and sanction contest
```

The same Law 241/DPGR legal-effect question therefore governs both plan periods.

### Monumental-tree route

The 2024 plan directly applies EU Article 7(3) to officially recognized, negative monumental olives. It does not yet include DGR 1075’s interim recognition workflow for visually qualifying but unlisted olives.

The Piana alternative capitozzatura/grafting route remains broadly worded and requires exact authorization under L.R. 4 and higher law.

### Planting

DGR 1593 already contains the Article 18 interpretation and named crop/variety authorizations later repeated in DGR 1075. The exact planting determination remains necessary to establish authority, route and geography.

### Crop-specific 400 m routes

DGR 1593 does **not** contain:

- voluntary vineyard eradication across a 400 m extension;
- mandatory removal of marginal or abandoned almond trees across a 400 m extension.

Those routes enter after DGR 1593 and therefore cannot be applied to earlier events through the plan’s general 400 m survey language. Survey extent is not removal authority.

### Vector measures

The 2024 plan establishes the plan-plus-later-circular pattern for juvenile and adult measures. Exact timing and additional municipalities depend on the later act/circular.

The plan’s categorical November–March no-treatment statement remains subject to monitoring evidence and M5’s actual flight-season language. It cannot be treated as a fixed biological truth for every locality and year.

### Laboratory authority

DGR 1593 already lists laboratories that are accredited or still undergoing accreditation. Its adoption predates Ordinance 10/2025, but Regulation 2017/625 and the 2022 laboratory decree still require valid official designation/scope. DGR 1075 later inherits a stricter direct conflict after Ordinance 10 takes effect.

### Delegation

The 2024 convention already assigns ARIF surveillance, vector support, prescribed removal, substitute execution and evidence duties. The Osservatorio retains plan design, laboratories, prescriptions, second-level control and delegation revocation.

ARIF’s stated ability to request Prefect support remains a reserved-power question in both plans.

## DGR 1866/2022 and DGR 570/2023 — 2023–2025 plan state

DGR 1866 approves the 2023–2024 plan on 12 December 2022 and publishes it on 27 December 2022. The national committee approved the plan on 29 November 2022.

DGR 570, adopted 26 April 2023 and published 19 June 2023, extends the plan horizon through 2025 and approves the ARIF and Carabinieri agreements. Its integrated plan text is substantively the DGR 1866 plan.

### Area-state mechanism

The plan operates with:

- Salento Annex III containment and a 5 km containment edge;
- Valle d’Itria eradication area with a 5 km regional buffer;
- the revoked Canosa area under post-revocation surveillance;
- exact updating through DDS 127/2022 and later determinations.

The Valle d’Itria 5 km eradication buffer exceeds the then-current EU 2.5 km minimum. It is a potentially valid stricter adopted regional geometry, not an error merely because it is larger.

### Survey design

The plan uses:

- two successive 400 m risk bands outside the 50 m infected zone;
- high, medium and low risk values;
- total survey of selected Valle d’Itria populations;
- two-step RiBESS+ design;
- 7 or 14 samples per hectare by risk;
- annual Osservatorio determinations to complete the sampled surfaces.

These bands are survey design. They do not create a removal population beyond the infected zone.

### ST53 crop exclusion starts by 2022

DGR 1866 states that citrus, peach, apricot and plum are not surveyed or removed for pauca ST53 despite Annex II. It applies the exclusion in eradication and containment survey/removal routes.

DGR 570 republishes the same rule. DGR 1593 and DGR 1075 later retain it.

The unresolved authority interval therefore begins no later than 27 December 2022. A later plan’s repetition does not renew or cure the authority basis.

### Monumental eradication decision

The 2023–2025 plan expressly decides **not** to apply EU Article 7(3)’s historic-value derogation to officially recognized, negative monumental olives in eradication areas. The source explains that retained olives became infected quickly and acted as inoculum.

Article 7(3) permits—but does not require—the Member State to retain qualifying plants. A competent decision not to exercise the derogation is therefore legally different from finding the plant ineligible.

DGR 1593 later reverses this policy and applies Article 7(3). The temporal model must preserve:

```text
2023–2024 plan: derogation not exercised
→ 2024–2026 plan: derogation available/applied
```

The exact transition date follows the effective plan acts, not a generic monumental-tree key.

### Piana alternative route

The plan surveys a regional 5–20 km band with focus on the Piana. It offers infected plants either removal or capitozzatura/grafting alternatives with interim isolation and vector controls.

EU Article 13 requires infected-plant removal in containment without an equivalent Article 7(3) derogation. The regional alternative is therefore an authority conflict where it applies to Article 13 infected plants.

It cannot be generalized as a lawful reusable route without resolving the exact geography, branch, higher-law authority and regional authorization.

### Notice and owner execution

The plan establishes the same reusable procedure later carried forward:

- three working days for owner/cadastral data;
- seven working days for the Osservatorio prescription;
- seven days of municipal-albo publication claimed as notice;
- owner election through three days after publication;
- ten-day owner or ARIF execution;
- silence routes to ARIF;
- refusal routes to forced execution, cost charging and sanction contest.

This is the earliest held plan in the current reverse-temporal reading that establishes this exact process. The same Law 241/DPGR validity issue applies from this interval onward.

### Vector measures

The plan prescribes altitude-based monthly windows and municipality lists for juvenile measures, then leaves annual refinement to the Osservatorio. Adult treatment timing and product lists depend on monitoring and later communications.

Exact annual acts can modify the plan’s general schedule. A plan-year calendar cannot be reused across campaigns without the effective implementing act.

### Planting

The 2023–2025 plan:

- treats Article 18 as limited to new productive planting;
- treats monumental grafting and intra-area relocations as outside the prohibition;
- authorizes named “immune” species in Valle d’Itria under pre-M5 Article 18(c);
- authorizes resistant/tolerant/low-susceptibility species in Salento outside containment;
- directs removal and planting prohibition for specified plants at high-risk service stations.

These are regional interpretations and authorizations, not direct readings of Article 18. Each requires the exact pre-M5 route conditions, area and evidence. The plan’s “risk unchanged” or “immune” assertion cannot itself create an unlisted legal route.

### Experimental inputs

Civic vector monitoring, trained dogs and remote sensing are experimental/supporting tools. Their outputs identify survey priorities or suspected sites. They do not create official findings or legal areas.

### DGR 570 ARIF delegation

The 2023–2025 ARIF convention delegates:

- survey and sample delivery;
- execution of prescribed eradication/containment measures;
- substitute removal against noncompliant owners;
- communication and execution evidence.

The Osservatorio retains annual plan/manual design, laboratory selection, prescriptions, second-level control and delegation revocation.

The convention states that ARIF may request Prefect assistance. As in later conventions, the statutory reserved-power question remains.

### DGR 570 Carabinieri agreement

The agreement assigns Carabinieri Forestali control support for:

- movement of specified plants and wood;
- juvenile vector-control measures;
- adult vector-control measures;
- nursery checks.

It provides historical control windows:

- juvenile controls generally May–June, subject to Osservatorio adjustment;
- adult controls 1 August–30 November;
- spatial priorities based on then-current areas.

It defines evidence routes:

- farm treatment register and authorization documentation for farms;
- contractor invoice for nonprofessional owners;
- inspection/constatation record under Law 689.

Carabinieri may prepare violation/contest records and carry the sanction file through service of the contest. This does not make them the regional sanction-decision owner or collapse contest into final injunction.

### Genuine transition to DGR 1593

DGR 1593 changes the regional mechanism by:

1. replacing Salento/Valle d’Itria with a multi-subspecies area system;
2. reducing the operative containment edge from 5 km to 2 km after M5;
3. replacing the earlier risk-band/sample design with new subspecies-specific tables;
4. reversing the refusal to exercise Article 7(3) for negative monumental olives;
5. removing the fixed 5–20 km Piana survey framing while retaining a broad alternative-measure claim;
6. updating planting lists and areas;
7. replacing annual fixed municipality/product tables with a more dynamic circular-based scheme;
8. replacing the 2023 agreements/convention with the 2024 convention.

## DGR 343/2022 — 2022 plan

DGR 343 adopts the 2022 plan on 14 March 2022 and publishes it on 28 March 2022. The national committee approved it on 20 December 2021.

### Area-state structure

The plan operates with separate Salento containment, Monopoli, Polignano and Alberobello eradication areas. Canosa is revoked and subject to post-revocation surveillance.

It uses exact current area acts and separate area-specific survey designs. The plan’s map is a reporting surface, not a substitute for those acts.

### Survey design

The plan uses:

- 400 m high-risk and additional 400 m medium-risk bands;
- regional high/medium/base risk classes;
- two-step RiBESS+;
- source-native population, epidemiological-unit, confidence, prevalence and sample-allocation tables;
- separate pest-free subzones, including Canosa post-revocation surveillance;
- reinforced nursery sampling inside the revoked Canosa site.

Again, these are survey populations. They do not expand Article 7 removal beyond the infected zone.

### No ST53 crop exclusion in the base 2022 plan

DGR 343 applies survey/removal to all specified plants. It does not exclude citrus, peach, apricot or plum from pauca ST53 surveillance or eradication.

The later exclusion does not originate in the nationally approved base plan. DDS 67/2022 does not create it either.

DDS 67/2022 supplements the 2022 plan with three high-risk surveillance areas:

- the complete municipal territories of Polignano a Mare, Monopoli and Alberobello;
- 100 m on both sides around high-vehicle-throughput and stopping sites identified by ARIF in those municipalities and along major roads;
- a 400 m band north of the containment zone from the Ionian to the Adriatic.

ARIF must identify the relevant sites, inspect and sample specified plants with particular attention to olive, and survey the remaining agricultural grids at seven samples per hectare plus symptomatic single-plant samples. This surveillance takes priority over the southern 400 m band and the monumental-olive plain beyond containment.

DDS 67 changes surveillance geography and priority. It does not change the Article 7 removal population or create the later ST53 crop exclusion.

### Monumental-tree choices

The plan exercises Article 7(3) for qualifying negative historic/monumental olives in eradication areas, subject to annual testing and all-stage vector treatments.

It expressly refuses the plan-described Article 13(2) derogation for infected monumental/qualifying olives in containment.

This is the opposite of the later 2023–2025 eradication choice, which refuses Article 7(3). The regional discretion changes through time and must remain distinct from plant eligibility.

### Notice and execution

The 2022 plan already contains the reusable seven-day publication/election/default process.

Its initial clocks are:

- owner/cadastral data within three working days;
- Osservatorio prescription within five working days;
- seven-day publication;
- owner election through three days after publication;
- owner or ARIF removal within ten days;
- silence routes to ARIF;
- refusal routes to forced removal, costs and violation contest.

DGR 1866 later changes the prescription clock from five to seven working days while retaining the remaining structure.

### Piana route

The plan surveys the regional 5–20 km band and permits infected plants to receive either removal or authorized capitozzatura/grafting alternatives.

This route conflicts with mandatory higher-law infected-plant removal wherever the selected EU branch provides no derogation. It remains an unresolved regional authority proposition, not a generally valid monumental-tree family.

### Vector measures

The plan fixes juvenile windows by altitude and requires two adult treatments separated by about 20–25 days, with monitoring-based timing communications.

DGR 1866 later replaces those exact fixed ranges with broader month-level guidance and a dynamic number of adult treatments. Therefore, 2022 campaign clocks cannot be carried into 2023 or later without the annual implementing act.

### Planting and service stations

The plan includes a planting prohibition for specified plants at service/rest areas in pest-free and buffer zones. It also contains pre-M5 planting/grafting interpretations completed by regional acts.

These remain distinct from later 400 m vineyard/almond removal routes. Service-station risk does not create a crop-wide 400 m removal rule.

### Official evidence and experiments

The plan’s official laboratory list predates the 12 April 2022 laboratory decree’s effective operation and must be resolved at event time.

Dog and remote-sensing experiments generate survey leads. A remote signal routes to official inspection and sampling; it does not create a finding.

## DDS 45/2025 — current surveillance-to-removal procedure

UOR 181 determination 45/2025 adopts the current operational procedure on 26 March 2025 and declares it immediately executable. It replaces DDS 31/2022.

### Reserved authority and delegated work

ARIF uses qualified, nationally appointed plant-health assistants. Assistants may perform official activities and propose a measure or sanction, but cannot prescribe official measures or issue phytosanitary certificates.

The source therefore confirms:

```text
field observation / assistant proposal
≠ Osservatorio prescription
```

Only the competent Osservatorio establishes/updates areas and prescribes removal or other legal measures.

### Survey work allocation

The procedure defines:

- two-person field teams;
- unique tablets and team identifiers;
- assigned macro-grids and one-hectare grids;
- daily ARIF organization and Osservatorio read/oversight;
- specific teams by area for funding/accounting traceability;
- advance municipal/Prefect/stakeholder communication;
- App Xylella field identity and chain of custody.

These are operational controls. They do not create legal areas or case status.

### Grid overlap rule

A grid crossing two demarcated areas is operationally assigned to the area containing the larger surface. A grid crossing pest-free and demarcated territory is assigned to the demarcated area.

This rule routes survey work. It does **not** change the legal regime of every parcel or plant inside the grid. Exact signed area geometry still controls legal applicability.

### Sampling and chain of custody

The procedure defines:

- visual inspection of every assigned grid;
- single symptomatic and same-species pooled asymptomatic samples;
- special vineyard and consociation pooling;
- pool sizes of seven or four plants by species;
- annual single sampling of retained monumental olives;
- exact App fields, photographs and plant geolocation;
- host/season-specific tissue selection;
- sealed bags, cool storage and same-day laboratory delivery;
- digitally signed transfer records from field team through laboratory.

Unsealed samples are rejected and reported. Sample acceptance, test result and official finding remain distinct.

### Current regional crop exclusions

DDS 45 directs that:

- containment-zone fruit grids do not sample citrus, peach, apricot or plum;
- asparagus is never sampled;
- pauca eradication does not remove citrus, peach, apricot or plum;
- multiplex eradication does not remove olive.

These rules go beyond the plan’s original ST53 exclusion. They remain `AUTHORITY_INTERPRETATION_REQUIRED` against effective Annex II, exact subspecies populations and continuing EU survey/removal duties.

The procedure’s factual susceptibility list cannot itself amend Union Annex II.

### Positive-finding survey

For containment findings, the procedure samples all specified/suspected plants within 50 m and surveys specified-population grids for another 400 m.

For pest-free/buffer findings, it samples monumental/qualifying olives within 50 m and all specified-population grids for 400 m.

The stated 50 m monumental sampling population is not the complete Article 7 removal population. It supports the protected-tree branch. The later removal section supplies the full a–e population.

### Laboratory status

DDS 45 lists screening and CNR confirmation/subspecies roles and allows laboratories accredited or still undergoing accreditation. It takes effect before Ordinance 10/2025 on 27 May 2025.

From Ordinance 10’s effective proposition onward, official-control plant samples require an accredited, designated official laboratory. A laboratory described only as “in accreditation” cannot be assumed valid for later official findings.

DDS 45 states that the 24 January 2022 national plan identifies CNR-IPSP as a national reference laboratory. The national plan supports the institutional role but does not identify the Bari seat; seat-specific ministerial designation remains separate.

### Regional diagnostic interpretation

The procedure standardizes Harper real-time PCR and defines:

- Cq < 32 → positive;
- Cq > 32 and < 35 → doubtful;
- Cq > 35/no value → negative.

A doubtful result is re-extracted and repeated. A doubtful pool is decomposed. A persistently doubtful single sample goes to CNR aliquot testing; if an aliquot remains doubtful, the result is reported and the plant must be resampled.

DTU 39 revision 1 instead contains a route where repeated/further inconclusive results may be considered “not detected/absent,” with further investigation left to the service.

The later, Xylella-specific regional procedure requires resampling for its defined doubtful route. The two sources must be adjudicated by exact result type, method, authority, scope and time. “Doubtful,” “inconclusive” and “not detected” cannot be flattened.

### Positive-result routing

Positive pools are decomposed to identify individual plants. Containment/buffer positives go to CNR for Dupas subspecies identification. Pest-free positives use Dupas and MLST. Results carry sample, area and municipality identity and have regional reporting clocks.

The EU pest-free confirmation requirement still requires a second positive molecular test targeting a different genome region. The regional workflow must be checked to ensure its Dupas/MLST sequence satisfies confirmation before the regional service treats the plant finding as official.

### Regional analysis clocks

DDS 45 states:

- positive results within seven working days of receipt;
- negative results within ten working days;
- CNR characterization within three working days of receipt;
- vector reports within five working days.

These are regional procedure clocks. They do not replace the later Article 29 regional/central notification clocks after official confirmation.

### Area act and operational GIS

The Osservatorio creates or updates an area by managerial act based on monitoring and laboratory evidence. Only after that act does InnovaPuglia transmit shapefiles for operational systems.

Therefore:

```text
managerial area act
→ signed/adopted legal geometry
→ operational shapefile/App update
```

The GIS does not create the area.

### Removal procedure

For eradication, the procedure requires:

1. authorized vector treatment;
2. 48-hour wait;
3. field verification/geolocation of all plants in the 50 m removal population;
4. removal of Article 7(1)(a)–(e) populations;
5. root removal or bounded difficult-site devitalization;
6. retained negative monumental-tree branch;
7. execution record with plant/species/surface and owner countersignature.

It then inserts the unresolved crop exclusions described above.

For containment, it treats infected plants, vector treatment, 48-hour wait, removal/destruction and execution evidence. Its citation to Article 15 for containment removal is wrong: Article 13 governs infected-plant removal; Article 15 governs surveillance. The semantic effect must remain attached to Article 13.

### Owner/ARIF execution and force

The owner may execute directly or use ARIF. ARIF schedules and informs the Osservatorio, Prefect and mayor. Denied access or obstruction routes to documented notice, Prefect/public-force request, forced removal, cost recovery and Osservatorio violation contest.

This procedure does not itself prove valid service or owner default. The current plan’s notice/election process and Law 241 remain prerequisites.

### Superseded procedure gap

DDS 45 supersedes UOR 181 determination 31 of 13 May 2022. The earlier protocol body is not held. Historical procedural meaning from 13 May 2022 through 25 March 2025 therefore remains:

```text
DDS 31/2022 = SOURCE_REQUIRED
```

TAR judgments 387/2022 and 388/2022 predate DDS 31 and cite DDS 289/2016 rather than DDS 31. They do not supply the superseded protocol. Later case acts may prove repeated clauses but cannot supply every unseen exception.

## Deferred Article 18 evidence and in-scope 400 m crop measures

### Deferred source evidence — DDS 48/2024 planting authorization

UOR 181 determination 48/2024 takes effect on 3 May 2024 and is published on 9
May 2024. It is retained only as deferred Article 18 source evidence and does
not enter the implemented regional forest.

For deferred reference, the source establishes an authority sequence:

```text
regional monitoring/scientific evidence
→ Osservatorio submission to central service
→ national committee evaluation
→ national service shared authorization
→ Osservatorio determination
```

This is stronger than the plan’s unsupported assertion alone.

#### Authorized geography

DDS 48 authorizes planting in the pauca infected zone defined by DDS 18/2024, excluding the containment edge where the planting prohibition remains.

DDS 75/2021 is the earlier complete authorization under pre-M5 Article 18(b). It authorizes Leccino and FS17 olive, citrus, peach, plum, apricot, almond and cherry in the pauca infected zone outside the then-5 km containment area. It rests on the favorable national-committee position communicated by the Ministry on 16 July 2021 and requires plants with compliant plant passports issued by authorized professional operators.

It confirms the earlier DDS 75/2021 authorizations for:

- Leccino and FS17 olives;
- citrus;
- peach, plum and apricot;
- almond and cherry.

It adds:

- Lecciana and Leccio del Corno olives;
- Salvia rosmarinus, Cistus, Myrtus communis, Rhamnus alaternus, Laurus nobilis, Phillyrea latifolia and Pelargonium.

The act distinguishes resistance, tolerance, immunity, low susceptibility and low infection frequency. Those terms are source-native evidence categories, not interchangeable legal states.

#### Pre-M5 legal routes

DDS 48 relies on pre-M5 Article 18(b) for the Annex III infected-zone authorization and Article 18(c) for named species in eradication zones.

Article 18(c) requires the same species to have been tested and found free through at least two years of Article 10 surveys. Regional evidence over many years may support the route, but the exact species/area/time evidence must be linked. “Immune” does not replace the statutory two-year predicate.

M5 later changes Article 18. Existing authorization after 17 October 2024 must be mapped to the current route, including the new scientifically justified limb where applicable, rather than carried forward under a repealed letter label.

#### Planting versus movement

DDS 48 separately replaces the Article 23 movement procedure. Planting authorization does not itself authorize a producer, site, consignment or movement.

Article 23 site authorization requires registration, annual official survey/testing, vector treatment, recipient declaration, specified passport markings, traceability and continued compliance. This transaction family remains deferred except as the boundary proving that Article 18 authorization is not movement authority.

#### Deferred predecessor gap

DDS 16/2023 is cited and its effect is partly confirmed, but its complete body
is not held. That gap does not block the Articles 1–17 forest.

### DGR 184/2025 — distribution, not new authorization

DGR 184/2025 assigns ARIF production and free distribution of Leccino/FS17 olive plants to noncommercial recipients for projects in the named Lecce, Brindisi and Taranto infected territory.

It expressly depends on DDS 48 authorization. It does not add a cultivar, change Article 18 conditions or authorize a new geography.

Its distribution/eligibility programme and its planting dependency remain
outside the current aperture. It has no implemented Stage A role.

### DGR 903/2025 — vineyard and almond measures

DGR 903 is primarily a compensation/aid act. That programme remains outside the aperture. Two phytosanitary propositions are reached because the current plan incorporates them.

#### Voluntary vineyard extension

DGR 903 allows compensation-linked **voluntary** removal of continuous vineyards within 400 m of the infected-zone perimeter under DDS 22/2025, where:

- there is no headland interruption; and
- the vineyard structure is intact and continuous.

The source does not make those vines part of Article 7’s mandatory 50 m removal population.

Therefore:

```text
VINEYARD_VOLUNTARY_400M
≠ legal removal duty
```

Inside this aid-excluded Stage A, the route is indexed as an optional, noncoercive boundary. It may not support a mandatory case branch or compliance default.

DGR 903 also conditions aid recipients against replanting fastidiosa-host plants in the voluntarily cleared extension. That is an aid/permission condition, not a general Article 18 prohibition for every landholder in the 400 m band.

#### Mandatory almond measure

DGR 903 separately orders mandatory ARIF removal of marginal or abandoned almond trees within 400 m of the infected-zone perimeter for the fastidiosa area. The reasoning cites:

- EFSA’s higher almond susceptibility;
- EFSA’s 400 m high-risk survey band;
- regional monitoring showing almond as the most infected species;
- precaution.

The act still requires a prior Osservatorio prescription before ARIF executes.

The measure is not Article 7’s 50 m infected-zone removal population. It is a regional precautionary extension.

Its controlling authority remains `AUTHORITY_INTERPRETATION_REQUIRED` because:

- survey-risk evidence does not itself authorize coercive removal;
- the act does not identify a clear Union/national provision authorizing a mandatory 400 m regional extension;
- competence for stricter national measures and regional implementation must be resolved;
- the relationship to D.lgs. 19 Articles 5, 6, 31–33 and current Article 7 must be adjudicated.

#### Almond qualification test enters in 2026

DGR 903 does not define “marginal” or “abandoned” for almond trees. Its Annex A/2 defines **productive versus nonproductive vineyards** for compensation. Those vineyard criteria cannot be reused as an almond-tree test.

The current plan adds explanatory references to nonintensive almond orchards lacking irrigation, fertilization and vector control, but it does not itself establish the complete case test.

DDS 11, DDS 12 and DDS 13/2026 supply a source-native field characterization. A marginal/abandoned almond tree is characterized by:

- little or no crown management, absent pruning and dry/dense branches;
- spontaneous vegetation invading the ground and under-crown area;
- no irrigation system;
- low productivity, with fewer, small and poor-quality fruits;
- phytosanitary condition showing symptoms/damage from harmful organisms.

This test becomes available for the 2026 preventive orders. It does not retroactively determine 2025 cases.

The phrase “is characterized by” does not expressly say whether every listed indicator is mandatory or whether the authority makes a holistic judgment. The AND/indicative interpretation remains for adjudication.

Therefore the prior mapping to vineyard productive-status rules is unsupported, but the general claim that no later almond test exists is retracted.

#### 400 m geometry ambiguity

DGR 903 says “within 400 m from the perimeter of the infected zone.” It does not expressly state whether the band is only outward, only inward or both sides of the perimeter.

Because Article 7 already governs the infected zone and the stated purpose is short-distance spread, an outward band may be intended. The source does not make that interpretation certain. Exact implementing prescriptions and geometry are required.

#### Effective sequence

DGR 903 is adopted on 26 June 2025 and published on 21 July 2025. Its aid programme has a separate Commission-identification effectiveness rule. The mandatory almond decision is followed by specific Osservatorio implementation acts.

The aid effectiveness event cannot be assumed to be the legal start of the separate mandatory removal proposition. The act and its implementing prescription must be resolved independently.

## Exact area-state reading

The area population contains independent legal lineages. An area-state version is created or changed by a specific Osservatorio act with attached adopted geometry/cadastral references. A plan, portal map or later recital may summarize that state but does not replace the act.

### General constitutive pattern

The held area acts use the same legal sequence:

```text
official plant finding and subspecies evidence
→ competent Osservatorio judgment
→ 50 m infected zone(s)
→ applicable buffer width
→ adopted signed maps/cadastral annex
→ selected eradication or containment regime
→ immediate executivity
→ operational GIS publication
```

The act’s effective state begins when the source makes it directly/immediately executable. BURP publication and portal publication are evidence/notice surfaces; they do not independently create the geometry.

### Partial-parcel rule

Many acts state that mandatory phytosanitary measures apply to cadastral parcels that fall even partially within the relevant zones.

This is a legal inclusion rule, not a generic geometry simplification. It does not authorize:

- treating an entire parcel as infected where only the buffer intersects;
- replacing plant/zone location with one parcel-level label;
- using a one-hectare survey-grid majority assignment as legal regime.

Each decision still requires the exact intersected legal zone and the provision’s subject population.

### DDS 69/2021 area update

DDS 69, adopted and immediately executable on 27 July 2021, applies the Article 5(1) reduced-buffer route to Canosa after finding all four source conditions satisfied. It reduces the Canosa buffer to not less than 1 km, adopts Canosa infected/buffer geometry in Annex 1 and applies mandatory measures to parcels intersecting that area even partially.

The act confirms the Monopoli, Polignano and Salento areas established or updated by DDS 35/2021 and adopts the complete regional demarcation in Annex 1 bis. That regional map distinguishes infected, containment, buffer and pest-free geography.

The 2021 and early-2022 case orders select their exact subzone within this state. In particular, the Alberobello orders identify the Salento buffer, where Article 7 eradication applies; they do not create a separate Alberobello area state at that time.

### Canosa revocation

DDS 177/2021 revokes Canosa after:

- the reduced-buffer Article 5 route;
- official surveys at 95% confidence/1% prevalence;
- negative plant and vector testing;
- a supported isolated-finding judgment.

It creates two years of intensive post-revocation plant surveys and continuing vector monitoring and requires Commission notification.

A revoked area therefore remains an active workload state. It is not simply deleted.

### Pauca Salento / Valle d’Itria / ex-Salento succession

#### DDS 127/2022

It does three legally material things:

1. applies Articles 13–17 in the new regional containment geography;
2. excludes regional L.R. 4 alternative measures;
3. suspends unexecuted 50 m host removals in five prior eradication orders.

M4 does not add the named municipalities to Annex III until 5 June 2024. DDS 18 therefore creates a direct temporal authority conflict from 14 March to 4 June 2024:

```text
regional containment act
vs.
EU Article 12 + pre-M4 Annex III
```

The regional act cannot amend Annex III. The controlling regime and effect on the five suspended orders require proposition-specific authority interpretation. It is not safe to treat DDS 18 as valid containment from its adoption date merely because M4 later reaches the same geography.

This act resolves the plan’s operative 2 km geography. The stale 2.5 km plan glossary does not control against it.

## Case-body reading

### 2021 batch 1 — DDS 92, 93, 94, 95 and 100

The first five held 2021 orders are read in full.

They instantiate two distinct higher-law branches:

- containment orders DDS 92, 94, 95 and 100 remove only officially infected plants under Article 13;
- eradication order DDS 93 removes the complete 50 m Article 7 population and applies the then-exercised historic-tree retention route.

The owner, parcel, sample, plant and coordinate schedules are instance payload. They do not create reusable legal propositions.

#### Common 2021 notice/execution process

The five orders use:

```text
seven-day municipal-albo publication + PEC where present
→ owner election during publication or within seven days after it ends
→ ten days after election for owner/ARIF execution
→ silence routes to ARIF
→ refusal routes to forced execution, cost recovery and enforcement
```

They separately say publication acquires notice effect after the seventh day.

#### Internal clock conflict

The same orders also state that if the owner has not concretely begun removal within ten days of notification, the Osservatorio will order forced removal and enforcement consequences.

That can conflict with the expressly granted election/execution route:

```text
notice day 0
→ election may occur through day 14
→ execution may follow within ten further days
```

versus:

```text
forced-removal trigger at day 10 from notice
```

The source does not reconcile the two anchors. The case map’s single notice/clock summary omits this conflict. It must be preserved for authority interpretation and event-time default adjudication.

#### Eradication recital miscitation

DDS 93’s dispositive correctly invokes Article 7 and sets the complete eradication population. Its urgency recital says removal follows “pursuant to Article 13(1).” That recital citation is wrong for the selected buffer-zone eradication branch.

The correct source meaning is not inferred from the bad citation alone; it is established by the title, area state, full Article 7 dispositive and removal population. The miscitation remains a source defect.

#### Execution evidence and aid boundary

The field officer’s record verifies removal/destruction and is described as indispensable for the contribution route. Its phytosanitary evidentiary role and its aid prerequisite role are separate.

Aid declarations, bank details and contribution eligibility remain outside the Stage A aperture. Their presence cannot make execution evidence an aid-only fact.

#### Prefect request

The orders allow an inspector or ARIF to request Prefect assistance. D.lgs. 19 Article 33 states that the competent plant-health service may request it. The same reserved-power issue seen in the plans is therefore instantiated in cases from 2021.

### 2021 batches 2–3 — DDS 101–107, 113, 117 and 120

DDS 101–107, 113, 114 and 117 repeat the established containment/eradication procedures. DDS 114 is a standard Article 13 Fasano order with the 2021 seven-day election route and no new reusable clause.

DDS 102’s eradication recital correctly cites Article 7, confirming DDS 93’s Article 13 recital as an isolated miscitation rather than a general 2021 interpretation.

#### Ex-containment Article 13 route

DDS 120 orders removal of 64 officially infected plants in Ostuni’s “ex containment” territory. The plants were found through L.R. 4 regional surveillance outside the minimum Article 15 edge band.

The order still applies Article 13 because the geography remains within the Annex III infected zone.

This establishes:

```text
Article 15 minimum surveillance edge
≠ full Article 13 infected-zone scope
```

A plant outside the edge band but inside the Annex III infected zone can be found through additional regional surveillance and remains subject to Article 13 infected-plant removal.

The case map must therefore select Article 13 from:

```text
exact Annex III infected-zone state
+ official infected finding
```

not from membership in the minimum edge layer alone.

### Corrective and supplemental orders

The directly read representatives are DDS 6/2022, DDS 165/2024, DDS 11/2025, DDS 117/2025 and DDS 63/2026.

#### Identity correction with duty transfer

DDS 6/2022 corrects a plant’s parcel/owner attribution. It expressly removes the former owner’s obligation and prescribes Article 13 removal to the actual owner.

This shows party/parcel identity is instance payload, but a correction to that payload has legal effect on who bears the duty. The correction does not create a new reusable removal rule.

It also omits vector treatment because the February timing makes it unnecessary under the then-effective procedure. That is an event-time treatment branch, not a permanent exemption.

DDS 117/2025 responds to evidence that the original orders were never notified to the current owner. It corrects two annexes and re-prescribes the full Article 7 population to the current owners.

This proves:

```text
wrong recipient / failed service
→ cannot be treated as ordinary owner silence or refusal
→ requires correction and effective re-prescription
```

#### DDS 117 source defects

DDS 117 states that olive is not susceptible to fastidiosa ST1 and excludes it from eradication. That is a new source-specific species exclusion requiring direct comparison with the effective Annex II population and evidence; it cannot be generalized from the pauca or multiplex exclusions.

The same order cites Article 14 for pre-removal vector treatment in an eradication case, where Article 8 is the operative eradication provision. It also closes with an Article 13 immediacy citation despite prescribing Article 7 eradication. Both citations are source defects.

#### Omitted-parcel supplement and explicit mass-publicity basis

DDS 63/2026 adds an omitted parcel to Cagnano Varano’s prior eradication order after ARIF cannot complete work because the owner was absent from the annex.

Unlike the generic case orders, it expressly states the basis for seven-day mass publicity:

- some recipients are unlocatable; and
- individual notification is particularly burdensome.

This is the first directly read case act that states the Law 241 mass-publicity predicate rather than merely asserting publication has notice effect.

The predicate remains case-specific. It cannot retroactively validate every earlier order’s publication route.

### 2023 removal transition

The directly read representatives are DDS 2, 45, 51 and 55/2023, with DDS 128/2021 confirming the prior ex-containment class.

#### DGR 1866 case posture

DDS 2 applies the 2023–2024 plan to Valle d’Itria eradication. It adds to the case order:

- the decision not to exercise Article 7(3) for negative recognized monumental olives;

These are temporal implementation choices. They do not apply to 2021–2022 orders.

The ten-day-from-notice forced-removal clause remains. With a three-day post-publication election window, forced removal can begin at the same point the owner’s later ten-day execution period would begin. The internal clock conflict therefore remains.

#### Monumental recognition/authorization hold

DDS 45, 51 and 55 concern olives that visually have monumental characteristics but are not yet officially recognized.

They route those trees through:

```text
negative test + apparent monumental characteristics
→ report to regional environmental section
→ commission recognition decision
→ recognized: case-specific landscape authorization before removal
→ not recognized: removal
```

The pending-recognition trees are held from removal while recognition/landscape authorization resolves.

This is not Article 7(3) retention. Under the DGR 1866 interval, the regional authority still decides not to exercise Article 7(3). The recognized tree may ultimately be removed after the separate D.lgs. 19 Article 33 / D.lgs. 42 authorization route.

#### Recurrent source miscitation class

The 2023 eradication orders repeatedly:

- cite Article 14 for pre-removal vector treatment, where Article 8 governs eradication; and
- cite Article 13 for immediate execution, where Article 7 governs the removal branch.

These are recurring source defects, not isolated typos. Serialization must retain the act’s selected eradication meaning while exposing the incorrect citations.

#### DDS 51 identity ambiguity

DDS 51 is titled as a correction of DDS 48/2023 but republishes a complete removal prescription. The held text does not clearly state the exact correction beyond the reissued plant/parcel package.

The predecessor relationship and effect on DDS 48 require exact identity/effect adjudication. The title alone does not prove full replacement, partial correction or a new independent order.
