## Reading posture

This reading starts from the Regulation as law. It does not start from case counts, semantic keys, accepted row identities, or the existing dependency tree. The tree supplies questions to test after meaning is read. It does not define meaning.

The bounded mechanism is not a list of independent article families. It is one connected legal process:

```text
defined pest, plant and vector populations
→ annual risk-based surveillance and official confirmation
→ contingency readiness
→ official plant finding
→ demarcated legal geography
→ eradication unless containment is lawfully selected
→ route-specific removal, vector control, destruction and surveillance
→ continuing exceptions and authority judgments
→ planting/grafting authorization inside infected zones
```

The Regulation assigns duties principally to Member States and competent authorities. Italian and Puglia law must identify the competent actors, procedures, notices and execution machinery. Regional acts select or implement legally available branches. They do not create the EU branch universe.

## Articles 1–3: vocabulary, surveillance and readiness

### Article 1

Article 1 fixes four populations that recur throughout the mechanism:

- the specified pest includes *Xylella fastidiosa* and every subspecies;
- host plants are Annex I plants for planting other than seeds;
- specified plants are the Annex II susceptible subset tied to the particular pest subspecies;
- vectors include known Cicadomorpha transmitters and other insects suspected of transmission.

These definitions do not ordinarily require a direct case edge. They determine which subjects satisfy later predicates. A case, survey, area or planting decision that uses one of these concepts depends on the applicable definition and Annex version even when it does not cite Article 1.

The vector definition is especially important because it includes suspected transmitters. An authority cannot require confirmed transmission status before applying every vector-related investigation or control provision if the operative text reaches suspected vectors.

### Article 2

Article 2 establishes a recurring surveillance and diagnostic process independent of any outbreak case.

The annual survey duty applies to host plants and, where infection is suspected, other plant species. Vectors may also be surveyed. Competent authorities perform or officially supervise the work. Survey design is risk-based and spans the environments in which relevant plants are grown or traded, subject to the ecoclimatic exception.

Article 2 separates several independently operating meanings:

- annual survey coverage;
- competent-authority performance or supervision;
- risk-based location and population design;
- sampling and testing of plants and, where applicable, vectors;
- sufficient-confidence/low-presence survey design;
- infected-vector follow-up within at least 400 m;
- seasonally appropriate timing;
- primary and confirmatory molecular testing;
- subspecies identification for each infected plant species in a demarcated area;
- survey-result reporting through Regulation 2016/2031.

These meanings should not be judged by direct case-link counts. Their natural downstream descendants include campaign plans, survey designs, sampling operations, laboratory work, official findings, demarcation evidence and reporting. Some may also become case predicates—for example, a confirmed infected vector or the molecular confirmation needed before an official plant finding—but Article 2 is not itself a case catalogue.

The infected-vector route is distinct from plant confirmation. It requires immediate survey, sampling and testing around the vector finding. It does not itself state that a demarcated area is established.

Missing survey or laboratory evidence does not make the annual duty disappear and does not prove absence of the pest.

### Article 3

Article 3 requires a maintained national contingency plan. The plan must cover eradication, later movement controls and official checks. Within the present aperture, the plan’s eradication and readiness provisions are fully relevant; movement and official-check families are reached dependencies rather than reasons to compile Chapters VII–XI as operating programs.

The plan also incorporates Regulation 2016/2031 Article 25(2) and must state:

- minimum and surge resources for suspected or confirmed presence;
- procedures to identify owners;
- procedures to notify removal orders;
- procedures to access private property.

Article 3 therefore bridges EU substantive measures to national and regional readiness and execution law. It is expected to connect to national plan, competence, notice and access provisions rather than to each individual removal case directly.

## Articles 4–6: legal geography as a lifecycle

### Article 4

An officially confirmed plant finding triggers establishment of a demarcated area without delay. The area may be limited to confirmed subspecies. Pending subspecies confirmation, demarcation covers the pest and all possible subspecies.

The demarcated area consists of:

- an infected zone with a radius of at least 50 m around the infected plant;
- a buffer of at least 2.5 km for eradication;
- a buffer of at least 5 km for containment.

These are legal minimums. The competent authority may adopt wider source-supported geography. The exact legally operative state therefore combines the EU minimum with the national/regional constitutive act and its adopted geometry.

Article 4(3) requires Commission publication of the Member State notifications. That publication does not replace the national or regional act that establishes or changes the area.

### Article 5

Article 5 governs two distinct derogation routes.

#### Reduced eradication buffer

The eradication buffer may be reduced to no less than 1 km only where confidence is high that the initial presence did not spread and all conditions hold:

- immediate sampling and removal of all specified plants in the infected zone;
- no further infected plants found through official annual testing after eradication;
- a first-year survey across at least 2.5 km around the infected zone, designed for 90% confidence at 1% prevalence with the first 400 m treated as higher risk;
- no infected vectors in the infected zone or immediate vicinity, based on twice-per-flight-season testing that supports exclusion of natural spread.

The reduction is a reserved Member State decision after the conditions are evidenced. The evidence does not itself alter geography. The reduction and its justification must be notified immediately.

#### No immediate demarcation

The authority may initially avoid demarcation only where:

- evidence supports recent introduction with the infected plants or physical vector protection;
- inspections indicate infection before introduction;
- no infected vectors are detected nearby.

That decision creates continuing duties: at least one annual survey, determination of whether other plants are infected and whether further measures are needed, notification of the justification, and reporting of survey results. The survey uses Annex IV testing and 90% confidence at 1% prevalence.

Article 5 therefore should connect to authority decisions, survey evidence, notification and area-state alternatives. Its absence from direct case links is a flag, but direct case mapping is not the only correct representation.

### Article 6

Article 6 defines two lifting routes and post-lifting surveillance.

The ordinary route permits lifting after four years without detection under Article 10 surveys. The reduced-buffer route permits lifting after 12 months only when:

- the initial presence is confidently isolated with no further spread; and
- official tests practically close to lifting meet 95% confidence at 1% prevalence.

The second route creates two further years of intensive surveillance at 80% confidence and 1% prevalence. Both lifting routes carry notification duties.

Lifting is a legal authority decision. Negative survey evidence supports it but does not automatically revoke an area. Every area state must therefore have an exact creating, changing or lifting act and temporal interval.

## Articles 7–11: eradication

### Article 7

Article 7 requires immediate removal from the infected zone of distinct plant populations:

- known infected plants;
- symptomatic or suspected plants;
- plants of the infected plant’s species regardless of health;
- other species found infected elsewhere in the demarcated area;
- other specified plants not immediately sampled and molecularly tested.

Negative-tested Article 7(1)(e) plants need not be removed. A separate two-year route permits no immediate sampling/testing where the specified plants have not been found infected in that area during the previous two years, but annual Article 10 surveillance continues.

Removal must account for risk and necessary precautions.

The historical/social/environmental-value derogation applies only to Article 7(1)(b)–(d) plants and requires all conditions:

- official designation under the stated value/protection grounds;
- annual inspection, Annex IV sampling/testing and confirmation of no infection;
- appropriate all-stage vector treatment for the plant or area.

The authority decision to retain a plant is separate from the continuing testing and treatment duties. Missing evidence cannot be treated as a negative test or continuing eligibility.

### Article 8

Article 8 creates vector-control duties that continue beyond individual removals.

In infected zones, all-stage treatment is required, especially before and during removal during vector flight. Agricultural areas in both infected and buffer zones require vector-control practices at the most appropriate time, regardless of removal. Non-agricultural areas require control at least in infected zones, also regardless of removal.

The legal method set includes efficient chemical, biological or mechanical treatment as appropriate to local conditions. Regional plans and determinations may operationalize timing and methods within competence; completion records do not activate the underlying duty.

The lack of direct case links is therefore a likely mapping limitation. Article 8 should usually connect to recurring campaign, land-management and removal-support processes, and sometimes to a case-specific prerequisite.

### Article 9

Article 9 requires destruction of Article 7(1) plants and parts in a way that prevents spread. The default route is in situ, nearby within the infected zone, or at the shortest location where covered transport prevents vector infection.

The authority may limit destruction to foliage and branches based on risk. The wood then receives Article 8(1) treatment. Roots must be removed or devitalized. If wood is not destroyed, the authority must verify it is free of leaves and branches.

Removal, destruction-location selection, limited-destruction judgment, wood treatment, root disposition and leaf/branch verification are related but independently variable legal meanings.

### Article 10

Article 10 establishes continuing annual surveillance throughout the demarcated area.

- Infected zones: host plants, including retained specified plants, are sampled and tested at 90% confidence and 0.5% prevalence.
- Buffer zones: host plants and symptomatic/suspected other plants are sampled and tested at 90% confidence and 1% prevalence, with the first 400 m treated as higher risk.
- Vectors are monitored to assess spread risk and control effectiveness.

This Article directly supports area continuation, Article 6 lifting, Article 7’s two-year testing route, surveillance planning and retained-plant oversight. Direct case links are neither necessary nor sufficient to represent those relationships.

### Article 11

Article 11 preserves an open but bounded eradication-measures obligation:

- other measures contributing to eradication under ISPM 9 and integrated approaches under ISPM 14;
- measures addressing complications that could prevent, hinder or delay eradication, including destruction, access, ownership and responsible-person identification;
- investigation and traceability of infection origin and associated host-plant movements, followed by communication to affected Member States and the Commission.

These are not discretionary omissions merely because exact implementation requires authority judgment. The later national/Puglia readings must show how investigation, access, ownership, destruction obstacles and cross-authority communication are implemented.

## Articles 12–17: containment

### Article 12

Containment is not the default response to every infected-zone case. The competent authority may apply Articles 13–17 instead of eradication only in an infected zone listed in Annex III.

The executable branch therefore requires:

```text
applicable Annex III version
+ infected-zone identity
+ competent-authority containment decision
```

Area geometry alone does not select containment. A case order may evidence branch selection, but cannot make containment legally available where Annex III does not.

### Article 13

In an Annex III infected zone, plants found infected through Article 15(2) monitoring must be removed. Timing depends on official identification and vector flight season:

- immediate removal after official identification;
- if detected outside flight season, removal before the next flight season.

Necessary precautions apply during and after removal.

A scientific-purpose derogation may retain infected plants only at Article 15(2)(b) cultural/social-value sites. This is a distinct authority decision, not the Article 7 historical-value route.

### Article 14

Article 14 establishes containment vector treatment.

Treatment applies to all vector stages on Article 13(1) plants before removal, particularly during flight season, and around Article 13(2) retained plants. Agricultural practices also apply annually in the Article 15(2)(a) edge band and Article 15(2)(b) cultural/social-value sites.

These duties are continuing or removal-supporting processes. Their lack of direct case links cannot be accepted as proof that they have no operational descendants.

### Article 15

Article 15 connects containment monitoring, the infected-plant removal population and buffer surveillance.

Immediate 50 m sampling/testing applies around infected plants, covering:

- specified plants of species already found infected in the same demarcated area;
- other symptomatic or suspected plants.

Annual monitoring applies at least:

- within at least 2 km from the infected-zone border with the buffer;
- near designated cultural/social-value sites outside that band.

Within those parts, infected host species are surveyed at 90% confidence and 0.7% prevalence, and vectors are sampled/tested. The 2 km edge-band requirement does not apply to wholly contained islands more than 5 km from the nearest Union land territory.

Buffer-zone surveillance uses 90% confidence and 1% prevalence with the first 400 m adjacent to the infected zone treated as higher risk. Vector monitoring supports spread-risk and treatment-effectiveness assessment.

Article 15 should connect to annual survey design, retained-value sites, infected-plant findings, Article 13 removal, vector work, buffer state and Article 18’s excluded band. Its absence from direct case links is an important mapping question, not a reason to invent a synthetic Article 15 family.

### Article 16

Containment destruction broadly parallels Article 9 but applies to plants found infected under the containment route. Destruction occurs in situ or nearby within the Annex III infected zone.

The authority may limit destruction to branches and foliage only where it concludes there is no further-spread risk. Wood receives Article 14(1) treatment, roots are removed or devitalized, and retained wood must be verified free from leaves and branches.

The exact branch and evidence must remain tied to containment, not merged with eradication merely because the physical work resembles it.

### Article 17

Article 17 requires measures addressing complications that could prevent, hinder or delay containment, especially destruction, access, ownership and responsible-person issues.

It is not a duplicate of Article 11. It applies to the containment branch and does not include Article 11’s broader origin-tracing provision.

## Deferred boundary — Article 18 planting and grafting authorization

Article 18 is read and indexed but excluded from the implemented Stage A
aperture. Its planting/grafting permission programme does not mint propositions,
regional authorization workflows or downstream product states. If a later
aperture opens, the source meaning remains:

1. insect-proof production sites free from pest and vectors;
2. planting or grafting in Annex III infected zones, outside the Article 15(2)(a) edge band, using preferably resistant/tolerant varieties or the same species shown pest-free through at least two years of infected-zone surveys;
3. replanting in eradication infected zones of the same species shown pest-free through at least two years of Article 10 surveys;
4. scientific planting of other specified plants outside the Article 15(2)(a) area.

The routes are alternatives. Each has distinct geography, population, evidence and authority implications. “Preferably” in route (b) is not equivalent to an absolute resistant/tolerant-variety requirement. The national/Puglia reading must determine what administrative decision, evidence and source-native stricter conditions lawfully implement each route.

No current downstream implementation is required. The source reading is retained
only to prevent planting permission from leaking into Articles 1–17 or being
confused with movement authority.

## Deferred and later provisions

Article 18 and the remainder of the Regulation are not one undifferentiated
dependency set. Article 18 is indexed as a planting/grafting authorization
boundary; later transaction families remain separately deferred.

### Articles 19–31 and 33

Articles 19–27 define movement and plant-passport transaction programs. Articles 28–31 define third-country introduction programs. Article 33 defines border checks. They are not fully implemented in the current Stage A aperture.

The in-scope mechanism reaches their boundary in limited ways:

- Article 3 requires the contingency plan to account for movement and official checks.
- Article 18 planting authorization and Article 23 movement operate in different legal dimensions. Authorization to plant inside an infected zone does not itself authorize later movement.
- Article 32 is directly named in Article 35 reporting and protects the internal-movement boundary.
- Definitions, authorized sites, inspection and testing concepts from later chapters enter only where an admitted provision expressly depends on them.

No movement, passport, consignment, production-site or import transaction object is inferred merely because the full instrument was read.

### Article 32

Article 32 requires systematic official checks when specified plants move out of a demarcated area or from infected to buffer zones. Checks occur at relevant movement locations, include documentary and identity checks, and apply regardless of declared origin, ownership or responsible person. Non-compliance with Articles 19–23 triggers immediate destruction with spread precautions.

For the current aperture, Article 32 is a boundary and enforcement dependency. It does not transform Chapters VII–IX into the operator’s outbreak-response program.

### Article 34

Article 34 requires public awareness of the pest threat, demarcated-area boundaries and the measures against vectors under Articles 8 and 14. It therefore has a direct dependency on legal geography and both eradication and containment vector-control duties.

Public information is a continuing Member State obligation. A case notice or published map may contribute, but neither substitutes for the complete awareness duty.

### Article 35

Article 35(1) requires annual reporting by 30 April on measures and results under Articles 2, 4, 5, 7–18 and 32. Articles 10 and 15 survey results use Annex V templates.

Article 35(4) requires immediate notification of official identifications in the Article 15(2)(a) containment edge band.

These are downstream reporting and notification effects of the in-scope process. They do not activate the underlying measures.

### Articles 36–38

Article 36 requires incompatible national protective measures to be repealed or amended and immediately reported. Article 37 repeals the predecessor decision. Article 38 sets entry into force and delayed application.

Article 38 is a temporal dependency because the second sentence of Article 2(4) and third sentence of Article 5(1)(c) applied only from 1 January 2023. Point-in-time law must not project those statistical-design requirements into earlier decisions.

## Annexes I–IV

### Annex I

Annex I defines the host-plant population. It is incorporated through Article 1(b) and therefore reaches annual surveillance, sampling, demarcation investigation, movement boundaries and other provisions that use “host plants.”

The list changes through amendments. A plant is not a host plant for all historical dates merely because it appears in the current Annex. The applicable Annex I version must be resolved at the relevant event and decision time.

### Annex II

Annex II defines specified plants by susceptibility to the relevant subspecies. The list is partitioned by subspecies, including fastidiosa, multiplex and pauca.

This is not a generic species catalogue. Executable use requires:

```text
applicable Annex II version
+ pest subspecies
+ plant taxon
```

The current pauca list includes, among others, *Olea europaea* subspecies, *Prunus*, *Nerium oleander*, *Polygala myrtifolia*, *Lavandula*, and other named taxa. Regional biological exclusions or case classifications cannot rewrite Annex II; they may only apply supported facts or lawful source-specific distinctions.

### Annex III

Annex III defines the infected zones where Article 12 may make containment available.

For Italy in the current 24 November 2025 consolidation, Part A includes:

- Lecce province;
- Brindisi province;
- the listed Taranto municipalities;
- the listed Bari municipalities: Alberobello, Castellana Grotte, Locorotondo, Monopoli, Polignano a Mare and Putignano.

Annex III supplies legal list membership. It does not supply the detailed current regional infected-zone polygon, buffer geometry or case location. The containment branch requires Annex III membership plus the competent-authority decision. Exact regional area state then supplies the operative geography.

Every historical case must use the Annex III version effective at its event/decision time. A municipality added later cannot be borrowed backward.

### Annex IV

Annex IV lists authorized tests for:

- screening and identification of *Xylella fastidiosa* presence;
- identification of subspecies.

The current source lists Harper real-time PCR/LAMP, Ouyang real-time PCR and Minsavage conventional PCR for presence; and MLST, Hernandez-Martinez, Pooler & Hartung, Dupas and Hodgetts methods for subspecies as specified.

A positive result, official confirmation and subspecies identification must be linked to the applicable legal method version and source procedure. A later diagnostic edition cannot be projected into an earlier event without authority.

## Annex V reporting dependency

Annex V provides the reporting templates for Articles 10 and 15 plant and vector surveys. It preserves important distinctions among:

- demarcated area and approach;
- infected and buffer zones;
- target population, epidemiological units and inspection units;
- detection methods, sampling effectiveness and method sensitivity;
- risk factors, levels, relative risks and population proportions;
- as-run examinations, samples, tests and results;
- achieved confidence and design prevalence;
- vector species, capture method, frequency, timing and results.

These reporting fields evidence how the survey was designed and executed. They do not independently create survey law, select the eradication/containment branch or establish legal area geometry.

## Integrated downstream representation required by meaning

The current source reading indicates that direct case links are only one dependency type.

| EU meaning | Natural downstream representation |
|---|---|
| definitions and Annex I/II populations | predicates used by surveys, findings, areas, planting and cases |
| annual surveillance and diagnostics | campaigns, survey designs, sampling/laboratory processes and official findings |
| contingency readiness | national plan, resources, owner identification, notice and access procedure |
| demarcation, derogation and lifting | authority decisions plus exact area-state transitions |
| eradication/containment selection | Annex III version plus competent-authority decision |
| removal | case branch and recurring execution process |
| vector treatment | recurring territorial duty and removal prerequisite |
| destruction/wood/root disposition | execution route and authority judgment |
| annual demarcated-area surveillance | continuing area duty, evidence and reporting |
| other measures/investigation | recurring response, obstacle resolution and traceability |
| planting/grafting | permissioned authority decision tied to area and evidence |
| reporting/awareness | cross-authority reporting and public communication duty |

Coverage therefore means every proposition has the dependency form its semantics require. It does not mean every Article must attach directly to a removal case.

## Point-in-time EU meaning

### 20 August 2020 base state

The base Regulation entered into force on the third day after its 17 August 2020 Official Journal publication. Articles 1–18 established the original mechanism.

Two original statistical-design sentences had delayed application until 1 January 2023 under Article 38:

- Article 2(4)’s survey-design sentence;
- Article 5(1)(c)’s 90%-confidence/1%-prevalence design sentence.

Their text existed in the instrument before 2023, but the specified application date controls executable use.

### 11 October 2021 — M1

Implementing Regulation 2021/1688 entered into force on the twentieth day after its 21 September 2021 publication. It:

- replaced Annex I;
- replaced Annex II;
- replaced Annex IV and added DOI identifiers to the named methods;
- changed Article 33’s official-check location reference.

It did not alter Articles 1–18. The consolidation’s apparent Article formatting changes are not legal versions.

### 23 December 2021 — M2

Implementing Regulation 2021/2130 entered into force on the twentieth day after its 3 December 2021 publication. It replaced Annexes I and II to:

- use *Salvia rosmarinus* instead of the obsolete *Rosmarinus* denomination;
- include *Salvia rosmarinus* as susceptible to pauca as well as the already represented fastidiosa/multiplex scope;
- make the related list corrections.

This changes plant-population predicates, not the structure of Articles 1–18.

### 1 January 2023 — delayed application

Article 2(4)’s statistical survey design requirement and Article 5(1)(c)’s third-sentence 90%/1% design requirement begin to apply. A 2020–2022 event cannot be judged as though those delayed sentences were already executable.

### 28 September 2023 — M3

Implementing Regulation 2023/1706 entered into force on the twentieth day after its 8 September 2023 publication. It replaced Annexes I and II based on the updated host database and corrected taxonomy.

The amendment:

- added newly supported hosts and subspecies-specific susceptible plants;
- narrowed genera to supported species where appropriate;
- removed *Phillyrea latifolia* from the multiplex list because its inclusion was erroneous;
- removed unsupported *Fortunella*;
- replaced obsolete *Osteospermum* names with *Dimorphotheca* names;
- updated author and nomenclature details.

This demonstrates why Annex versions cannot be reduced to immutable crop categories or inferred from current nomenclature.

### 5 June 2024 — M4

Implementing Regulation 2024/1320 entered into force on the twentieth day after its 16 May 2024 publication. It amended Annex III for Italy after Italy confirmed eradication was no longer possible in the named areas.

It added:

- Fasano to the Taranto municipality list;
- Alberobello, Castellana Grotte, Monopoli, Polignano a Mare and Putignano alongside Locorotondo in the Bari list.

This makes Articles 13–17 legally available for those Annex III infected zones from this effective date, subject to competent-authority containment selection. It does not retrospectively convert earlier eradication cases into containment cases.

### 17 October 2024 — M5 core mechanism

Implementing Regulation 2024/2507 entered into force on the twentieth day after its 27 September 2024 publication. Its Articles 1–18 changes apply from that date. The amendment creates independently variable temporal changes:

- **Article 1:** adds the legal vector definition, including suspected transmitters.
- **Article 2(1):** expands annual surveys from host plants to other suspected plant species and permits vector coverage.
- **Article 2(3):** adds the ecoclimatic open-air exception.
- **Article 2(4):** replaces fixed 80%-confidence/1%-prevalence wording for general Member State surveys with sufficient-confidence/low-presence design and adds infected-vector follow-up within at least 400 m.
- **Article 4(1):** limits the immediate demarcation trigger to official confirmation in plants. An infected-vector finding invokes Article 2(4a), not automatic Article 4 demarcation.
- **Article 5(4):** reduces the no-immediate-demarcation follow-up survey minimum from two years to one year and adds Annex IV/90%-confidence/1%-prevalence requirements.
- **Article 7(1)(e):** clarifies the unsampled/untested population, expressly preserves negative-tested plants, and adds a two-year no-infection route that defers immediate testing while keeping Article 10 annual surveillance active.
- **Article 7(3):** broadens the potential protected-plant grounds beyond historic value, while retaining the continuing testing and vector-treatment conditions.
- **Article 8:** distinguishes agricultural from non-agricultural territorial duties and clarifies flight-season removal treatment.
- **Article 9(3):** adds competent-authority verification that retained wood is free from leaves and branches.
- **Article 14(1):** clarifies flight-season treatment around containment removal/retention routes.
- **Article 15(2)(a):** reduces the containment infected-zone edge band from at least 5 km to at least 2 km.
- **Article 16(3):** adds the retained-wood leaf/branch verification under containment.
- **Article 18:** expands route (b) to planting or grafting, permits the same species with two-year pest-free evidence as an alternative to the resistant/tolerant preference, clarifies eradication replanting, and adds scientific route (d).
- **Annexes I/II:** add and correct host/susceptibility entries and remove an erroneous multiplex entry.
- **Annex III:** adds the Portuguese containment area.
- **Annex IV:** adds Dupas and Hodgetts subspecies tests.
- **Article 35:** removes paragraphs 2 and 3 but leaves the in-scope annual-reporting paragraph 1 and immediate-notification paragraph 4.

The amendment also changes later movement/import provisions. Those changes remain outside full Stage A implementation except for their exact reached boundaries.

### 1 July 2025 — M5 delayed later provisions

Only M5 Article 1 point 15, points 17(b) and 18(c), plus its amendment to Regulation 2020/1770, begin applying on 1 July 2025. These concern named high-risk plants in Articles 25(2), 28(d), 29(e) and traceability-code rules.

This delayed date does **not** apply to the M5 Articles 1–18 changes. Treating the complete M5 package as delayed until July 2025 would be a temporal error.

### 24 November 2025 — M6

Implementing Regulation 2025/2231 entered into force on the twentieth day after its 4 November 2025 publication. It replaces Annex III Part B to add Occitania municipalities to the French containment list.

It does not change the Italian Annex III list or Articles 1–18. The applicable 24 November consolidation nevertheless carries the updated Union-wide Annex III version.

## Temporal consequences for the dependency tree

The reading requires exact versioned dependencies rather than article-name links:

1. A vector finding before 17 October 2024 does not use the later Article 2(4a) 400 m route.
2. Article 4’s explicit plant-only confirmation trigger begins on 17 October 2024.
3. Article 5’s no-demarcation follow-up changes from two years to one year on 17 October 2024 and gains an explicit survey-design requirement.
4. Article 7 removal/testing and protected-plant routes change materially on 17 October 2024.
5. Article 8’s non-agricultural route and Article 9/16 retained-wood verification begin on 17 October 2024.
6. Article 15’s edge band is 5 km before 17 October 2024 and 2 km from that date.
7. Article 18’s grafting, same-species evidence alternative and scientific route begin on 17 October 2024.
8. Annex I/II population membership must use the effective list at the event/decision time.
9. Italian Annex III membership changes on 5 June 2024. Later French/Portuguese changes do not change Italian membership.
10. Annex IV method availability changes on 11 October 2021 and 17 October 2024.

No case, area, survey or authorization may borrow a later version backward.
