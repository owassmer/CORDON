# Telos review — S7: the producer market

Adversarial market reviewer, independent. Question: who would actually use or pay for Xylella-related information products? Everything judged by real purchase/use probability. Figures verified against a primary source or marked **UNVERIFIED**. Date: 2026-08-16.

---

## A. Customer universe with numbers

**Olive farms.** ISTAT 7° Censimento Agricoltura 2020 (report 28 Jun 2022): Puglia has ~170,000 farms growing woody crops on 491,000 ha; olive = 94% of those farms and 71% of that surface → **~160,000 olive-growing farms** (reviewer's derivation from the two ISTAT percentages). National mean olive area is 994,318 ha / 619,378 farms ≈ **1.6 ha/farm** — the base is overwhelmingly micro/part-time. ISMEA (Regione Puglia press, Nov 2024): Puglia has **330,700 ha olive-oil surface in production** (31.6% of Italy) — Bari 98,900, Brindisi 63,300, Lecce 55,000, Foggia 52,100, BAT 33,300, Taranto 28,100 — and produces ~100k of Italy's 224k t of oil (2024/25). The *professional* segment is far smaller: Unioncamere Puglia (Evolio Expo, BariToday 2025): **>16,000 imprese, >700 frantoi, >20,000 addetti**.
**Mills (frantoi).** **746 active** (ISMEA 2024/25; 17.2% of Italy). Older AGEA 2014/15 count: 939 (Lecce 251, Bari 228, Brindisi 143, Foggia 136, Taranto 97, BAT 84) — consolidation is real.
**Cooperatives / OPs.** APROL Lecce OP: **28,749 members incl. 40 co-op bodies** (unaprol.it). Aproli Bari OP: medium-large Bari-province farms (aproli.it, no member count published). Puglia Olive OP. Unaprol nationally: >100,000 farms. Exemplar coop on the front: **Oleificio Cooperativo Coltivatori Diretti Produttori, Ostuni — 386 members, 3,800 ha of largely secular olives, 30,000 q olives/yr, 6,500 q oil, ~800 q DOP-certified** (oliodiostuni.com).
**DOP bodies.** *Collina di Brindisi DOP*: consortium seated in Ostuni (C.da S. Lorenzo, tel. 0831 338049; control body Bioagricert); production zone = Carovigno, Ceglie Messapica, Cisternino, Fasano, Ostuni, S. Michele Salentino, S. Vito dei Normanni, Villa Castelli — **exactly the comuni of the current pauca front**. *Terra di Bari DOP*: Bari+BAT provinces, control body CCIAA Bari, active operator register updated 16/06/2026 (ba.camcom.it). *Terra d'Otranto DOP* (Salento; president Giovanni Melcarne — a named grafting advocate). Coldiretti Puglia and CIA Puglia: the two dominant unions (membership counts **UNVERIFIED**); Coldiretti+Unaprol+CAI run the "polo antixylella" (RAI TGR, May 2024).
**Wine, ST1-adjacent.** Consorzio Vini DOC Gioia del Colle: **73 members, 87% of the DOC's production** (pugliain.net 2021), zone on the Murge next to the ST1 belt. Bari table-grape district: Puglia 23,400 of ~41,000 ha national, ~€640M/yr at origin (industry est., from S4 facts). DOC Ostuni: near-dormant 1972 denomination with essentially one active champion (Amalberga, below); no consortium found (**verified absence in searches**).
**Masserie / agritourism.** **960 active agriturismi in Puglia**, 15,284 beds, 27,948 restaurant seats (Coldiretti/Terranostra on ISTAT 2023 report); Puglia tourism 2025: 6.7M arrivals / 22.7M presences, olive-grove trekking and oleoturismo explicitly cited as demand drivers (Coldiretti Puglia, Jul 2026). The **Piana degli Ulivi Monumentali** (Monopoli–Fasano–Ostuni–Carovigno): ~250,000 monumental-grade trees (Coldiretti, RAI Nov 2024) or ~350,000 censused secular/millennial trees (AMO Puglia via Olivo e Olio) — counts diverge, both secondary; Coldiretti claims the plain "has already lost 1/3" (**UNVERIFIED**). Ostuni comune alone: ~332,000 olive trees on 6,650 ha (italia.it, **UNVERIFIED precision**).
**Nurseries.** RUOP-registered professional operators with plant-passport authorization (Puglia RUOP codes run past IT-16-0909; olive-specialized subset unpublished — **GAP**). Authorized replant set: Leccino, FS-17, Lecciana, Leccio del Corno (DDS 48, 3 May 2024). Lecciana is proprietary to Agromillora subsidiaries only. "EMERGENZA XYLELLA – Puglia" passport regime governs in-zone sales (DDS 54/2020). The polo antixylella positions itself as the certified-plant supply channel.

**Structure of the market:** ~160k farms exist but ≲16k are businesses; buying power sits in ~750 mills, a few dozen coops/OPs, 3 oil DOP consortia, 1–2 wine consortia, ~960 agritourism estates, and a handful of nurseries. Any producer product that requires 100k individual purchases is dead on arrival; anything real is sold to or through the ~50 intermediary bodies.

## B. The exemplar — Cantine Amalberga (Ostuni)

**What it is (verified, amalberga.it + La Stampa 20 May 2025 + Corriere del Vino 2025):** Vallegna Società Agricola S.r.l., SP 17 km 6, Ostuni (BR). Founded ~2016 by local agri-entrepreneur Dario De Pascale with partners Fracassetti and Candia; oenologists Valentino Ciarla and Gloria Battista. **~20 ha, ~80,000 bottles/yr**; 11 ha owned vineyards plus ~12 ha of linked farms; organic; two production sites (Ostuni, Manduria); new underground designer cellar (first vinification 2023); shop + AMA CHILOMETROZERO restaurant in Ostuni's centro storico; wine bar in a restored trullo; tours, tastings, cooking classes. Mission: relaunch the dormant **DOC Ostuni** (Ottavianello "Stùne", Ostuni Bianco) on autochthonous varieties. It also sells **"Olio Extravergine di Oliva Biologico Leccino – Vallegna Ostuni"** — an olive-oil line already on the resistant cultivar (whether pre-existing Leccino stock or post-Xylella replant: **UNVERIFIED**). Estate grounds feature centuries-old olives, oaks, trulli, dry-stone walls (iCru Magazine 2024).
**Does Xylella touch it?** Directly, yes, on three of its four value streams. Ostuni is the epicentre of the current pauca front: **1,516 olive positives in the 2021 campaign — top comune in Puglia (verified on-disk, CAMP_2021.xlsx: 1,522 total, 1,516 olive; next: Cisternino 520, Fasano 279)**; CNR's Boscia (Quotidiano Nazionale, 9 Dec 2024): "important recrudescence between Ostuni and Ceglie Messapica", the monumental plain "under serious attack", and a September 2024 public meeting in Ostuni overflowed a 300-seat hall. **On-disk finding (mine): Ostuni has ZERO rows in the 2023–2025 campaign workbooks** — post-demarcation, official sampling moved to the buffer/containment belt (and 2024/2507 cut the containment survey belt 5→2 km), so an Ostuni-area estate now sits in an official-data shadow: the front is at its peak locally exactly when parcel-level public signal stopped. Its *wine* core is biologically safe today: pauca ST53 does not infect grapevine, and the ST1 (PD-genotype) outbreak is ~60 km NW near Bari, declared non-expanding Feb 2026 (Regione Puglia; S4 facts).
**What such an estate concretely loses:** (1) the olive-oil product line if its non-Leccino trees convert; (2) landscape/amenity capital — the trulli-and-secular-olives setting IS the tourism product (oleoturismo, weddings, tastings); dead groves along SP17 degrade every visit; (3) brand narrative ("Valle d'Itria" aesthetics, cited by the company itself as the tourism draw); (4) option value of monumental trees on the property. **Decisions it actually faces:** test which trees (€50/sample, below); graft monumental specimens now vs wait (€5M regional measure, 80–100% aid, dotazione "too limited" — Olivo e Olio); replant blocks with Leccino/FS-17/Lecciana; execute mandatory vector tillage windows; how to keep telling the story to tourists. **Generalization:** the Ostuni estate's need is 2–4 discrete decisions per decade, each triggered by "when does the front reach this parcel and how fast" — a question nobody currently answers for them (Section C). It is NOT a weekly-alert subscription need.

**Gap map.** (1) No product answers "when does the front reach my parcel, at what probability, and which year should I graft/replant" — front-timing at estate scale. (2) No monumental-tree triage (which of my 200 secular trees to graft first). (3) No independent cultivar field-performance evidence at purchase time (buyers take Agromillora's and nurseries' word). (4) The DSS incumbents skip Xf deliberately: their engine is weather-driven treatment timing, and Xf offers **no recurring treatment decision** — nothing to alert weekly. The entire private Xf spend observable today is: lab tests (€50), resistant plants, grafting labour, and dossier fees.

## D. Demand evidence and top 3 offerings

**Evidence producers pay for monitoring/advisory:** Ag-4.0 Italy market €2.3B in 2024 (−8%); **41% of farms use ≥1 solution but only 9.5% of SAU is managed with them; 84% of adopters used a public incentive; 57% of farms are digitally "behind" and >90% of those have invested nothing and don't intend to** (Osservatorio Smart AgriFood, PoliMi, Feb 2025). Adoption is 55% among OP members vs 38% among unaffiliated farms → **the channel is the co-op/OP, not the farmer**. Elaisian: >1,500 paying farms in 15 countries (2022), >$2.5M raised, Regione Puglia itself invested €160k (BeBeez 2021) — proof olive growers pay ~€300/yr, but only for weekly-actionable fly/disease alerts. Xylella-specific: the 300-seat Ostuni overflow (Boscia, Sep 2024) shows information hunger — at price zero; Art.6 oversubscription (9,186 requests, €222M vs €80M — Regione Puglia Jul 2024) shows producers mobilize paperwork when money is attached; grafting uptake exists but stalled on "too limited" public funding (Olivo e Olio) — i.e., producers graft when someone else pays. **WTP ceiling for producer software ≈ €300/yr, and Xf doesn't fit the subscription mold.**

**Top 3 by probability-of-use × value (each with named channel):**
1. **Front-timing & replant/graft decision brief for the Ostuni–Fasano–Carovigno belt** ("the front will cross your comune band in year X±Y; graft monumentals before Z; replant blocks after felling with cultivar table"), built from the 13-year workbook series + published spread rates, refreshed yearly. Sold once to intermediaries, not farmers: **Consorzio DOP Collina di Brindisi (C.da S. Lorenzo, Ostuni), Oleificio Cooperativo Ostuni (386 members), AMO Puglia, Comune di Ostuni/GAL Alto Salento**; estates like Amalberga consume it via the consortium. Realistic ticket €2–10k/body one-off (**reviewer estimate**). P(use) moderate-high — it lands exactly in the §B data shadow; value real (timing a €10–50k estate investment).
2. **Monumental-tree grafting triage** — ranked list of which censused trees (LR 14/2007 registry) to graft first given front timing, packaged for the €5M grafting measure and AMO's 25-ha conservation zone. Buyers: **AMO Puglia, Save the Olives, Unaprol/polo antixylella (IKEA "Mosaico Verde" precedent: 100 trees grafted, Carovigno 2023 — AGI)**. Producers use it free; the NGO/CSR sponsor pays. P(use) moderate; value concentrated in irreplaceable assets.
3. **Independent cultivar field-performance sheet at point of sale** (Leccino vs FS-17 vs Lecciana vs Leccio del Corno conversion/survival from repeat-tested coordinates — the S2 review's #2 action, repackaged). Channel: **polo antixylella (Coldiretti/Unaprol/CAI), nurseries, Aproli Bari, APROL Lecce** — they have a commercial incentive to fund credibility material for 9,186 pending replanters. P(use) moderate; value = steering ~1M plants/yr toward evidence.

All three monetize through intermediary bodies at €1k–10k tickets. Direct farmer-paid Xylella SaaS: no evidence anywhere that it exists or could.

## E. Kill list (products that sound good; nobody pays)

- **Farmer-facing Xf early-warning subscription (satellite/AI, €/ha/yr).** KILL. Previsual detection at scale is unproven outside research aircraft (Zarco-Tejada); even a true positive gives the farmer no private action — felling is the state's act, there is no cure, and testing is €50 at CNR. Incumbents (Elaisian, Agricolus, xFarm) already skip Xf for exactly this reason. ARPU ceiling €300/yr is spent on fly alerts that pay back annually.
- **Per-tree IoT/sap sensors for Xf.** KILL. Hardware cost ≫ tree value except monumentals; no validated in-planta signal; maintenance burden on a 90%-digitally-inert base.
- **"Xylella dashboard/map" web app for producers.** KILL. emergenzaxylella.it + Coldiretti press + free bulletins occupy the free tier; a map answers no decision the farmer owns. (The paid version only works as the *brief* in D1, sold to a body.)
- **"Xylella-free" certification/traceability label (blockchain or otherwise).** KILL. Plant passports and DOP control bodies already certify what law requires; oil from infected trees is safe and legal, so the label monetizes a fear consumers shouldn't have — reputational poison for a DOP client.
- **Parametric Xf insurance design.** KILL for this team. In the front band infection is a near-certainty (uninsurable trend risk); behind the front it's moot; ahead of it, no actuarial buyer without an insurer partner. Zero producer purchase probability from an info shop.
- **ST1/Pierce's alarm service for Bari grape growers.** KILL. Declared non-expanding Feb 2026 after >50,000 samples/yr of intense official surveillance; a private vendor adds nothing but fear, and the growers' organizations know it.
- **"Adopt an olive tree" / donation apps for estates.** KILL as loss-reduction: exists already, tiny revenue, zero effect on spread or replant throughput; at best an estate-marketing gimmick (Amalberga could run one without us).
- **General farm-management upsell "with a Xylella module."** KILL. xFarm/Elaisian would bundle it for free the moment it mattered; competing on FMIS against freemium incumbents is suicide.

---

### Verdict summary

The producer "market" is real but thin, and it is not the farmers. ~160k Apulian olive farms exist on paper, but the paying universe is ~16k businesses, 746 mills, a few dozen coops/OPs/consortia, ~960 agritourism estates — and demonstrated willingness-to-pay for olive information tops out at ~€300/yr (Elaisian/Agricolus/xFarm), always for weekly-actionable pest alerts, 84% subsidy-assisted, adopted mostly through OPs. Xylella generates 2–4 discrete decisions per decade (test, graft, replant, comply), not a subscription. Cantine Amalberga verifies perfectly as the exemplar: a ~20 ha, 80k-bottle organic winery relaunching DOC Ostuni, with a Leccino oil line and a tourism business built on the secular-olive landscape — its wine is biologically safe, its setting and oil line are not, and it sits in the epicentre comune (1,516 olive positives 2021, verified on-disk) which — my sharpest finding — has zero official sample rows in 2023–2025: the front peaked locally exactly when public parcel-level signal stopped. That data shadow is the one gap producers would plausibly fund, once, through their bodies: a front-timing/graft-replant brief for the Collina di Brindisi DOP consortium, the 386-member Ostuni cooperative, and AMO Puglia, at €1–10k tickets. Everything shaped like farmer-paid SaaS, sensors, dashboards, labels, or insurance goes on the kill list — the incumbents' own product choices prove there is no recurring Xf decision to sell.
