# 2016–2026 intervention landscape

A cited challenge to CORDON §0 / §1.4 / §4.3: *“there is no field-transferable treatment worth pursuing.”*

This note does **not** argue that a sterilizing outdoor cure exists. It argues that CORDON collapsed three different outcomes into one lock, and that the lock is therefore too strong.

The three outcomes, used throughout:

| Outcome | What would count as success |
|---|---|
| **S — symptom suppression** | Canopy stays productive; dieback slows; harvest kept |
| **C — pathogen clearance** | Bacterium eliminated from outdoor trees (the EFSA 2016 “cure”) |
| **T — reduced transmission** | Lower acquisition, fewer infectious vectors, slower front |

CORDON treated “no C” as “nothing worth pursuing.” The post-2016 record is: **C still fails outdoors; S and T have field products, field numbers, and a legal landscape already using them.**

Evidence grades, used on every entry: **RCT** / **multi-year field** / **single-season field** / **greenhouse** / **in vitro** / **anecdote** / **conference abstract**.

---

## 0. What EFSA actually locked in 2016

**EFSA PLH Panel, 5 April 2016, EFSA Journal 14(4):4456.** Local PDF: `raw/papers/efsa-2016-treatments.pdf`.

The Panel was asked whether Scortichini (CREA) and Carlucci/Lops (Foggia) had a field cure. Neither group released raw data (patent / “preliminary”). The Panel therefore could not peer-review the trials. What it *did* lock, in its own words:

1. Treatments under test “may reduce the symptoms… but do not eliminate the pathogen.”
2. “There is currently no treatment known to cure plants, by eliminating the pathogen, from xylem-limited bacterial infections, under field conditions.”
3. Combined pruning + soil work + micronutrients “may have contributed” to greener canopies; individual effects unidentifiable (no factorial controls).
4. Treated trees “are still systemically infected… and could be acting as sources of inoculum,” possibly *more* attractive to vectors because of lush flush.
5. Long-term Cu/Zn soil accumulation and possible loss of bacterial susceptibility remain open.
6. Even so, the Panel “acknowledges the potentially positive effects… in prolonging the productive phase” inside the containment zone, and asked for multi-season data.

So 2016 locked **C**, left **S** open, and flagged **T** as a possible *worsening* (lush treated trees as better acquisition hosts). CORDON quoted the first half and dropped the rest.

**EFSA 15 May 2019** (opinion 2019;5666 plus press “Xylella update: still no cure”) restated the same sentence three years later: chemical/biological measures “may temporarily reduce disease severity in some situations, but there is no evidence that they could eliminate *X. fastidiosa* in field conditions over a long period of time.” That is still **C**. It is not a ban on pursuing **S** or **T**.

Portaccio, Vergine, Bene, De Pascali, Sabella, De Bellis & Luvisi, *Pathogens* 14:840 (23 Aug 2025) — the 2025 review CORDON did not have when it locked — ends in the opposite direction from §4.3: *“N-acetylcysteine and zinc- and copper-based formulations, which are effective and potentially transferable to the field for crops such as citrus and olive trees.”* That sentence is the single cleanest published falsifier of “nothing transferable is worth pursuing.” It does **not** falsify “no outdoor clearance.”

## 1. Dentamet / Zn–Cu–citric acid

**What was claimed.** A foliar (later also endotherapeutic) Zn 4% / Cu 2% / citric-acid biocomplex reduces OQDS severity, lowers *Xfp* load, and keeps harvests on the two most susceptible Salento cultivars.

**What was shown.**

| Year | Paper | Design | Grade | Numbers |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| 2018 | Scortichini et al., *Phytopathol. Mediterr.* (3-year Veglie trial, 2015–17; in vitro + field) | Foliar Dentamet on naturally infected *Ogliarola salentina* and *Cellina di Nardò* | **multi-year field** | Treated trees ~10² CFU-equivalents vs untreated 10⁴–10⁵; symptom severity down; “most untreated trees were dead” by trial end. Not sterile. |
| 2019 | EFSA 2019;5666 | Regulatory read of Scortichini 2018 | review | Symptom and growth-rate effects acknowledged; **no elimination**. |
| 2021 | Tatulli et al., *Pathogens* 10:85 | In vitro on De Donno / Temecula1 / CFBP8416 + mid-term field, Cannole and Galatone, cvs Leccino / Ogliarola / Cellina | **multi-year field** + **in vitro** | 1:10 and 1:50 dilutions fully inhibit growth in vitro; 1:100 still active; biofilm drop strongest on De Donno. Field: 18–23 kg olives/tree; post-treatment 2.1–2.2 × 10³ CFU/g (Cellina in one grove 4.5 × 10⁴). Cultivar differences mostly not significant. |
| 2019–23 | Girelli / Del Coco / Fanizzi / Scortichini NMR series (*Phytopathol. Mediterr.*, *Plants*, *Sci. Rep.* 12:5973, 2022) | Foliar vs endotherapy metabolomics | **single-season / short-term field** | Endotherapy separates metabolic profiles more cleanly (quinic acid and mannitol down; polyphenols / oleuropein-related up). Foliar more durable over months. Hussain et al. (in Portaccio): foliar wins on duration; both need agronomy. |
| 2023 | *Sci. Rep.* 13, satellite NDVI of Dentamet groves 2018–20 | Remote sensing of treated vs untreated fields | **multi-year field** (proxy) | Treated-field NDVI rose 2018→2020. Not qPCR. |
| 2025 | Portaccio et al. Table 2 | Synthesis | review | Dentamet: in vitro titer **yes**, biofilm **yes**, field symptoms **yes**, field titer **partial**. |

**What remains uncertain.** No independent RCT with a public raw dataset. Scortichini’s group is on almost every Dentamet paper. EFSA 2016 never saw the raw trial. Titer drops 1–3 log, not to zero. Re-invasion of new flush is unquantified, so the 2016 **T**-risk (lush treated canopies as better acquisition hosts) is still open. Soil Cu/Zn loading over a decade is unmeasured in these groves. Whether Dentamet plus vector control beats vector control alone has not been isolated.

**Does it challenge ‘no cure’?** **C: no.** **S: yes, multi-year, on the two cultivars CORDON calls doomed.** **T: unknown, possibly adverse.** Challenges the *worth-pursuing* lock: a commercial foliar already used by Italian growers, with published 3–4 year titer and yield numbers, is a field object. CORDON’s table already admits “promising field-transferable, not sterilizing” and then treats that as a reason to walk away.

## 2. N-acetylcysteine (and NAC + zinc)

**What was claimed.** A cheap biofilm-disrupting cysteine analogue that worked on citrus variegated chlorosis will transfer to olive.

**What was shown.**

- Muranaka et al. 2013 (pre-2016, citrus *pauca* 9a5c): **in vitro** NAC ≥1 mg/mL blocks biofilm and kills planktonic cells; **greenhouse** hydroponic + trunk injection reduces CVC symptoms and load; stopping treatment brings symptoms back. Grade: **greenhouse**, citrus not olive.
- Field citrus translocation (reviewed in Portaccio 2025): single NAC pulse = 89% in roots / 5.3% stem / 1.9% leaves; every-two-weeks × 3 months = 69.8 / 11.2 / 19. Transpiration in CVC plants recovered toward healthy (peak 0.97 → toward 2.54 mmol m⁻² s⁻¹). Grade: **multi-year field**, citrus.
- Saponari / Dongiovanni olive field (XF-ACTORS / 2nd EFSA Xylella conference Ajaccio 2019; Dongiovanni 2017 slides; cited as Saponari et al. in Portaccio [23]): **partial symptom relief only at low starting titer; phytotoxicity and leaf drop at higher doses.** Grade: **single-season / multi-year field**, olive. Portaccio Table 2 scores olive NAC as titer **no** in vitro (meaning not shown for *Xfp* olive strain the way citrus was), symptoms **partial**, field titer **partial**.
- Baldassarre et al. CaCO₃ nanocarriers loaded with NAC + caffeic acid (in Portaccio [73]): **in vitro** 60–70% growth cut, wall damage, vesicles. **In olive trees, two months of nanovector treatment: titer unchanged at 10⁵ CFU/mL, no symptom gain.** Grade: **in vitro** + **single-season field** fail.

**What remains uncertain.** Olive xylem is not citrus xylem. Dose that is bioactive without defoliation is narrow. No published olive RCT with a durable titer curve.

**Does it challenge ‘no cure’?** **C: no, on olive.** **S: weak, olive; stronger, citrus.** **T: not measured.** Does **not** falsify the lock for Apulian groves. It *does* show that “field-transferable” is host-specific: the same molecule is a citrus field tool and an olive disappointment. CORDON’s one-line lumping of Dentamet *and* NAC as the same EFSA 2016 object is sloppy — NAC never earned the olive field case Dentamet did.

## 3. BIOVEXO bio-pesticides / endophytes

H2020 BBI-JU 887281, May 2020–April 2025. X-biopesticides (onion-extract OSCs + antagonistic endophytic bacteria) and V-biopesticides (plant-waste extract + entomopathogen + microbial metabolite). Field sites: Apulia olives, Alicante olives, Mallorca olives and almonds.

**What was claimed.** Near-market biopesticides that reduce *Xylella* pressure and vector pressure inside an IPM package, without felling.

**What was shown.**

- CORDIS Period-3 report (to 31 Oct 2023, posted 3 May 2024): three years of field trials; “results are promising but more field trials are needed.” Selected 2 X-biopesticides + 1 V-biopesticide. **Endotherapy abandoned for lack of efficacy.** Observed: “slowdown of the *Xylella* symptoms progression on the canopies” on X-treated plants, and “reduction of the juvenile and adult populations of *P. spumarius*” on V-treated plants. Grade: **multi-year field**, consortium self-report.
- BIOVEXO public-deliverable page (2025): explicit negative on clearance — *“Our results show that X-biopesticides do not have any effect on Xylella population sizes, even if a disease reduction occurs.”* That is the most honest sentence in the 2020–25 biocontrol literature.
- Olive Oil Times 19 Sep 2025: “three new biopesticides… may soon be available,” symptom-mitigation framing. Trade press, not a paper. Grade: **anecdote / press** on top of the CORDIS field.
- Endophyte biology (not BIOVEXO product, but the same idea): Vergine et al. 2020 *Pathogens* 9:35 — Leccino keeps a stable endophytic community under OQDS; Cellina goes dysbiotic. Antelmi et al. 2019 (Ajaccio) — *Methylobacterium* spp. as candidate antagonists. *Paraburkholderia phytofirmans* PsJN is the usual cited inoculant; **no olive field clearance paper**. Grade: **observational field** (microbiome) / **in vitro**.

**What remains uncertain.** Which two X- and one V- products, at what dose, on which cultivar, with what AUDPC. No public qPCR table. Registration status as of Aug 2026 is “close to market,” not on-label. Endotherapy kill is itself a result: delivery into xylem is not the bottleneck BIOVEXO thought it was.

**Does it challenge ‘no cure’?** **C: no — they said so.** **S: yes, multi-year, consortium-grade, unpublished as a paper.** **T: yes, V-product cut nymphs and adults.** Challenges the lock only as an **S+T package worth finishing**, not as a CORDON-shaped therapeutic. CORDON already said “do not compete with BIOVEXO.” That is the right posture. The lock error is treating BIOVEXO’s existence as proof that *we* should not think about treatments; it is actually proof that the professional backbone is pursuing **S+T**, not **C**.

## 4. BeXyl 2026 outputs / 5th EFSA Xylella conference (Mola di Bari, 23–25 June 2026)

~400 people. EFSA + BeXyl + EUPHRESCO III + CIHEAM Bari + CNR-IPSP + Puglia Plant Health. Day 1 was the first dedicated session on olive resistance. Conference materials: https://link.europa.eu/8y8wY7. Day 1–2 recordings on EFSA’s YouTube.

**What was claimed (press, 29 Jun / 6 Aug 2026).** “Concrete results… about olive resistance, biological control, vector management, and surveillance that can directly inform policy.” Genome editing and plant microbiomes named as “potential tools.” EFSA will publish an updated EU pest-risk assessment by end-2026 and is consulting on latency/incubation.

**What was shown (as of 15 Aug 2026, from EFSA/Seed World/Agro-Food Industry writeups and the Day-1 transcript fragments).**

- Resistance session: genomic/molecular mechanisms; long-term cultivar field comparisons; Leccino-offspring / synthetic-community talk (cultivable bacteria “associated with Leccino resilience,” proposed as a design basis for syncoms). No cultivar was announced that beats Leccino/FS-17 on **C**.
- Control session: still framed as integrated, not a molecule. One Q&A on FS-17 / Leccino plantings: trees are 3–4 years old; **productivity not yet scored**; trial continues.
- Vector day: “effective treatment against the bacterium not [available]; vector control remains one of the main management strategies.” *Metarhizium* mentioned among biological agents.
- **No paper, poster, or press line from Bari 2026 reports outdoor clearance of *Xfp* from olive.**

**What remains uncertain.** The BeXyl deliverable PDFs and the conference abstract book were not fully paged for this note. Greenhouse claims circulating since Jan 2026 (Frantoio, Gordal; see `queries/assumption-reopen-live.md`) still await the paper.

**Does it challenge ‘no cure’?** **C: no. The 2026 meeting re-enacted 2016.** **S/T: the professional community is investing here, not in a grove sterilant.** The conference is evidence that CORDON’s *computational* wedge (resistance decoder + front nowcast) is what Bari is actually asking for. It is not evidence that a treatment track opened.

## 5. Phage — MATE 2, XylPhi-PD, Texas A&M cocktail, Phi1/Phi3

Separate olive-in-vitro from grape-in-field. They are not the same product.

### 5.1 Olive / *pauca* phages — in vitro only

| Year | Object | Grade | Shown | Not shown |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| 2014 | Ahern et al., California *Xylella* phages | **in vitro** / grape greenhouse later | Lytic isolates exist | Olive field |
| 2021 | Clavijo-Coppens et al., Mediterranean *Xanthomonas/Xylella* phages | **in vitro** | Host range into the family | Olive field |
| 2024 | Sabri, El Handi, Cara, De Stradis, Valentini, Elbeaino, *Front. Microbiol.* 15:1412650. Local PDF + extract. *Xylella* phage **MATE 2**, sewage, southern Italy | **in vitro** | Broad *Xanthomonadaceae* activity; 10 min adsorption; pH 4–10; 4–60 °C; **suppresses *Xfp* in liquid culture for 7 days**; 63,695 nt, 95 ORFs, no lysogeny / AMR / toxin genes; *Carpasinavirus*. Authors: “efficacy on *Xf*-infected olive trees in the field has yet to be determined.” | Any plant |
| 2024–25 | Sabri/Elbeaino follow-on: Phi1 (*Pradovirus*, 92.5% ANI to *Xanthomonas* phage phiXc10) and Phi3 (*Salvovirus*, 96.7% to *Xylella* phage Salvo) | **in vitro** | Strictly lytic, no off-target on 14 olive isolates + 10 beneficials | Field |

MATE 2’s own discussion flags **endolysin** as a next object. That is a protein, not a replicating phage — a different regulatory path, still paper-only.

### 5.2 Grape field phage — real product, wrong host

- Das, Pandey, Filártiga, Navarrete, Hayes, Gonzalez et al., *PLOS ONE* 2015: four-phage cocktail, greenhouse + early field, Pierce’s disease. Pathogen down, symptoms prevented. Grade: **greenhouse / early field**, grape, subsp. *fastidiosa*.
- **XylPhi-PD** (A&M Inphatec / Wilbur-Ellis). EPA-registered, OMRI-listed. Texas A&M natural-infection study: 3 monthly treatments, **44% relative reduction in PD incidence** vs control. Multi-site California: **57% year-to-year incidence drop** after two seasons; one three-year writeup claims **55% less detectable *Xf*, +17% yield, 80–100% prevention of new infections** (trade/tech-note numbers, not a journal article). Cabernet/Chardonnay plots have reported 70–88% incidence cuts in some site-years. Grade: **multi-year field**, grape. **Not olive. Not *pauca*. Not EU-registered.**

**Does it challenge ‘no cure’?** **C on olive: no.** **S on grape: yes, and it is for sale.** The honest transfer claim is: *xylem-limited Xylella is not unreachable by a biological*; the dishonest one is “therefore spray MATE 2 on Ogliarola.” Delivery, subspecies, biofilm, EU GMO/biocontrol law, and acquisition-from-treated-canopy are all untested. CORDON’s AMP/phage paper-only rule stays correct. The lock error is writing “phage = in vitro, dismiss” as if XylPhi-PD did not already leave the lab.

## 6. Vector control (tillage, insecticides, kaolin, natural enemies)

This is not a “treatment of the tree.” It is the only intervention **PNAS priced in nine figures**, and it is already law.

**What was claimed.** Cut nymphs in ground cover + kill/repel adults on canopy → slower front, fewer superinfections.

**What was shown.**

- Dongiovanni, Di Carolo, Fumarola, Tauro, Altamura, Cavalieri 2018 *Arthropod Manag. Tests* 43:tsy073 (nymphs) and Dongiovanni, Altamura, Di Carolo, Fumarola, Saponari, Cavalieri 2018 *AMT* 43:tsy034 (adults). Grade: **multi-year field**, 2015–17, olive. **Pyrethroids and neonicotinoids**: adult mortality 76.7–100% at 3 DAT; persistence 15–20 d (pyrethroids) / 20–25 d (neonicotinoids). Organophosphates worse. **Kaolin, zeolite, sweet orange oil, buprofezin**: weak on nymphs.
- Same group, ground-cover: shallow ploughing, winter + spring tillage, winter-only tillage, barley/*Lolium* sowing, herbicide, flame, mulch — all cut juvenile *P. spumarius* and *N. campestris* relative to unmanaged weeds (Morelli 2021 review of the 2015–17 package).
- Dáder et al. 2019 *Insects* 10:225: sulfoxaflor and natural pyrethrin+PBO as neonicotinoid alternatives on juveniles. Grade: **lab / semi-field**.
- Kaolin as **T** tool, not just a nymphicide: Dongiovanni et al. (in Morelli 2021) — three-year calendar sprays on a new planting inside the infected zone, tested as a feeding disruptor. Effect on *acquisition/transmission* was limited; this is not a canopy sterilant.
- Trunk injection of dimethoate / imidacloprid vs spray (Dongiovanni): injection slower, not better, more expensive.
- Natural enemies: *Ooctonus vulgatus* (Mymaridae) egg parasitoid (Mesmin et al. 2020 *PeerJ*); *Verrallia aucta* (Pipunculidae) in northern Italy (Molinatto et al. 2020); Liccardo et al. 2020 *PLoS ONE* model of biological control. Grade: **observational / model**. Not an operational release.
- Schneider et al. 2020 *PNAS*: slowing radial spread 5.18 → 1.1 km yr⁻¹ is worth **€0.5–1.3 billion over 50 years**. That number is a **T** number. It is why tillage + adult insecticides are mandatory in demarcated areas.

**What remains uncertain.** How much of the 2024–26 Salento “trees coming back into production” (Boscia, via BeXyl/Il Post, Jan 2026) is vector decline vs weather vs Dentamet vs just the susceptible canopy already being dead. Attribution is not a trial. Resistance to pyrethroids/neonics is a stated EFSA 2015/2019 risk and is not being monitored in public.

**Does it challenge ‘no cure’?** **C: no.** **T: this *is* the transferable treatment, already transferred.** CORDON §4.3 says “slowing the front is the only intervention PNAS priced in the high hundreds of millions” and then builds a nowcast — which is correct — while §0 still talks as if treatments were a closed door. Vector control is the door that is open.

## 7. Resistant replant as an intervention (not just genetics)

Leccino, FS-17 (Favolosa), Lecciana, Leccio del Corno. This is the only **landscape-scale, legal, already-planted** intervention in the infected zone.

**What was claimed.** Resistant cultivars get infected, carry less bacterium, die back less, incubate longer, and — epidemiologically — feed less inoculum to *P. spumarius*.

**What was shown.**

- EFSA 2017;4772: Leccino tolerant to ST53; FS-17 also. Grade: **regulatory review** of field + inoculation.
- Giampetruzzi et al. 2016 *BMC Genomics* (local PDF): 659 DEGs Leccino vs 447 Ogliarola. Mechanism paper, not a spray.
- Field load (Morelli 2021, citing the CNR field pair): Leccino **4 × 10⁴ CFU/mL vs Ogliarola 2 × 10⁶** — **~100× lower**. Internal spread limited. Greenhouse-reproducible. Grade: **multi-year field** + **greenhouse**.
- Cavalieri / Dongiovanni / Saponari acquisition-transmission tests (HPIS 2017; later Entomol. Gen. 2021): cultivar changes how efficiently *P. spumarius* acquires. That is **T**, not just **S**.
- Pavan et al. 2021 (local PDF): large germplasm screen; **no commercial cultivar beat Leccino/FS-17**; Frantoio and Nocellara Messinese scored partial resistance. Grade: **multi-year field**.
- La Notte, Saponari, Mousavi, Mariotti, Abou Kubaa, Nikbakht, Melcarne, Specchia, Altamura, Ligorio, Boscia, Surano, Saldarelli, Giampetruzzi 2024 *Front. Plant Sci.* 15:1457831 (local PDF). 171 field survivors under high inoculum, 6 years; 139 unique SSR profiles. Leccino, Cellina, Ogliarola the common parents. **Among 61 Leccino progeny, 67% HR/R/T vs 32% Cellina and 49% Ogliarola.** Leccino × Cellina >90% HR/R/T; Leccino × Ogliarola 47%. Four open-pollinated Leccino seedlings artificially inoculated: all resistant; **S105 and S215 (Leccino × Cipressino) symptomless at 24 months post-vector-inoculation**; S218 and S234 mild dieback; Cellina collapsed; S105 bacterial colonization stable, S215 cleared to 2/n positives at 24 mpvi. S105: 4,513 DEGs vs 13,030 (S215) and 17,227 (S234). Grade: **multi-year field** + **greenhouse inoculation**.
- 2024–25 regional authorisation: **Lecciana** and **Leccio del Corno** added to the legal replant set (Agromillora Nov 2025; Bruno et al. 2024 *Agronomy* 14:2119). Hundreds of hectares already going back in. Grade: **operational landscape**, not an RCT.
- ResiXO (Regione Puglia): Leccino grafted onto centennial Ogliarola/Cellina to keep the trunks. Ongoing, not a published titer trial.

**What remains uncertain.** “Resistant” is not immune (CORDON §1.5 is right). Super-intensive Lecciana physiology under ST53 is young (3–4 year trees at Bari 2026; yield unknown). Whether a Leccino/FS-17/Lecciana landscape actually drops regional *R₀* is an epidemiological claim, not yet a measured one. Gatti 2026 social conflict around felling is the political constraint on the complementary 50 m cut.

**Does it challenge ‘no cure’?** **C: no — infected trees stay infected.** **S: this is the only intervention that has already saved a regional industry’s replant path.** **T: mechanistically yes (100× less load → less acquisition); landscape-yes is not yet measured.** If “treatment worth pursuing” includes “what a grower can legally put in the ground next winter,” CORDON’s lock is already false. CORDON knows this (§7: “Replant-with-Leccino is already happening”) and still wrote §0 as if treatments were the closed door. Replant *is* the treatment.

## 9. Odd, farmer, and industry objects (read, not dismissed)

### 9.1 Sniffer dogs

BeXyl dogs Ellis and Paco (trainer Mario Fortebraccio); UN-FAO / IPPC 2024 video; BBC Future Jan 2023. Grade: **operational pilot**, detection. Compared to qPCR, not to a spray. **Not a treatment.** Belongs in Artifact A (front nowcast), not in a cure argument. CORDON §1.4 already has this right.

### 9.2 Thymol + IoT

Cagnarini, De Angelis, Liberati, Valentini, Falanga, Valentini, Dongiovanni, Carrieri, Chiriacò 2025 *Plants* 14:1380. 40 infected olives, 3% free thymol vs cellulose-nanoencapsulated thymol, advanced sensors. **Slight titer drift on the nano arm; not statistically different from free thymol.** Grade: **single-season field**. Portaccio: in vitro thymol-NP is real; field titer is not. IoT is monitoring, not therapy.

### 9.3 NAC + zinc combos

See §2 Baldassarre nanocarrier: the combination that looks best on a slide (**C** in vitro) is the one that **did nothing to titer in olive**. Combining two “promising” actives is not a result.

### 9.4 NuovOlivo® (farmer / small-company detergent)

Vegetable-oil + Mediterranean-herb ash lye (thyme, rosemary, sage, oregano, chamomile, mallow, willow, pepper, etc.) + Ca/S. Trials on Cellina and Ogliarola at two Apulian sites (Portaccio [38], [39] 2024 expansion). **Claimed up to 99.04% drop in bacterial DNA in some samples; new flush, flowers, fruit; infection index down vs control.** Small *n*, limited controls, pruning not synergistic. 2024 multi-grove expansion: better vegetative growth, high between-grove variance. Grade: **single-season / poorly controlled field**, close to **anecdote** on the 99% figure (DNA ≠ viable CFU). **Do not dismiss:** it is a published, named product with a second-year expansion and a DNA number. **Do not promote:** it has not met a Tatulli/Scortichini-level titer design, let alone EFSA.

### 9.5 Carlucci & Lops 2016 (the other 2016 trial EFSA heard)

*L’Informatore Agrario* 8/2016: 58–63. Severe prune + commercial bioactive sprays + soil work. Greener flush in 5 months; **all trees still PCR-positive**; harvests taken. EFSA 2016 heard them, did not see the data, noted missing factorial controls. Grade: **single-season field / grey literature**. This is the ancestor of every “prune + tonic” farmer protocol in Salento. It is **S**, not **C**, and EFSA already said so.

### 9.6 Kalex Zn® + Kalex Cu® + Siliforce® (Del Grosso et al. 2025)

*Plant Disease* (APS, 22 Aug 2025; Portaccio [87]). In vitro: Kalex Zn bactericidal in 4 h; Kalex Cu / mix in 2–4 h; Siliforce weaker. **Field, Cellina di Nardò, Mar 2021–Sep 2022, combined with plant-protection products:** disease-index rise **20% treated vs 61.5% untreated**; titer 4.88 → 4.66 log₁₀ CFU/mL (May–Sep 2022); **17 kg/tree treated vs 2 kg untreated in 2021; ~15.7 vs ~7.5 kg in 2022**; oil acidity and peroxides slightly better. Grade: **multi-year field**. This is the 2025 paper that most looks like “Dentamet, but someone else.” Still **S + partial C**, not clearance. It is field-transferable in the same sense Dentamet is.

### 9.7 Ammonium chloride

In vitro biofilm OD 1.6 → 0.15 at 1% NH₄Cl. 2019–20, five groves, four sprays Mar–Oct ± biostimulant: more flush, fewer symptoms, **no titer difference**. Grade: **single-season field**. Classic **S without C**.

### 9.8 Menadione / benzethonium (Zhang, Pierce’s)

100% in vitro inhibition; phytotoxic on grape; some disease-progress cut. Grade: **greenhouse**, grape. Not an olive object.

## 11. What actually dies, and what does not

**Still true, 15 August 2026.**

- There is no outdoor, sterilizing cure for *Xylella* in olive. EFSA 2016, EFSA 2019, Portaccio 2025, BIOVEXO’s own deliverable line, and Bari 2026 all agree. CORDON must not claim one.
- In vitro ≠ field. Nanocarriers, AMPs, MATE 2, oxylipins, AgNPs: the graveyard is full of 2-log lab wins that vanished in a trunk.
- Lush **S** without **C** can worsen **T**. EFSA 2016 said this out loud. Nobody has closed it.

**False as written in CORDON §0 / §1.4 / §4.3.**

- “No field-transferable treatment worth pursuing” equates **C** with **S+T**. The EU strategy *is* **T** (tillage + insecticides + 50 m cut) plus **S-by-genotype** (replant Leccino/FS-17/Lecciana/Leccio del Corno) plus optional **S-by-spray** (Dentamet, now also Kalex Zn/Cu). All three are in the ground.
- “EFSA closed that door” is a misread of 4456. The Panel closed **C**, asked for more seasons on **S**, and flagged **T** risk. The seasons happened (Scortichini 2018, Tatulli 2021, Del Grosso 2025). **S** held. **C** did not appear.
- “Phage is in vitro” is true for olive and false for grape. The existence of XylPhi-PD is why a decoder that ranks xylem-exposed *Xfp* proteins is not fantasy. It is not a reason for us to ferment a lysate.
- NAC and Dentamet are not one row. Dentamet has olive multi-year titer + yield. NAC has citrus multi-year and an olive shrug.

**What a computational team should do with this (without breaking CORDON’s wet-lab ban).**

1. Keep Artifact A pointed at **T**: the nowcast is how you spend the €0.5–1.3bn. That lock is intact.
2. Keep Artifact B pointed at **S-by-genotype**: S105 vs S215 vs S234 is a better ranking problem than “invent a peptide.” La Notte already handed us the contrast.
3. If a treatment track is reopened, the only objects with enough field gravity to be worth a partner’s time are **(a)** Dentamet/Kalex-class Zn-Cu under a real factorial with vector-pressure measurement (the open **T** question), and **(b)** scoring Leccino-offspring / Lecciana yield at year 5–7. Not MATE 2. Not CRISPR. Not NAC-nano.
4. Do not email Bari a treatment pitch. The 2026 meeting told us what they want: resistance, vectors, surveillance. That is A+B.

**One-sentence verdict.** EFSA 2016 still kills grove sterilization; it never killed “treatments worth pursuing,” and the decade since produced three that are already being pursued at landscape scale — vector suppression, resistant replant, and Zn-Cu symptom management — none of which CORDON has to invent, and two of which (front + genotype) are exactly the artifacts it already chose.
