# Telos review — S1: slow the spread (adversarial, independent)

Reviewer: external adversarial research review, 16 Aug 2026. Scope: everything that changes the front's km/yr — vector control, felling/buffers, compliance, front dynamics. Metric: expected reduction of projected economic loss only. All numbers below re-verified against primary sources this session unless marked **unverified**.

## A. € mechanism and magnitude of this lever

Mechanism (Schneider et al. 2020, PNAS 117:9250–9259, doi:10.1073/pnas.1912206117): loss = discounted foregone grower profits + lost orchard investments, integrated over the area invaded in 50 y. Radial range expansion rate is the multiplier on invaded area; slowing it shrinks the integral. Verified numbers from the paper:

- Italy, 50-y impact: €1.86–5.17B if production ceases after die-off; €0.59–1.57B if resistant replanting is feasible (range = spread-rate scenarios RR05–RR95).
- Slowing spread 5.18 → 1.1 km/y cuts Italy's impact ~41% (no replant) / ~44% (replant): ~€1.29B or ~€0.46B saved. The abstract's "€0.5–1.3B" is this pair.
- Same slow-down if the pathogen reaches Spain: €7.12B saved (no replant; Spain total impact €0.71–16.86B). Greece: €0.87B saved (total €0.21–1.94B). The S1 lever is worth ~5× more in Spain than Italy because Spain starts from a point introduction.
- Key input caveat: Schneider's 5.18 km/y is the *median of an EFSA expert elicitation* (95% CL 0.73–14.0 km/y; EFSA Journal 2019;17(5):5665), not a measurement. The only empirical estimate, from the same Puglia monitoring data this repo holds, is **10.0 km/y (95% CI 7.5–12.5) for 2013–2018** (Kottelenberg et al. 2021, Sci Rep 11:1061, doi:10.1038/s41598-020-79279-x). Reality ran at Schneider's upper scenarios. Anything that verifiably moves km/yr is therefore worth on the order of €0.1–0.3B per km/y for Italy alone (linear interpolation across Schneider's scenario span — **rough, mine, not the paper's**).

Physical sub-levers and their measured strength:
- Nymph control: soil tillage reduced P. spumarius nymph density ~60% vs control at plot scale (Abruzzo field trials; J Econ Entomol, PMID 34137895). Double tillage / herbicide / pyroweeding most effective in Apulian trials (Dongiovanni et al. 2018, cited with numbers in Morelli et al. 2021 review, Microorganisms 9:1771, doi:10.3390/microorg9081771).
- Adulticides: pyrethroids and acetamiprid, 76.7–100% caged-adult mortality at 3 days, persistence 15–25 days (Dongiovanni et al. 2018 trials, via Microorganisms 9:1771). Cage efficacy ≠ landscape transmission reduction; no published study links the mandatory area-wide campaigns to reduced conversion rates at landscape scale (none found this session).
- Felling + buffers: EU floor is 50 m infected-zone radius, ≥2.5 km eradication buffer, ≥5 km containment buffer, 1 km derogation (IR (EU) 2020/1201 Arts. 4–5, EUR-Lex 32020R1201). EFSA's 2019 modelling: reducing buffer width increases area infected, in both eradication and containment (EFSA J 17(5):5665). White et al. 2017 (Biol Invasions 19:1825, doi:10.1007/s10530-017-1393-5): narrow control zones are jumped by stochastic long-distance dispersal; LDD, not local diffusion, sets the spread rate.
- Measured dispersal vs buffer geometry: single flight up to 5.5 km on flight mills (Lago et al. 2021, Sci Rep, doi:10.1038/s41598-021-96904-5, which states buffers were *reduced* from 10 km to 2.5–7 km and 100→50 m radius); MRR median 26 m/day, 98% within ~750 m lifetime (Bodino et al., cited in Bodino et al. 2023, Phytopathology). The 10 km/y front is faster than any measured vector movement → human-mediated LDD dominates (Bodino et al. 2023; White et al. 2017).
- Compliance: ECJ condemned Italy (C-443/18, judgment 2019, ECLI:EU:C:2019:676) for late inspections and felling delays. FVO/DG SANTE audit (Nov 2018): only **10.7% of >3,000 detected positive olives felled** in the 20-km containment strip at audit time, with felling slipping into the vector flight season (Agence Europe report of the audit; audit PDF not opened this session).
- Net effect of the whole apparatus, modelled on 2013–2020 surveillance data: containment reduced spread from 7.2 to **5.7 km/y** (95% CI 5.4–5.9) — and most of that reduction traces to one anomalous simulated year; counting felled+infected trees together the front moved 7.6 km/y, i.e. felling as implemented bought almost nothing (White-group ABC model, PLOS Comput Biol 2025, doi:10.1371/journal.pcbi.1013539).

Answer to the standing question: **the €0.5–1.3B lever is not being pulled at full strength.** The realized rate (5.7–10 km/y modelled/empirical through 2020) is nowhere near the 1.1 km/y Schneider low scenario; the felling arm demonstrably underperformed; buffer/monitoring geometry was *relaxed* in 2024 (containment monitoring strip cut from ≥5 km to ≥2 km, design prevalence 0.7%: consolidated CELEX 02020R1201-20241017; Valle d'Itria comuni moved from eradication to containment: Regione Puglia press 15 Mar 2024; Reg. (EU) 2024/1320 per FruitJournal 17 Dec 2024 — amendment numbers not independently opened). No published empirical front rate exists after April 2018 data. That number is the missing input.

## B. State of the art — who already knows and does what

- Front-rate estimation from the monitoring data: **done through Apr 2018** by Kottelenberg/Hemerik/Saponari/van der Werf (WUR + CNR): 10.0 km/y, logistic front ~156 km wide (1st–99th pct), back-extrapolated start ~2008. They solved the moving-sampling-frame problem with distance-ring aggregation + simulation bias checks.
- Mechanistic spread + containment-efficacy modelling: **done through 2020** by the White/Chapman group (UKCEH et al., PLOS Comput Biol 2025): ABC fit to 495,626 inspections (2,329 positives), introduction ~2003 (95% CI 2000–2009), short-range kernel σ≈0.32 km, mean LDD ≈6.4 km, wind weakly favoured over isotropic, simple road-hitchhiking model *not* favoured; felling effect small; recommendation: surveillance at and beyond the wavefront + rigorous rapid felling.
- Within-grove transmission: White et al. 2020 — each infected tree infects ~19 trees/y; presymptomatic stage ~1.2 y with low infectivity (**as summarized in MDPI decade review PMC11175074; original not opened**).
- Road/landscape association: Bajocco et al. — ecological niche model of infected trees 2015–2021 found the road system the primary diffusion engine, semi-natural land a brake (**via PMC11175074, original not opened**). Bucci: 98% of infected trees within 100 m of another; >75% of new hotspots >1 km from any known infected plant (**same secondary route**). So "roads matter" is published; fine-grained directional front analysis with post-2021 data is not.
- Vector biology/control: CNR-IPSP + CRSFA (Dongiovanni, Cavalieri, Bodino, Cornara, Saponari) own the field-trial literature (nymph tillage, adulticides, MRR, phenology; Saponari et al. 2019 Phytopathology 109:175 is the canonical review). EFSA maintains the vector data collections (2019). Vector PCR sentinel monitoring (IAMB, for the Region) exists and detected ST1 in vectors *before* plant finds (Cornara et al. 2025, EJPP, doi:10.1007/s10658-024-02945-7).
- Operations: Osservatorio Fitosanitario + ARIF run surveys, injunctions, felling; action plans DGR 1866/2022 (2023–24) and the 2023–25 plan codify Ribess+ survey design, 50 m surveillance rings, mandatory tillage/treatments; DG SANTE audits (June 2022 audit noted in DGR 1866/2022); Commission enforcement via C-443/18.
- Slowdown attribution: CNR communications assert containment efficacy (reduced vectors + inoculum) ("Xylella in Puglia, frenano espansione e pressione", CNR IRIS 462572 — popular-press tier). Ciervo & Scortichini 2024 (J Phytopathol e13272, read via secondary) counter that demarcated-area incidence is ≤0.23% and felling now exceeds positives ~13× (2022-23: 366 positives all hosts, 4,389 felled; 15,100 trees felled in 10 y — Rivista di Agraria 2024 write-up of access-to-records data; **contested source, Scortichini disputes Xf causality**). The 2024 Bari San Giorgio ST53 focus (6 olives + 3 almonds, a ~20 km jump past Polignano, first crossing of the 41st parallel; FruitJournal 17 Dec 2024) shows LDD is still live. Nobody has published a design-adjusted front rate covering 2019–2025. The repo's own CAMP digest shows why raw counts can't decide: the 2022–23 collapse coincides with a survey redesign and the 2024–25 test budget shifting to ST1/multiplex.

## C. Verdict on CORDON's existing work as a contribution to S1

**Score: 0/10.** Nothing in this repo has measured, modelled, or informed any determinant of km/yr.

- The repo's compute went to tree-level pre-visual detection from Sentinel-2 (A0/A1/A2/A7, all null). That is S3 work at best, and its S1 value chain (earlier detection → faster felling → slower front) was already broken at the felling link: the PLOS 2025 model and the FVO 10.7% audit show removal, not detection, is the binding constraint. Even a *positive* satellite result would have moved ~€0 of S1 loss without a compliance fix. Four nulls moved exactly €0 and replicated published expectations (Zarco-Tejada 2018; Hornero et al. 2020, RSE 111480, which scoped S2 to visible-damage incidence).
- CORDON.md §1.2–1.3 quotes Schneider's 5.18 km/y as the spread anchor and asserts "the front has slowed" from press synthesis (Agromillora, Il Post/Boscia). It never cites Kottelenberg 2021 — the empirical 10.0 km/y estimate computed *from the same workbooks sitting on this disk* — and never attempts its own estimate. A program whose stated telos is the slow-spread lever, holding the monitoring data through Dec 2025, produced no spread number. S0's self-review already concedes this (its point 3 and 6); concession is not contribution.
- Salvage on disk, unexploited: (1) the 12-workbook tree-level series 2013–2025 — five campaigns *beyond* anything published (Kottelenberg stops Apr 2018, White-group stops 2020); (2) repeat-tested coordinates with negative→positive conversion windows (98/86/42/12 across four campaign pairs) — bounded infection times no published Puglia analysis uses; (3) the CAMP_XLSX chronology correctly flagging the 2018 Lecce exit and 2022–23 frame shift, i.e. the exact confounders a slowdown estimate must model. These are assets, not results.

## D. Top 3 actions, ranked by expected Δ€

**D1. Publish the missing number: design-adjusted front rate c(t), 2013–2025, with slowdown attribution.**
- Who must act on it: EFSA PLH and DG SANTE (buffer/monitoring-strip widths under 2020/1201 were relaxed in 2024 with no post-2018 empirical rate in the record); Regione Puglia (containment-zone geometry); economists updating Schneider (his rate input was an elicitation the data contradicted).
- € logic: buffer geometry is the EU's main S1 instrument, and EFSA 2019 showed narrowing it increases invaded area. If the true current rate is ~2 km/y, Italy is tracking near Schneider's low scenarios and marginal money should shift to S2/S4; if it is still ≥5 km/y with the 2022–23 "collapse" a surveillance artifact, the 2024 relaxations are underwriting an upper-scenario trajectory (Δ between adjacent Schneider scenarios ≈ €0.1–0.3B/km/y for Italy, **rough interpolation**; Spain tail adds up to €7.12B of slow-down value if export of the epidemic follows).
- 2–6 week deliverable (2-person computational team, genuinely not done elsewhere): replicate Kottelenberg's ring-logistic estimator on 2013–2018 (validation), extend to 2019–2025 with explicit per-campaign sampling-frame correction (zone polygons per year, subspecies filter for 2024–25, Gargano treated as a separate focus), report c per campaign-pair with CIs, plus a formal three-way attribution test (front deceleration vs frame shift vs saturation) using the repeat-tested-coordinate panel as a fixed spatial denominator. Send to van der Werf/WUR and the White group — both have stated they need updated data; this is a collaboration wedge that requires no wet lab and no partner permission.

**D2. Felling-delay dose–response from conversion windows + public injunction records.**
- Who must act: Osservatorio/ARIF (felling queue prioritization); Commission (post-C-443/18 enforcement posture); national plan (compensation timing — delays are partly litigation- and payment-driven).
- € logic: the one modelled containment gain (7.2→5.7 km/y) is the felling arm, and it underperformed exactly where compliance failed (10.7% felled, flight-season slippage). EFSA 2019 modelling makes removal-within-~30-days the condition for local eradication. If delay measurably raises neighborhood conversion probability, every week cut from the injunction-to-felling pipeline is bought spread reduction at near-zero capital cost; if it does not, the 50 m healthy-tree removals (5,792 trees felled for 512 positive olives, 2021–23, per the contested Ciervo-line data) lose their epidemiological justification and their political cost is being paid for nothing. Either answer reallocates real budget.
- 2–6 week deliverable: survival analysis P(negative→positive conversion within 50/100/400 m of a prior positive | time-to-felling of that positive), using the 98/86/42/12 converter cohorts and felling dates scraped from published determinazioni (they are public PDFs with coordinates, e.g. BURP DET_47/2023). Survivorship bias (positives felled) and coordinate≠tree identity must be modelled, not footnoted. Nobody has published this; the White-group model *assumed* felling parameters instead of estimating them.

**D3. Landscape-scale test of the mandatory vector-control campaign (tillage compliance via satellite × conversion rates).**
- Who must act: Regione (enforcement targeting of tillage/treatment obligations); growers' organizations.
- € logic: vector suppression is the mechanism CNR credits for both the Salento remission and the slowdown, but its evidence base is plot-scale (60% nymph reduction) and cage-scale (adulticide mortality). Whether the *actual area-wide campaign* reduces transmission is unmeasured. Parcel-scale spring tillage is directly visible in Sentinel-2 — the orchard-scale use of S2 that the literature supports, unlike the tree-scale use this repo burned weeks on. If tilled-landscape cells convert less, enforcement gets a map; if not, the campaign's design (timing, coverage) is wrong and should be redesigned before more seasons are spent.
- 2–6 week deliverable: per-parcel spring bare-soil/tillage classification (2019–2025) over the containment + buffer corridor, joined to CAMP conversion outcomes; report conversion odds vs neighborhood tillage fraction with distance-to-front controls. Expected Δ€ ranked third because the causal chain to a decision is longer and compliance data may confound (no public parcel-level compliance dataset located this session — **gap itself worth reporting**).

## E. Kill list for this lever

1. **Stop running tree-level satellite detection branches under an S1 rationale** (A3 as "front" work, A4 WV-3 SWIR proposal, A5 airborne). Detection feeds S3; the S1 bottleneck is removal speed and buffer geometry. Four nulls already bought this lesson.
2. **Stop citing 5.18 km/y as the observed Puglia rate and "the front has slowed" as fact.** 5.18 was an elicitation median; the empirical record says 10.0 km/y through 2018, modelled 5.7 through 2020, and nothing after. Fix CORDON.md §1.2/§1.3 or measure it (D1).
3. **Stop treating the 2022–23 positive collapse as epidemiology** anywhere in the repo until D1's attribution test runs. The repo's own CAMP digest documents the frame shift that confounds it.
4. **Drop the "don't compete with Planetek/REDoX" rule for S1.** They fly detection sensors; front dynamics, felling dose–response, and compliance analysis have no incumbent commercial actor. The rule protected nothing and deterred the highest-Δ€ work.
5. **Stop sequencing S1 work behind Artifact A / partner-brief gates.** D1 and D2 need no lag number, no airborne data, and no permission — only the workbooks already on disk and public injunction PDFs.
