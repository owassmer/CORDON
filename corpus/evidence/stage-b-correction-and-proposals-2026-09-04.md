## Proposed resolution P1 — annual containment-vector cadence

Recommendation: adopt an annual recurrence for the current national-plan containment-vector investigation, while preserving its distinct geography and biologically appropriate timing. Do not derive a fixed day, number of visits, or sampling workload from this ruling.

Evidence actually examined:

- The complete section 9.3 in the admitted 2026 plan, including its annual-surveillance heading, introductory annual surveys, plant-survey clauses, and vector-monitoring paragraphs.
- The original archived PDF, pages 37 and 38 (one-based PDF pages), rendered and visually inspected. The bottom of page 37 and top of page 38 are one continuing sentence, not independently headed duties.
- Union Article 15(2), (4) and (5) in the admitted current consolidation. They distinguish infected-zone annual surveys, buffer plant sampling, and vector monitoring. They do not justify equating the populations.
- The predecessor 2022 national plan section 6.7. It provides annual surveillance context and a vector-monitoring duty, but not the same explicit sentence; it is context, not a substitute source for the 2026 cadence.

Reasoning: the 2026 sentence first describes annual vector investigation throughout an eradication area, then contrasts containment investigation in the buffer and specified infected-zone parts. The most coherent reading is that the contrast changes geographical coverage while the annual cadence carries through. This reading is supported by the surrounding annual-surveillance frame, but it is an interpretation of the whole sentence, not an independently repeated annual adverb in its containment half.

The strongest contrary reading is that the repeated future verb after the contrast begins a separately qualified duty whose period remains unspecified. That is possible enough to warrant explicit owner adjudication. It does not support the previous categorical claim that the complete sentence supplies no cadence.

If adopted, B would record period 1 year, retain the source's containment scope, and leave an independently specified occurrence count and calendar deadline unset. The full sentence must remain the evidentiary basis; a clipped second half cannot be presented as independent proof. Do not extend this ruling automatically to the 2022 version or to all vector duties.

Pending state: the existing B period remains null and its note now explicitly marks this interpretation unresolved. Null is not a finding that no duty or recurrence exists. No annual cadence has been adopted during this correction.

Operational consequence: avoiding a missing annual containment-vector surveillance obligation without inventing a detailed field schedule. The real objective is reduction of projected Xylella loss; the wording must still support the obligation.

## Proposed resolution P2 — receipt as reporting evidence, not an additional act

Recommendation: completion means successful reporting/submission through the route applicable at the time, supported by authoritative evidence. A system acknowledgement or recipient receipt is evidence of that completion, not a separately imposed recipient-acknowledgement duty under the inspected reporting provisions.

The [original Article 22(3)](https://eur-lex.europa.eu/legal-content/EN/TXT/?uri=CELEX:32016R2031) specifies reporting and its payload. [Regulation 2024/3115, Article 1(4)](https://eur-lex.europa.eu/legal-content/EN/TXT/?uri=CELEX:32024R3115) adds the electronic submission route, while Article 1(19) includes reports in Article 103's system remit. Neither inspected clause expressly makes a separate acknowledgement act a second legal completion condition.

This does not make a draft, queued transmission, local attempt, or unsupported assertion of sending sufficient. An authoritative record that the report was successfully submitted to the required destinations can prove completion; an acknowledgement may be the available proof. Where evidence is missing or conflicting, completion remains unverified rather than being presumed performed or legally breached solely because one particular receipt artifact is absent. The precise technical evidence contract is later-stage work; no system API or evidence format is prescribed here.

The strongest contrary concern is that successful electronic submission may itself depend on server acceptance, or an independently controlling procedural rule may specify a receipt event. The recommendation preserves proof of successful submission and would yield to such a demonstrated rule. It rejects only the unsupported addition of a separate receipt duty. This bounded examination does not certify every operational reporting protocol.

If adopted, amend the A evidence contract accordingly and have each B reporting version complete on that version's reporting act. Do not impose the post-2025 route on the original version, and do not treat the Osservatorio's preparation/transmission to a national actor as proof that the national actor completed Union reporting.

Pending state: the separate-receipt question is explicitly unresolved in the reporting producer's evidence contract and the B note. B completion points to performance of the applicable A version, rather than silently deciding the question through an extra hard-coded receipt requirement.
